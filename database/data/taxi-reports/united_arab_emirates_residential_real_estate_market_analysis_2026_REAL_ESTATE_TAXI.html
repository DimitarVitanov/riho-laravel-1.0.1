02 · DIRECT ANALYSIS ANSWER

A high-liquidity global market, but Dubai and Abu Dhabi are at different points in the cycle

The UAE entered 2026 with exceptional transaction momentum. Dubai Land Department reported AED 252 billion of total real-estate transactions in Q1 2026, 31% higher in value than a year earlier. Abu Dhabi Real Estate Centre reported AED 66 billion, up 160.7%, including AED 50.97 billion of sales and purchases.

Price evidence requires more discipline. The latest widely reported April 2026 price growth came from the private REIDIN index, not a federal transaction-price index. Dubai remained 6.09% higher year-on-year but declined 1.76% month-on-month, while Abu Dhabi was 27.76% higher year-on-year with a 0.32% monthly decline. Dubai therefore appears to be moderating after a multi-year expansion, while Abu Dhabi remained in a stronger growth phase.

Real Estate Taxi conclusion: the UAE remains one of the Middle East's deepest international residential markets, but the strongest investment is no longer simply “buy Dubai.” Buyers need to select the emirate, district, building, developer, title, service-charge structure and delivery risk with much greater precision.

03 · DATA DISCIPLINE

Official government activity, private price indices and advertised yields

01

Official transaction activity

Dubai Land Department and Abu Dhabi Real Estate Centre provide official transaction values, transaction counts, mortgage activity, project registrations and rental-contract data. These are the strongest sources for market liquidity and registered activity.

Transaction value is not the same as residential price appreciation because it includes differences in property mix, luxury sales, land, commercial property, mortgages and off-plan launches.

02

Private price indices

REIDIN, ValuStrat and major property consultancies publish private residential price indices and weighted-average values. These are useful for segment direction but are not official federal or emirate-wide government price indices.

This report labels them as private data and does not present them as government transaction statistics.

03

Asking-price and asking-rent data

Global Property Guide uses advertised purchase prices and advertised rents to calculate gross yields. Its Q2 2026 UAE average was 4.94%.

These figures measure market expectations and should be replaced with completed-sale comparables, signed rents, concessions and actual annual costs before investment.

04

Real Estate Taxi inference

Real Estate Taxi analysis combines official liquidity, private pricing, rental evidence, regulation, financing, supply and property-specific risk. Analytical scenarios are not forecasts, guarantees or official statistics.

04 · DUBAI MARKET

Strong activity with clearer signs of price and rental moderation

05

Official Q1 2026 activity

Dubai recorded AED 252 billion of total real-estate transactions in Q1 2026, up 31% year-on-year, through 60,303 transactions, up 6%.

Foreign investment reached AED 148.35 billion. The broad figures confirm exceptional liquidity and international participation, but they include multiple real-estate sectors and transaction types.

06

Private price direction

REIDIN reported Dubai residential prices up 6.09% year-on-year in April 2026, with apartments up 5.49% and villas up 9.86%. The monthly index declined 1.76%.

This indicates continuing annual growth but a more selective short-term market. Prime villa areas may outperform apartment districts exposed to heavy new supply.

07

Off-plan concentration

Private analysis of DLD data estimated approximately 45,200 Dubai residential transactions in Q1 2026, with off-plan property representing about 72% of activity.

Off-plan dominance can support launch prices during expansion, but it increases exposure to future completions, payment-plan incentives, assignment restrictions and developer execution.

08

Dubai rental market

DLD reported Q1 2026 rental-contract value of AED 32.2 billion, with 118,385 new contracts and 135,607 renewals. Cancelled contracts declined 25%.

Private rent indices nevertheless showed annual rental growth slowing materially by April 2026. New-let rents in some prime apartment communities were reported below prior-year asking levels while regulated renewals remained firmer.

05 · ABU DHABI MARKET

Record transaction activity and stronger price and rent growth

09

Official Q1 2026 activity

ADREC reported AED 66 billion of real-estate transactions across 13,518 deals in Q1 2026. Sales and purchases totalled AED 50.97 billion across 8,940 transactions, while mortgages totalled AED 15.03 billion.

Hudayriyat Island, Reem Island, Saadiyat Island and Yas Island were leading transaction areas.

10

Price and rent direction

The private REIDIN Abu Dhabi residential price index was 27.76% higher year-on-year in April 2026, with apartments up 31.46% and villas up 7.84%. The monthly index declined 0.32%.

ADREC's official repeat-lease price index was 16% higher year-on-year in March 2026, confirming strong leasing pressure even though private rent-index growth was beginning to decelerate.

11

Supply outlook

ADREC projected Abu Dhabi-region residential supply to increase by 10,272 units in 2026, from 314,976 to 325,248 units, and to reach 333,564 in 2027.

Demand may continue to absorb new supply, but each island and master community must be tested against its own handover schedule.

12

Investment zones

Foreigners can own property in approved Abu Dhabi investment areas, including freehold ownership where permitted. Reem, Yas and Saadiyat are major international ownership and investment locations, but exact title eligibility must be confirmed.

06 · OTHER EMIRATES

Higher yield can come with thinner liquidity and concentrated project risk

13

Ras Al Khaimah

Ras Al Khaimah is increasingly driven by resort, branded-residence and tourism development. It may offer stronger growth narratives and rental potential, but also more concentrated demand, off-plan supply and hospitality-cycle exposure.

14

Sharjah and Ajman

Sharjah and Ajman generally offer lower entry costs and can produce higher gross yields than prime Dubai. Foreign ownership, leasehold or usufruct structures vary by area and project.

Tenant demand, traffic, service charges, building condition and resale depth must be analyzed locally.

15

Fujairah and Umm Al Quwain

These smaller emirates can present niche coastal, industrial or lifestyle opportunities but typically have thinner transaction evidence and a smaller international resale audience.

A higher advertised yield should include a larger liquidity and management-risk premium.

16

Emirate-specific regulation

The UAE does not operate one uniform property-registration system. Ownership zones, fees, tenancy systems, escrow rules and approval processes differ by emirate. Due diligence must therefore be carried out through the regulator and land department for the exact location.

07 · RENTAL YIELDS

Gross returns range widely by location, unit size and service-charge burden

17

Q2 2026 advertised yield

Global Property Guide estimated the UAE average gross rental yield at 4.94% in Q2 2026, down from 5.45% in Q4 2025.

Selected Dubai asking-data examples ranged from above 7% for some studios and one-bedroom units to below 3% for large luxury apartments. The national average therefore hides substantial segment variation.

18

Gross versus net return

  • Use achieved rent after free periods and incentives.
  • Deduct vacancy and tenant-change periods.
  • Include annual service and community charges.
  • Include leasing commission, management and maintenance.
  • Reserve for air-conditioning, lifts, façade and water damage.
  • Include mortgage interest, insurance and compliance costs.
  • Model sale commission and registration cost on exit.

In high-service-charge towers, a 5% gross yield can become a low single-digit net yield before financing.

19

Short-term rental economics

Holiday-home demand is strongest in Dubai and selected resort markets. Investors must verify licensing, building rules, operator fees, tourism charges, cleaning, utility cost, furnishing replacement and monthly seasonality.

Peak nightly prices should never be multiplied across the full year.

20

Rent regulation

Dubai uses the official rental index and statutory notice procedures in landlord-tenant relationships. Abu Dhabi and other emirates operate their own tenancy frameworks.

Review the current lease, notice history, rent cap or index position and dispute risk before purchasing a tenanted property.

08 · FOREIGN OWNERSHIP AND RESIDENCE

International access is broad but title and location remain decisive

21

Dubai foreign ownership

Foreign nationals may acquire freehold title in designated Dubai ownership areas and may acquire other interests such as usufruct or long leases where permitted. Freehold eligibility must be confirmed on the title and official DLD records.

22

Abu Dhabi and other emirates

Abu Dhabi permits foreign freehold ownership in designated investment areas. Other emirates have their own foreign-ownership zones and title structures.

Do not assume that a developer marketing a property internationally proves that the exact legal right is freehold.

23

Golden Visa relevance

Official UAE sources state that qualifying real-estate investors generally require one or more properties valued at at least AED 2 million. Documentation, financing, valuation and residence conditions must be satisfied.

Property purchase does not automatically guarantee approval, and the visa rules should be checked at application time.

24

Residence is not investment return

A visa benefit can improve personal utility, but it should not be capitalized into the purchase price without evidence that future buyers will value it equally. The property must still work as an independent real-estate investment.

09 · FINANCING AND CURRENCY

Dollar-linked rates and bank-specific underwriting shape leveraged returns

25

Policy and market rates

On 16 July 2026 the Central Bank of the UAE listed a 3.65% Base Rate, 4.15% lending-facility rate, 3.768% DONIA and 3.518% overnight EIBOR.

Mortgage offers commonly reference EIBOR plus a bank margin, but the exact rate, reset period and floor are contract-specific.

26

Mortgage regulation

CBUAE rules impose loan-to-value and debt-burden limits and require stress testing. Permitted leverage varies by borrower nationality, first or subsequent property, value and whether the unit is completed or off-plan.

Obtain a written offer and valuation before signing a non-refundable reservation.

27

Currency peg

The dirham is pegged to the US dollar at approximately AED 3.6725 per USD. This reduces USD exchange-rate uncertainty but creates exposure for euro, sterling and other-currency investors.

28

Affordability stress test

Model the mortgage after the introductory period, EIBOR increases, service-charge increases, vacancy and a lower resale price. A low down payment can magnify loss if off-plan or apartment prices weaken.

10 · TAXES AND PURCHASE COSTS

No broad annual federal property tax does not mean low all-in cost

29

Dubai registration cost

Dubai's property-sale registration charge is generally 4% of the sale value, commonly allocated by agreement between buyer and seller, plus trustee, title, map, mortgage and administrative fees.

Broker commission and developer transfer or no-objection charges may apply separately.

30

VAT treatment

The Federal Tax Authority states that residential sales and leases are generally exempt from VAT. The first supply of a newly completed residential building within three years can be zero-rated. Commercial property is generally subject to 5% VAT.

Serviced apartments, mixed-use property and business activity can require different treatment.

31

Income and corporate tax

The UAE has no broad federal personal income tax on ordinary salary or passive personal income. However, real-estate activity conducted as a business, through a company or at sufficient scale may create corporate-tax, VAT, licensing and accounting obligations.

Natural-person real-estate investment treatment should be confirmed under current FTA guidance.

32

Annual ownership costs

Budget service and community charges, cooling, maintenance, insurance, property management, municipality-related tenant fees where applicable, vacancy utilities and major reserve expenditure.

11 · OFF-PLAN, SUPPLY AND DEVELOPMENT RISK

The UAE's growth engine is also its principal investment risk

33

Developer and escrow checks

Verify developer licensing, project registration, land control, escrow account, construction percentage, contractor, payment schedule, completion date, delay remedies and cancellation rights through the relevant land department.

34

Payment-plan economics

Post-handover plans, fee waivers and guaranteed rent can increase the headline price. Compare the discounted cash value of payment terms with the cash price of completed competing units.

35

Future handovers

Map the entire district pipeline, not only the selected tower. Multiple completions can reduce rents, increase concessions and weaken resale even while emirate-wide transactions remain strong.

36

Assignment and resale

Off-plan contracts may restrict assignment until a payment percentage or construction milestone is reached. Verify developer consent, transfer fees, mortgage eligibility and buyer demand before relying on resale.

12 · BUILDING, CLIMATE AND INSURANCE RISK

Heat, humidity and high-rise systems create long-term capital expenditure

37

Technical systems

Review façade, glazing, waterproofing, roof, district cooling or chiller system, air-conditioning, lifts, pumps, fire systems, plumbing, electrical infrastructure and basement drainage.

38

Coastal exposure

Salt, humidity and water intrusion affect coastal towers and villas. Inspections should test façade seals, balconies, parking structures, mechanical equipment and evidence of repeated leakage.

39

Service-charge governance

High-rise value depends on professional management and adequate reserves. Review approved service charges, arrears, reserve-fund balance, tender history, insurance, planned capital works and owner disputes.

40

Flood and extreme-weather risk

Review drainage, basement access, elevation, road access, flood history and insurer exclusions. Unusual rain and heat can temporarily disrupt occupancy and reveal construction defects.

13 · INVESTOR FIT AND OUTLOOK

The market rewards selection, cash-flow discipline and exit planning

42

Potentially suitable investors

  • Long-horizon buyers seeking USD-linked Gulf exposure.
  • Investors targeting completed, occupied and liquid property.
  • Buyers with local legal and property-management capability.
  • Owner-occupiers who value residence and lifestyle utility.
  • Cash buyers able to negotiate against developers or motivated sellers.
43

Potentially unsuitable investors

  • Highly leveraged investors requiring guaranteed positive cash flow.
  • Short-term flippers dependent on continuing broad price growth.
  • Remote buyers relying only on developer marketing.
  • Buyers ignoring service charges and future supply.
  • Investors who cannot absorb delayed completion or a longer exit.

14 · BUYING PROCESS AND DUE DILIGENCE

Verify the title, project, building economics and exit before payment

45

Acquisition checklist

  • Confirm foreign-ownership eligibility for the exact unit and title.
  • Verify seller identity, title deed, mortgage and registered restrictions.
  • Check developer, broker and project registration.
  • For off-plan property, verify escrow account and construction status.
  • Review the sale and purchase agreement, assignment and default clauses.
  • Verify measured area, parking, storage and common-area rights.
  • Obtain completed-sale comparables from the same building or district.
  • Obtain signed-rent comparables and concession evidence.
  • Review approved service charges, reserves, arrears and planned works.
  • Commission technical inspection for completed property.
  • Map future handovers and competing rental inventory.
  • Secure mortgage approval and valuation before non-refundable commitment.
  • Calculate registration, broker, trustee, mortgage and NOC costs.
  • Check VAT, corporate-tax and licensing treatment for the intended use.
  • Verify Golden Visa eligibility separately from investment performance.
  • Identify the future buyer and realistic resale period.
15 · FAQ

Frequently asked questions

01

What were the latest official UAE transaction figures available for this report?

Dubai Land Department reported AED 252 billion of total real-estate transactions in Q1 2026 across 60,303 transactions. Abu Dhabi Real Estate Centre reported AED 66 billion across 13,518 transactions in Q1 2026.

02

Are the latest Dubai and Abu Dhabi price-growth percentages official government indices?

The April 2026 price-growth figures used in this report come from REIDIN and are private market indices. Dubai was up 6.09% year-on-year and Abu Dhabi 27.76%. They are kept separate from official DLD and ADREC transaction totals.

03

What was the latest UAE gross rental yield?

Global Property Guide estimated the UAE average gross residential yield at 4.94% in Q2 2026 from asking prices and asking rents. It is not a completed-transaction or net-income figure.

04

Can foreigners buy property in the UAE?

Foreigners can buy freehold property in designated ownership areas and can acquire other rights where permitted by the relevant emirate. Eligibility, title type and location must be confirmed for the exact property.

05

What property value may qualify for a UAE Golden Visa?

Official UAE and Dubai immigration sources state that qualifying real-estate investors generally need one or more properties valued at at least AED 2 million, subject to current documentation, financing and residence requirements.

06

What is the latest verified UAE policy-rate reference?

The Central Bank of the UAE listed a Base Rate of 3.65%, a lending facility rate of 4.15% and overnight EIBOR of 3.518% on 16 July 2026. Retail mortgage pricing is separate.

07

Does the UAE impose VAT on residential property?

The Federal Tax Authority states that residential supplies are generally exempt from VAT, while the first supply of a newly completed residential building within three years can be zero-rated. Commercial property is generally subject to 5% VAT.

08

What is the main UAE residential investment risk?

The main combined risk is paying a late-cycle or off-plan premium while underestimating service charges, future completions, mortgage cost, developer and escrow risk, climate-related maintenance and resale liquidity.

REAL ESTATE TAXI VERDICT

The Middle East's deepest international market—but no longer a one-direction trade

The UAE combines exceptional liquidity, international access, USD-linked currency stability and powerful population and investment inflows. It also has high off-plan concentration, large future supply, substantial service charges and sharply different emirate cycles. A disciplined buyer should prioritize completed or de-risked property, transparent operating costs, proven rent and a broad resale audience rather than relying on developer incentives or recent market-wide appreciation.
