DIRECT ANALYSIS ANSWER

Romania entered mid-2026 with rising prices but softer cumulative transaction volume

Romania’s residential market is not behaving like a single national cycle. Official transaction-price data show strong appreciation, while the first half of 2026 recorded fewer total property transactions than the same period of 2025. This combination points to price resilience supported by constrained supply, wage and replacement-cost pressure, urban concentration and selective demand, rather than a broad volume boom.

Bucharest remains the deepest and most liquid market, Cluj-Napoca commands the highest asking-price premium, and university or employment centres can deliver stronger rental depth. The main investment discipline is to separate official completed-sale momentum from portal asking prices and estimated yields. A property can sit inside a rising national index while still being overpriced at building, street or unit level.

MARKET EVIDENCE

National Price and Transaction Intelligence

Official HPI data, cadastral transaction counts, portal asking prices and yield estimates are treated as separate evidence layers.

01

1. Market snapshot and evidence hierarchy

Romania has several housing indicators that answer different questions:

Eurostat House Price Index
Official transaction-price momentum for dwellings purchased by households.
ANCPI transaction statistics
Counts of houses, apartments, land and other properties registered in the cadastral system.
Portal asking prices
Seller expectations for active listings; useful for submarket comparisons but not completed sale prices.
Estimated gross yields
Rent divided by purchase price before vacancy, tax, maintenance, management and financing.

Real Estate Taxi rule: the correct comparable must match source type, city, district, age, condition, legal status and property format.

02

2. Official house-price growth accelerated in Q1 2026

Eurostat reported that Romania’s House Price Index increased 7.8% year on year and 3.2% quarter on quarter in Q1 2026. The same official series showed annual growth of 6.6% in Q3 2025 and 6.7% in Q4 2025, indicating that the first quarter of 2026 strengthened rather than cooled.

The HPI is a national transaction-price index. It does not mean every apartment in Bucharest, Cluj or a small town appreciated by the same percentage.

03

3. Portal asking prices show even faster nominal momentum

Global Property Guide’s reviewed Romania source reported a national monthly asking-price indicator up 12.42% year on year in March 2026. For December 2025 it cited an average listed apartment price of about €1,965/m², with large city differences.

The gap between official HPI growth and portal asking-price growth is analytically important. It can reflect a changing listing mix, seller optimism, delayed negotiation, higher-quality new stock or stronger pressure in the most visible urban markets.

04

4. Long-term appreciation is strong, but not linear

Global Property Guide’s July 2026 comparative series estimated Romania’s inflation-adjusted residential price increase at 54.88% over ten years, 14.40% over two years and 9.47% over five years. These figures show that long-term performance has been meaningful, but the path included periods when inflation absorbed much of nominal growth.

Investors should therefore model real purchasing-power returns rather than relying only on nominal price charts.

05

5. June transactions improved, but H1 volume remained below 2025

ANCPI recorded 51,808 property sales in June 2026, 1,494 more than May and 2,615 more than June 2025. However, the first six months of 2026 totalled 272,657 transactions, which was 16,229 fewer than the comparable 2025 period.

This is a mixed signal: monthly liquidity improved by June, while the cumulative market remained more selective than a year earlier.

06

6. Mortgage registrations remain an important demand signal

ANCPI recorded 32,663 mortgage registrations nationwide in June 2026. Bucharest, Iași and Ilfov were the leading counties for mortgage operations. Mortgage counts include more than newly purchased homes, but they help indicate the intensity of secured-credit activity.

A rising mortgage count alongside lower cumulative transactions can also reflect refinancing, collateral changes or a shift toward financed urban buyers.

CITY & REGIONAL MARKETS

Bucharest, Cluj-Napoca, Brașov, Timișoara, Iași and Constanța

Employment, university demand, infrastructure, tourism and development constraints create very different local price and rental profiles.

07

7. Bucharest — deepest liquidity and broadest buyer pool

Bucharest recorded 10,398 property sales in June 2026, the highest county-level total in Romania. GPG’s December 2025 portal compilation placed the average listed apartment price near €2,204/m², with new apartments around €2,500/m² and existing apartments around €2,159/m².

The capital offers the broadest mix of owner-occupiers, corporate tenants, students, public-sector demand and investors. Yet district, building age, seismic classification, parking, energy performance and access to metro lines can move value dramatically.

08

8. Cluj-Napoca — highest asking-price premium, lower yield efficiency

Cluj-Napoca remained Romania’s most expensive major city in the reviewed portal data, at roughly €3,235/m² in December 2025. Its premium reflects a strong university base, technology employment, constrained central supply and investor familiarity.

High entry pricing compresses income yield. GPG’s January 2026 estimate placed Cluj’s gross apartment yield around 4.41%, materially below Bucharest and several secondary cities.

09

9. Brașov — hybrid lifestyle, tourism and commuter demand

Brașov combines local employment, tourism, second-home demand and access to mountain destinations. GPG’s December 2025 asking-price compilation placed the city around €2,228/m². The market can support both long-term and short-stay strategies, but seasonality, condominium rules, parking and professional management materially affect net return.

10

10. Timișoara — employment-led demand and wider price dispersion

Timișoara recorded 1,615 transactions in June 2026 at city level, the highest among county capitals in ANCPI’s release. Its diversified employment base and proximity to western European markets support residential demand. Portal data around €1,865/m² in December 2025 suggested a lower entry point than Bucharest, Cluj or Brașov.

11

11. Iași — university depth and strong mortgage activity

Iași recorded 1,137 city transactions in June 2026 and was among the leading counties for mortgage registrations. The city benefits from education, healthcare and regional-service demand. Investor underwriting should distinguish student-oriented small units from family apartments and new peripheral developments where transport and infrastructure delivery are critical.

12

12. Constanța and the coast — mixed permanent and seasonal markets

Constanța combines a permanent urban economy with tourism and coastal second-home demand. The portal average of roughly €1,943/m² in December 2025 masks large differences between the city, Mamaia, waterfront projects and ordinary residential districts.

Coastal properties require additional review of seasonality, building maintenance, salt exposure, parking, short-term rental compliance and off-season liquidity.

13

13. Secondary cities — yield can improve where liquidity remains adequate

GPG’s January 2026 estimates identified Galați among the highest-yield markets assessed, at around 8.09% gross. Higher headline yield often compensates for thinner resale liquidity, weaker demographic trends or less diversified tenant demand.

The strongest secondary-city investment case normally combines a university, hospital, industrial employer, transport node or public-administration function with limited new supply.

SUPPLY · RENTAL · YIELD

Construction Pipeline, Rental Demand and Net Income Reality

Romania’s high owner-occupation rate keeps the formal rental sector relatively small, but large cities and university centres have deep tenant demand.

14

14. Housing completions declined in 2025

INS data reported by Romania’s national news agency showed 59,062 dwellings completed in 2025, 1,916 fewer than in 2024, a decline of about 3.14%. Urban completions represented 53.7% of the total, down from 56.8% a year earlier.

Falling completions support prices where demand remains robust, but national delivery totals do not reveal whether supply is well located, legally complete or aligned with household budgets.

15

15. Bucharest–Ilfov supply remains central to national dynamics

The Bucharest–Ilfov region recorded a modest annual increase in 2025 completions even as six of eight development regions declined. The capital region’s scale means small changes in permits, infrastructure or developer finance can materially influence the national market.

Investors should check the entire competing pipeline within the micro-location, not only the target building.

16

16. Rental market is small nationally but concentrated locally

Romania has one of Europe’s highest owner-occupation rates, so the market-rent segment is small as a share of households. This does not mean weak rental demand in Bucharest, Cluj, Iași, Timișoara or Brașov. It means rental liquidity is highly concentrated in cities, employment nodes and student districts.

A national rent average is therefore less useful than verified local listings, achieved rents and vacancy evidence.

17

17. Asking rents continued to rise

The reviewed GPG report cited an average asking rent of about €550 per residential unit in Q3 2025, around 6% higher than a year earlier. It also cited typical one-bedroom asking rents of approximately €600 in Bucharest, €590 in Cluj-Napoca, €500 in Brașov, €430 in Timișoara and €450 in Iași.

These are portal asking rents, not lease-registry transaction data. Furnishing, utility responsibility, parking, building quality and contract term can materially change achievable rent.

18

18. Gross yield estimates are attractive by European standards

Global Property Guide estimated Romania’s average gross apartment yield at 6.02% in January 2026. Its city estimates included approximately 7.52% for Bucharest, 8.09% for Galați and 4.41% for Cluj-Napoca.

Gross yield is a screening metric only. A realistic net model should deduct vacancy, repairs, common charges, insurance, management, tax, furnishing replacement, legal costs and financing.

19

19. Net yield depends on tenant quality and operating discipline

A lower-rent apartment with stable long-term occupancy can outperform a higher headline rent with frequent turnover. Investors should model at least three scenarios: stabilized long-term rent, conservative vacancy and repair stress, and a downside case with delayed leasing or reduced rent.

For short stays, include platform fees, cleaning, utilities, licensing, local restrictions and seasonality.

20

20. Energy performance is increasingly financial, not cosmetic

Older blocks may trade at a discount but can carry higher energy costs, weaker comfort and future renovation requirements. Newer buildings can command premiums, yet construction quality, developer track record, utilities and final acceptance documents must be independently checked.

Energy certificates should be treated as one input, not a substitute for technical inspection.

FINANCE & MACRO RISK

Mortgage Conditions, Interest Rates and Affordability

Romania’s monetary setting remains restrictive, so cash flow and debt-service assumptions must be conservative.

21

21. BNR kept the policy rate at 6.50% in July 2026

The National Bank of Romania maintained its monetary-policy rate at 6.50% on 8 July 2026, with the lending facility at 7.50% and deposit facility at 5.50%. This confirms that financing conditions remain restrictive despite some easing in mortgage pricing from earlier peaks.

Borrowers should not assume rapid rate normalization. Fixed-period offers, variable-rate formulas, fees and early-repayment conditions need product-level comparison.

22

22. Mortgage pricing has eased, but affordability remains sensitive

The reviewed GPG report, drawing on NBR data, cited an average rate of 5.89% on new RON-denominated housing loans in November 2025, down from 6.14% a year earlier. Most new housing credit is now in lei, reducing direct household exposure to foreign-exchange risk.

Even small rate changes materially affect monthly payments at Romania’s current price levels, especially for entry-level buyers with high loan-to-income ratios.

23

23. Price growth can continue while transaction volume softens

A market with limited quality supply can record rising prices even when fewer properties change hands. Sellers may resist discounts, buyers may become more selective and only the best-located units transact.

This creates a risk of relying on stale asking comparables. Recent completed transactions and realistic marketing periods are more important than headline appreciation.

24

24. Fiscal consolidation is a material housing-market variable

Romania entered 2026 with significant fiscal-consolidation pressure and recent VAT and tax changes. These measures can affect disposable income, construction costs, developer pricing and investor returns.

Because tax treatment can change, every acquisition model should use current Romanian professional advice rather than archived online calculators.

25

25. Currency and cross-border capital risk

Romania uses the leu rather than the euro. Many property prices and rents are discussed in euro, while local income, taxes and financing can be in lei. A foreign investor therefore faces translation risk even where the purchase price is euro-linked.

Stress-test both rental income and resale proceeds against the investor’s home currency.

PURCHASE · TITLE · TAX

Ownership, Land Book, Notary and Due Diligence

Romanian property purchases require coordinated legal, cadastral, fiscal, technical and source-of-funds checks.

26

26. Land-book verification is the starting point

The Romanian land book records technical data, ownership, acquisition title, mortgages, precautionary measures and litigation affecting the property. ANCPI is the official authority for land-book and cadastral-plan extracts.

An information extract is essential for screening, but the transaction process also requires up-to-date notarial and registry checks immediately before signing.

27

27. Cadastral identity must match the physical property

Verify cadastral number, land-book number, measured area, floor, unit boundaries, parking, storage, land share and actual use. Unauthorised alterations, enclosed balconies, merged rooms or extensions can create financing and resale problems.

A technical survey is particularly valuable for houses, villas, attic conversions and older buildings.

28

28. Notarial deed and source-of-funds compliance

Romanian real-estate transfers are normally completed through an authenticated notarial deed. The notary coordinates legal formalities, but the buyer should still retain independent legal and technical advice where the transaction is complex, financed, corporate, development-stage or cross-border.

Expect identity, beneficial-ownership and source-of-funds documentation.

29

29. 2026 fiscal-certificate procedure requires attention

From 26 February 2026, local Romanian authorities announced a requirement for the buyer of a building or land to present a fiscal-attestation certificate confirming payment of local-budget obligations. Documents executed in breach of the statutory certificate rules may be invalid.

The exact certificate, issuing authority and timing should be confirmed by the acting notary before signing.

30

30. VAT and transfer-cost treatment must be checked transaction by transaction

A resale from a private owner, a new unit sold by a VAT-registered developer and a company transaction can have different tax treatment. Romanian VAT rules and transitional provisions changed recently.

The buyer should obtain a written cost schedule covering price, VAT status, notary fee, land-book registration, mortgage costs, agent fee, legal review and any local charges.

31

31. Foreign-buyer framework differs for buildings and land

EU and EEA buyers generally operate under the conditions linked to Romania’s EU membership. Romanian law can impose restrictions on land acquisition by non-EU foreigners, depending on reciprocity, treaty status, legal structure and the specific land category.

Foreign purchasers should obtain advice on the apartment, associated land share and any agricultural or non-urban land before signing a reservation or preliminary agreement.

32

32. New-build due diligence

For new construction, verify title to the development land, building permit, planning compliance, utilities, developer encumbrances, construction stage, handover standard, warranties, final acceptance and individual cadastral registration.

Law 207/2025 introduced registration and reception procedures for future assets, and ANCPI reported 809 such operations in June 2026. Buyers should understand exactly what is registered and what remains conditional.

33

33. Older-building and seismic risk

Bucharest contains older buildings with differing structural histories and seismic classifications. A central address does not remove engineering risk. Review official classifications, expert reports, renovation history, insurance availability and lender acceptance.

Discounts on technically weak stock can disappear quickly once repair, vacancy and resale constraints are recognized.

34

34. Due-diligence checklist

  • Current land-book extract and cadastral plan.
  • Seller identity, authority and marital or corporate approvals.
  • Mortgages, seizures, litigation, easements and restitution claims.
  • Building permit, final acceptance and lawful use.
  • Measured area, annexes, parking and land share.
  • Condominium debts, reserve fund and planned works.
  • Utilities, energy certificate and technical inspection.
  • Tax treatment, fiscal certificates and total acquisition cost.
  • Lease status, occupants and handover protocol.
  • Independent valuation and conservative exit scenario.
STRATEGY & OUTLOOK

Buyer Profiles, Risk Matrix and 2026 Scenarios

The best Romanian market depends on whether the buyer prioritizes liquidity, income, lifestyle, development or capital preservation.

35

35. Owner-occupier strategy

Owner-occupiers should prioritize employment access, schools, public transport, building condition and long-term monthly affordability. A lower purchase price in a weakly connected peripheral project may be false economy once commuting, resale and infrastructure risk are included.

36

36. Income-investor strategy

Income investors should target verified tenant depth rather than the highest advertised yield. Bucharest offers liquidity and broad demand; university cities offer repeat tenant flows; secondary cities may offer higher gross yield but slower resale.

The correct metric is stabilized net cash flow after all recurring and periodic costs.

37

37. Capital-growth strategy

Capital-growth buyers should focus on supply-constrained micro-locations, infrastructure improvements and durable employment demand. Paying a premium is justified only when the property has superior scarcity, legal quality and resale appeal.

38

38. Development and refurbishment strategy

Value-add opportunities exist in older urban stock, but legal and technical risk is higher. Confirm permitted use, structural feasibility, condominium approvals, utility capacity, contractor pricing and resale demand before acquisition.

39

39. Risk matrix

High probability / medium impact
Asking-price overstatement, slower resale, repair inflation and tenant turnover.
Medium probability / high impact
Title defects, unauthorised works, developer insolvency, seismic weakness or financing withdrawal.
Macro risk
Interest-rate persistence, fiscal tightening, weaker real incomes and currency volatility.
Upside drivers
Falling mortgage costs, limited quality supply, infrastructure delivery and strong urban employment.
40

40. 2026–2027 scenario outlook

Base case: nominal prices continue rising, but growth becomes increasingly segmented by city, building quality and affordability. Transaction volume recovers unevenly.

Upside case: mortgage rates ease, confidence improves and constrained delivery supports strong urban appreciation.

Downside case: fiscal tightening and persistent financing costs reduce demand, leading to longer marketing periods and more negotiation even if headline indices remain positive.

41

41. Negotiation intelligence

Negotiation should be based on time on market, repeated price reductions, legal readiness, technical defects, vacancy, competing new supply and seller financing needs. A fast, documented buyer can sometimes secure a better result than a nominally higher but conditional offer.
