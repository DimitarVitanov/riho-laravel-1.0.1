Austria Residential Real Estate Market Analysis 2026

Austria is moving from correction into a restrained nominal recovery. Lower financing pressure and stronger transaction activity support demand, but real prices remain soft, new construction is unusually weak and the investment case differs sharply between Vienna, the Alpine west and lower-cost eastern and southern states.

01 · EXECUTIVE INTELLIGENCE

A recovery market with a future supply problem

Austria’s housing market passed through a meaningful adjustment after financing costs rose in 2022. Existing apartments corrected more visibly than new stock, transactions weakened and development pipelines contracted. By 2025, the direction began to change: financing became more predictable, household purchasing power improved and buyers who had delayed decisions started returning.

The rebound is not a new speculative boom. Nominal prices have turned positive, but inflation-adjusted values remain below the previous peak. At the same time, the sharp fall in construction means that the next phase may be driven less by aggressive demand and more by an insufficient flow of new homes—especially in Vienna and other high-demand urban or Alpine markets.

Real Estate Taxi interpretation: Austria currently offers a better negotiation environment than the 2021–2022 peak, but the window may narrow for efficient, legally simple homes in supply-constrained locations. The strongest investment cases are based on durable rental demand and replacement cost—not on expecting rapid national price inflation.
REAL ESTATE TAXI VERDICT

Stable market, but low-yield mistakes are expensive

Austria can fit conservative buyers seeking legal clarity, euro exposure and long-term demand. It is less attractive for investors who ignore acquisition costs, rent regulation or the difference between existing leases and new-market asking rents.

  • Demand and transaction activity are recovering.
  • Construction weakness supports selected locations.
  • Gross yields are generally modest.
  • Federal-state and municipal rules can change the usable value of a property.
02 · PRICE INTELLIGENCE

Nominal recovery does not mean the correction is fully reversed

The OeNB residential property price index rose 2.12% year on year in Q4 2025, while the inflation-adjusted index was still down 1.74%. Vienna performed somewhat better than the rest of the country, but the national average hides large differences between apartments, houses, new projects, existing stock and thin regional submarkets.

04 · DEMAND & LIQUIDITY

Deferred buyers returned in 2025

Preliminary Statistics Austria data recorded 38,329 dwelling transactions in the first three quarters of 2025, nearly 20% more than a year earlier. The value of transacted dwellings rose even faster, to approximately €13.51 billion. This suggests that lower financing pressure and improved confidence translated into completed deals rather than only more online enquiries.

Demand is still selective. Efficient apartments, realistic seller pricing and properties suited to owner-occupation or long-term rental generally have a wider buyer pool. Large houses with high energy costs, legally uncertain holiday use or expensive renovation requirements may remain illiquid even in a recovering market.

Liquidity test: estimate the number of realistic future buyers for the exact property. A Vienna apartment near transport and employment has a different exit market from a luxury chalet that can only be used as a main residence.
05 · BUYER BEHAVIOUR

What is absorbing first

  • Well-located existing apartments priced below equivalent new construction.
  • Energy-efficient homes with predictable monthly operating costs.
  • Vienna units suitable for owner-occupation or legally clean long-term rental.
  • Regional homes connected to employment, universities or transport.
  • Scarce Alpine assets where permitted use is confirmed before purchase.
Weakest assumption: that every Austrian residential asset benefits equally from lower interest rates. Financing helps demand, but it does not repair a poor micro-location, an overregulated income model or excessive renovation exposure.
06 · SUPPLY & CONSTRUCTION

The delivery pipeline is still contracting

Austria completed 54,472 dwellings in 2024, 15.59% fewer than in the previous year. New-building completions fell more sharply, by about 20.19% to 41,762 units. Permit activity improved in the first three quarters of 2025, but from the weakest 2024 base since 2010, so new supply is unlikely to recover quickly.

Vienna illustrates the lag. Housing completions dropped to 9,688 units in 2025 and were expected to decline again in 2026. Privately financed rental delivery weakened particularly sharply. The immediate implication is upward pressure on new-market rents; the medium-term implication is stronger value support for well-maintained existing homes.

REPLACEMENT-COST LAYER

Existing property may gain relative value

High land, labour, construction and financing costs limit how cheaply new homes can be delivered. Existing properties that are structurally sound and energy-efficient can therefore compete well even if they need cosmetic modernization.

  • Compare the purchase price with true renovation-adjusted replacement cost.
  • Inspect reserves and planned common-building work.
  • Do not confuse a low asking price with low total capital required.
07 · RENTAL MARKET

Strong demand, but gross yield is only the starting point

Actual rents captured by national statistics averaged €507.3 per dwelling and €7.7 per square meter in Q3 2025. These figures include a broad mix of existing contracts and regulated or subsidized housing, so they should not be used as direct market rent assumptions for a newly purchased apartment.

Across the monitored private submarkets, estimated gross yields averaged 3.82% in January 2026. Vienna showed a stronger 4.84% average, Graz approximately 3.73% and Salzburg about 2.91%. The gap demonstrates why high-price prestige markets can deliver weaker income returns despite strong long-term demand.

08 · VIENNA RENTAL LAYER

One city, multiple rent systems

Vienna’s rental market combines municipal housing, subsidized stock, older regulated buildings and newer privately financed units. New-build and first-occupancy asking rents reported in late 2025 ranged from roughly €13.97/m² in District 11 to €23.30/m² in District 1, far above the broad national average.

For an investor, the decisive issue is not the portal rent but the legally achievable rent for the exact building and contract. Construction date, subsidy status, apartment category, lease structure and statutory rules can materially alter cash flow.

Real Estate Taxi rule: never calculate a Vienna yield until the legal rent regime has been identified in writing by a qualified local professional.
09 · MORTGAGE MARKET

Credit conditions improved, but 3%–4% financing is the new baseline

The average rate on new Austrian housing loans was 3.38% in December 2025, down from 4.16% two years earlier. Demand for housing loans strengthened through 2025, and the value of all new housing lending rose to approximately €22.7 billion.

The expiry of the KIM-V mortgage ordinance in mid-2025 gave banks more flexibility, but prudent standards remain relevant. Buyers should still expect close scrutiny of equity, debt service, affordability and term—particularly for non-residents or income generated outside Austria.

FINANCING STRESS TEST

Model the property, not only the interest rate

  • Test debt service at least 1.5 percentage points above the offered rate.
  • Include non-recoverable building charges and reserve contributions.
  • Use a realistic vacancy and maintenance allowance.
  • Check fixed-rate expiry, prepayment conditions and currency of income.
10 · FOREIGN BUYERS

EU and EEA access is simpler; third-country rules are state-specific

EU and EEA citizens are generally treated like Austrian citizens for foreign-property approval. Third-country nationals usually require authorization, but the exact process and exemptions depend on the federal state and, in some cases, bilateral agreements.

Ownership eligibility is only one layer. Agricultural or forestry property can require separate land-transfer authorization, and holiday-home or second-residence use may be restricted under state planning law and municipal zoning. A buyer can therefore be legally capable of purchasing a property but unable to use it in the intended way.

EU / EEA buyer

Usually exempt from foreign-national acquisition authorization, but still subject to planning, land-use and property-specific rules.

Third-country buyer

Authorization commonly required; social, cultural or economic interest and state-specific criteria may be relevant.

Agricultural land

Additional approval can apply regardless of nationality because productive land is regulated separately.

Holiday or second home

Permitted use must be checked in state planning law, municipal zoning and the property’s approvals before signing.

11 · ACQUISITION COSTS

The purchase price may represent only about 89%–94% of total capital required

Austria’s official guidance lists several material transaction costs. The standard real estate transfer tax is 3.5% of the purchase price, and registration of ownership in the land register normally costs 1.1%. Legal or notarial work is commonly estimated at approximately 1%–3%, while an estate-agent commission can reach 3% plus 20% VAT for transactions above the statutory threshold.

12 · OWNERSHIP COSTS

Inspect the building, not only the apartment

  • Monthly building operating charges and reserve fund.
  • Planned roof, façade, lift, heating or energy works.
  • Insurance and property management.
  • Heating system and future decarbonization exposure.
  • Vacancy, reletting and legal administration.
  • Income tax and capital-gains treatment for the buyer’s residency and structure.

Tax outcomes depend on residency, ownership structure, treaty position, holding period and use. Obtain an Austrian tax calculation rather than applying a generic online percentage.

13 · RENT REGULATION

Legal rent can differ materially from market rent

Austria’s rental framework is not uniform across all homes. Building age, subsidy history, contract type and the applicability of the Tenancy Act can affect permissible rent, term, indexation and termination rights. New affordable-rent measures introduced common limits on the timing and scale of increases and extended the minimum fixed term for many new or renewed agreements.

This regulation supports tenant stability but creates a valuation issue: two physically similar apartments can have different investment values because one permits a market-oriented new lease while the other is subject to a lower legal rent or an existing protected tenancy.

Austrian rental due diligence should include a written legal-rent memorandum, the full lease history, subsidy status, construction date, tenant deposit, indexation method and termination position.
14 · ENERGY & BUILDING RISK

Energy performance is becoming a liquidity factor

An energy performance certificate is part of the sale and letting process, but the certificate alone is not enough. Buyers should identify the heating source, envelope quality, realistic consumption, pending building upgrades and the owners’ association’s ability to finance them.

Older urban stockPotentially strong location and rent demand, but façade, roof, windows, lift and heating modernization can be expensive.

18 · 2026–2028 SCENARIOS

Three plausible paths for Austrian residential property

Base case · Moderate nominal recovery

Mortgage rates remain broadly stable, transactions continue normalizing and national prices rise at a low single-digit rate. Real gains remain limited while regional divergence persists.

Upside case · Supply shortage becomes dominant

Weak completions collide with renewed household formation and investment demand. Vienna and supply-constrained western states outperform, while asking rents strengthen further.

Downside case · Economic weakness delays demand

Slow GDP growth, unemployment and fiscal pressure weaken household confidence. Transaction activity remains improved, but sellers must accept wider discounts in non-prime and renovation-heavy segments.

20 · OUTLOOK

Austria is more likely to normalize than surge

The most likely near-term outcome is continued moderate nominal price growth supported by lower financing pressure and recovering demand. The market’s stronger medium-term support comes from supply: the current construction downturn will affect completions for several years, and replacement costs remain high.

Performance should remain uneven. Vienna and scarce western states can benefit from structural demand, while less connected rural markets may recover slowly. Existing efficient apartments may outperform renovation-heavy stock because buyers increasingly evaluate total monthly and future capital costs.

Most likely direction: improving transaction liquidity, low single-digit nominal price growth and continued rent pressure in undersupplied urban markets—without a return to the broad, rate-driven acceleration of the pre-2022 cycle.
BOTTOM LINE

Austria rewards legal and technical precision

A good Austrian purchase is supported by a real buyer or tenant pool, a verified use, manageable building costs and a price that remains logical after transaction friction.

  • Value locally, not nationally.

  • Budget building-level capital expenditure.

21 · FAQ

Austria real estate questions

Are Austrian property prices rising again in 2026?

Official data show a mild nominal recovery after the 2022–2024 correction. Inflation-adjusted prices remained negative in late 2025, so the market had stabilized rather than fully regained its prior real value.

Can a Croatian or other EU citizen buy property in Austria?

EU and EEA citizens are generally treated like Austrian citizens for foreign-buyer authorization. Agricultural-land controls, holiday-home restrictions, municipal zoning and other property-specific rules can still apply.

Can a non-EU citizen buy Austrian property?

Often yes, but authorization requirements are governed by the individual federal states. Eligibility, exemptions and required evidence should be confirmed before making an unconditional offer.

What are the normal buyer transaction costs?

The official framework generally includes 3.5% real estate transfer tax, 1.1% land-register fee, legal or notarial work, and potentially agent commission, mortgage registration and administrative costs. A transaction-specific calculation is required.

Which Austrian state has the highest apartment prices?

Vienna had the highest condominium transaction price per square meter in the H1 2025 comparison, while Vorarlberg and Salzburg were close behind. Prime resort areas can exceed their state averages substantially.

Is Vienna a high-yield rental market?

Vienna can show a better gross yield than Salzburg or some premium Austrian markets, but legal rent controls, acquisition costs and building expenses can materially reduce the net return.

Can I buy an Alpine apartment and use it as a holiday home?

Not automatically. Second-home and holiday-use restrictions can apply under state planning law and municipal zoning. The intended use must be confirmed for the exact unit before purchase.

What is the biggest hidden risk when buying in Austria?

For investors, the biggest hidden risk is often a rent or use assumption that is not legally achievable. For owner-occupiers, large future building works and energy-upgrade costs are common underestimated risks.

Is new construction safer than an older apartment?

New construction can reduce immediate maintenance and energy risk, but usually commands a premium and may provide a lower yield. A well-inspected existing apartment can offer better value if building reserves and planned works are understood.
