02 · DIRECT MARKET ANSWER

A resilient but highly segmented market: rents are rising faster than asking prices, new-home demand remains active, and high interest rates still shape liquidity

Brazil’s residential market in mid-2026 is not one uniform boom. The nationwide FipeZAP asking-price composite rose 5.59% over the twelve months to June, slightly above the 4.64% national consumer inflation rate over the same period. Asking rents rose faster, by 9.00%, supporting rental income but also signalling affordability pressure.

At the same time, ABECIP’s IGMI-R — based on residential property valuations connected to mortgage operations — increased 18.45% over the twelve months to May. That much stronger figure does not prove that every Brazilian home appreciated by 18.45%; the index has a different sample, valuation process and financing mix. Serious analysis therefore focuses on city, segment, property type, ticket size, buyer profile and data source.

03 · DATA HIERARCHY

What each Brazil dataset can — and cannot — prove

Brazil has several useful market indicators, but none is a single nationwide database of every completed residential sale. The first due-diligence task is to understand what was measured.

01

Official and transaction-linked evidence

ABECIP IGMI-R follows the value of residential properties used as collateral in mortgage operations. It is closer to transaction activity than a listing index, but still reflects the composition of financed and appraised properties rather than all cash and financed sales.

Land-registry and municipal ITBI records are the legal and tax evidence for individual transactions. Coverage and publication quality vary by municipality and registry system, so buyers should obtain property-specific registry documents instead of assuming a national headline is enough.

02

Asking-price and asking-rent evidence

FipeZAP is calculated from advertised prices for ready residential apartments across a broad city sample. It is timely and useful for direction, price-per-square-metre screening and city comparison.

It does not reveal the final negotiated price, concessions, furniture value, transaction failure, legal quality or condition of the unit. Asking-price growth can coexist with larger discounts or slower liquidity.

03

Developer and broker market evidence

CBIC, ABRAINC, Secovi and local market studies provide launches, sales, final supply, sales velocity and segment detail, particularly for new developments. These datasets are valuable but often represent participating companies or selected metropolitan markets.

Real Estate Taxi uses them as market evidence — not as a substitute for title review, valuation and project-level financial analysis.

04

Price direction: nominal gains are positive, but real performance is modest in the listing index

The FipeZAP composite for ready residential apartments rose 0.45% in June 2026, 5.59% over twelve months, and reached an average advertised value of R$9,852.94 per m². IBGE reported national IPCA inflation of 4.64% over twelve months to June 2026.

On a simple comparison, national listed apartment prices increased by roughly 0.9 percentage point more than consumer inflation. This is positive real asking-price movement, but not evidence of a large nationwide real capital gain after purchase costs, selling costs, vacancy, maintenance and taxes.

Real Estate Taxi reading: Brazil’s national listing market is appreciating, but the investment case in 2026 is more strongly supported by location selection and rental income than by assuming rapid nationwide real-price expansion.

06

The appraisal index tells a stronger story — but for a different market sample

ABECIP’s IGMI-R recorded +0.53% in May 2026, +4.60% from January to May and +18.45% over twelve months. The indicator is derived from property valuations associated with mortgage activity.

The gap versus FipeZAP’s 5.59% asking-price growth is analytically important. It may reflect different city weights, financed-property characteristics, appraisal timing, unit mix and market segments. It should not be averaged with FipeZAP or presented as a contradiction without first understanding both methodologies.

For a buyer, the correct response is to obtain recent executable comparables and a property-specific valuation — not to choose the index with the highest growth rate.
07

New-home demand remained resilient in Q1 2026

CBIC reported 110,722 residential units sold in Q1 2026, 4.1% more than in the same quarter of 2025. Minha Casa, Minha Vida accounted for 54,510 units, or about 49% of the quarter’s sales.

This shows that national new-home demand is strongly segmented. Subsidized and lower-ticket housing has different credit access, buyer incentives and sales velocity from middle- and high-income projects. A headline for “Brazilian new homes” may therefore conceal a soft luxury segment or a strong affordable segment.

08

Supply, launches and the development pipeline

Brazil entered 2026 after a large 2025 development cycle. CBIC reported 471,769 launches and 433,681 sales during 2025. In São Paulo, Global Property Guide’s review of Secovi-SP data noted record 2025 launches, followed by a 22.1% year-on-year decline in launches during January–February 2026 while sales continued to rise.

The market risk is therefore not simply “too much” or “too little” supply. It is mismatched supply: wrong price points, micro-locations, layouts, completion dates or financing structures. Buyers of off-plan units should review the project’s sales velocity, remaining inventory, construction timetable, developer balance sheet and bank financing.

09

Construction costs and replacement-cost pressure

IBGE’s SINAPI national construction cost increased 1.19% in June 2026 to R$1,976.37 per m², comprising R$1,114.74 for materials and R$861.63 for labour in the national reference basket.

SINAPI is not a market value and does not include land, finance, developer margin, professional fees, taxes, premium specifications or unusual site conditions. It is nevertheless useful for understanding cost pressure, insurance adequacy and whether an unusually low new-build price is realistic.

10

Apartments versus houses, and new versus existing stock

National data visibility is strongest for advertised ready apartments and professionally marketed new developments. Detached houses, gated-community homes, informal extensions and self-built stock can be far less standardized.

  • Apartments: easier price-per-m² comparison, but condominium fees, special assessments, building rules and short-term-rental restrictions can materially change returns.
  • Houses: more land exposure and privacy, but higher security, maintenance, boundary, drainage and unauthorized-construction risk.
  • New builds: modern systems and staged payments, but completion, indexation, specification and developer-solvency risk.
  • Existing homes: immediate inspection and established neighbourhood evidence, but hidden defects and regularization problems may be significant.
11

Market liquidity is local, ticket-sensitive and financing-sensitive

Brazil’s large urban markets can be liquid at mainstream price points, while luxury coastal units, oversized apartments, unusual houses and properties with documentation problems may take much longer to sell.

Liquidity analysis should include comparable days on market, listing revisions, negotiated discounts, competing inventory, financing eligibility and buyer depth. A property advertised for years at the same price is not evidence of market value.

Exit liquidity should be underwritten before purchase. The buyer who needs a fast resale may face a materially larger discount than the buyer who can wait through a credit cycle.
12

Mortgage credit recovered strongly in early 2026, but borrowing remains expensive

ABECIP reported R$17.17 billion of SBPE residential financing in May 2026. January–May financing totalled R$76.5 billion, up 23.9% from the same period of 2025. May financed 48,100 acquisitions and construction transactions.

However, the Selic policy rate was still 14.25% per year after the June 2026 Copom decision. Mortgage pricing depends on lender, borrower profile, loan-to-value, funding source, TR/indexation and product structure. Foreign non-resident borrowers may face narrower access and more documentation than resident Brazilian borrowers.

13

Inflation, interest rates and the real-return hurdle

IBGE reported June 2026 IPCA inflation of 0.16%, 3.36% year-to-date and 4.64% over twelve months. High nominal interest rates raise the opportunity cost of holding a low-yield property and make leveraged buy-to-let particularly sensitive to financing terms.

A gross residential yield around 5%–7% is not automatically attractive when risk-free local nominal rates are high. Property may still provide inflation-linked rent, diversification, personal use and long-term appreciation, but the comparison must be made after vacancy, condominium fees, IPTU, insurance, repairs, tax, management and currency risk.

04 · RENTAL AND OPERATING ECONOMICS

Rents are rising faster than sale asking prices — but gross yield is not net cash flow

The rental opportunity varies sharply by city, unit size, tenant profile, condominium rules, management quality and whether the owner is resident or non-resident.

14

Long-term rental market

FipeZAP’s composite advertised rent increased 9.00% over the twelve months to June 2026, faster than both its sale-price index and IPCA. Average listed rent reached R$53.79 per m² per month.

Long-term leases are governed by Brazil’s tenancy framework and the contract. The owner should verify permitted indexation, guarantees, responsibility for condominium charges and IPTU, inspection procedures, late-payment enforcement and termination rights. Legal drafting and local property management matter more than a national rent headline.

15

Gross versus realistic net rental yield

FipeZAP’s June rent-to-sale ratio implied an annualized composite gross yield of about 6.13%. Global Property Guide’s apartment sample produced a 5.71% national average gross yield in Q1 2026. Different values are expected because the datasets use different properties, cities and methodologies.

A realistic net-yield model should deduct vacancy, leasing commission, monthly management, condominium fees not recoverable from the tenant, IPTU where owner-paid, insurance, repairs, appliance/furniture replacement, legal and accounting costs, and income tax. A gross 6% property can become a 3%–4.5% net property, or lower, depending on the building and tenant turnover.

16

City yield screen and the danger of chasing the highest number

In the June FipeZAP sample, simple annualized advertised ratios were approximately 8.56% in Recife, 8.47% in Santos, 8.29% in Cuiabá, 8.23% in Belém, 7.15% in Barueri, 6.44% in Brasília, 6.43% in São Paulo and 6.25% in Rio de Janeiro.

Higher yield may compensate for weaker appreciation, tenant risk, smaller units, higher turnover, older stock or less liquid locations. The correct comparison is net operating income divided by the all-in acquisition basis, not portal rent divided by portal asking price.

17

Short-term rental potential and building-rule risk

Short-term rentals can be attractive in Rio de Janeiro, São Paulo business districts, Florianópolis, northeastern beach markets and selected tourism destinations. Revenue is highly seasonal and must be tested against occupancy, daily rate, cleaning, utilities, platform fees, local rules and professional management.

Condominium conventions and building decisions can restrict or prohibit short stays. Buyers should obtain the registered condominium convention, internal regulations and recent meeting minutes before relying on Airbnb-style income.

18

Currency exposure for foreign investors

Brazilian property prices, rents and most operating costs are denominated in BRL. A foreign investor’s return in USD, EUR or GBP can differ materially from the local-currency return because of exchange-rate movement.

Do not describe a BRL gain as a hard-currency gain without converting every cash flow at the relevant dates. Currency diversification can be a benefit, but unhedged exchange exposure can also erase rental income and capital appreciation.

19

Affordability and tenant depth

Rent growth above inflation supports landlords but can weaken tenant affordability. High borrowing costs also keep some households in the rental market while making home purchase difficult.

Tenant-depth analysis should be neighbourhood-specific: employment nodes, universities, hospitals, transit, household income, competing supply and unit-size demand. Premium finish does not create premium tenant depth in a weak micro-location.

05 · BUYING, TAX AND LEGAL STRUCTURE

Foreigners can generally buy urban property, but CPF, payment route, registry and rural-land rules require disciplined execution

Brazil’s property system is formal and registrable, yet buyers must verify the exact matrícula, seller authority, debts, construction regularity and tax treatment. Rural and border-area acquisitions require specialist advice.

20

Foreign-buyer access and CPF

Foreign individuals can generally acquire urban real estate in Brazil, whether resident or non-resident, subject to documentation, tax-registration, banking and compliance requirements. A CPF taxpayer number is normally essential for the transaction and ongoing tax administration.

Foreign ownership does not by itself grant residence. Immigration, tax residence, property ownership and banking are separate legal questions.

21

Rural land and special-zone restrictions

Foreign acquisition and leasing of rural land are subject to federal limitations, size thresholds, municipal concentration rules, project requirements and approvals. Border-zone and strategic-area rules can add another layer.

A property marketed as a “farm”, “eco-retreat”, “development land” or “large coastal estate” should not be treated as an ordinary urban apartment until the cadastral classification, rural registrations, environmental status and foreign-ownership eligibility are confirmed in writing.

22

Standard buying procedure

  1. Engage independent Brazilian counsel and obtain CPF/banking capacity.
  2. Identify the exact property matrícula and competent real-estate registry.
  3. Verify seller identity, marital status, authority, corporate documents and succession issues.
  4. Review title, liens, lawsuits, tax certificates, condominium debt, occupancy and construction regularity.
  5. Agree price, payment milestones, currency conversion and conditions precedent in a written instrument.
  6. Pay applicable municipal ITBI and execute the required public deed or financing instrument.
  7. Register the transfer in the property registry; signing alone does not complete ownership transfer.
  8. Update municipal, condominium, utility, insurance and tax records.
23

Purchase taxes and closing costs

Brazil does not have one national all-in purchase-cost rate. The principal transfer tax, ITBI, is municipal and its rate and taxable base vary. Buyers may also pay notarial deed fees, land-registry fees, sworn translations, apostilles, legal fees, valuation, banking and currency-transfer costs.

Budget from written local quotations based on the actual transaction value, municipality and ownership structure. Do not rely on a generic percentage from another city.

24

Annual ownership, rental income and capital gains

Recurring costs can include municipal IPTU, condominium fees, insurance, maintenance, utilities during vacancy, accounting and tax compliance. Rural property can involve ITR and separate cadastral obligations.

Rental income and capital gains are taxable under rules that depend on residence status, ownership vehicle, deductions, treaty position and transaction structure. Non-resident withholding and repatriation documentation require specific tax advice before purchase, not only when selling.

25

Title and registry due diligence

  • Current full matrícula and chain of ownership.
  • Mortgages, attachments, usufruct, easements, fiduciary transfer and judicial restrictions.
  • Seller civil, tax, labour and federal certificates where relevant.
  • Spousal consent, inheritance and corporate-authority requirements.
  • Condominium debt and extraordinary assessments.
  • Municipal tax debt, occupancy permits and approved plans.
  • Possession, tenant rights and any unregistered occupants.

The decisive evidence is property- and seller-specific. A clean-looking online listing is not legal due diligence.

26

Cadastral, zoning and construction regularity

Compare the registry description, municipal cadastral record, survey, physical boundaries and actual built area. Extensions, rooftop enclosures, pools, extra floors, merged units and parking spaces may not be properly approved or registered.

Verify zoning, heritage restrictions, environmental licensing, coastal rules, setbacks, right of way, utility capacity and any pending regularization. Unregularized area can impair financing, insurance, resale and redevelopment.

27

New-development and off-plan risk

Review the incorporation registration, land title, approved project, developer and builder, bank financing, construction schedule, specification, price indexation, delay clauses, defect warranty, cancellation rights and treatment of common areas.

Model every installment and indexation mechanism. A low headline launch price can become expensive after construction-index adjustments, legal costs, upgrades, parking, furniture and delayed delivery.

28

Payment route, AML and source-of-funds

Foreign buyers should document the lawful origin and conversion of funds, use regulated financial channels and preserve contracts, bank records, exchange contracts and tax registrations. Banks, notaries and registries may request beneficial-owner and source-of-funds evidence.

Do not pay an agent, seller representative or developer account that is inconsistent with the contract and legal ownership structure. Independent confirmation of recipient details is essential.

29

Condominium governance can change the economics

Condominium fees in Brazil can be substantial, especially in buildings with pools, elevators, security, staff, leisure facilities or deferred maintenance. Special assessments can materially reduce net yield.

Review at least the latest budgets, financial statements, arrears, reserve fund, insurance, lawsuits, engineering reports and meeting minutes. Confirm whether the seller or buyer is responsible for approved but unpaid extraordinary works.

06 · RISK, SUITABILITY AND OUTLOOK

Brazil can suit patient, locally advised investors — but high rates, currency risk and uneven documentation punish generic strategies

The next two years are likely to produce different outcomes by city and segment rather than one national result.

30

Climate, insurance and building-condition risks

Brazil’s hazard profile includes river and urban flooding, landslides, intense rainfall, coastal erosion, wind, drought, wildfire and extreme heat. Coastal humidity, salt corrosion, mould, waterproofing failure and façade deterioration are material building risks.

Obtain flood and slope maps, inspect drainage and retaining structures, review prior claims, test insurance exclusions and confirm rebuilding limits. In apartment buildings, inspect the condominium’s engineering and reserve position — not only the interior of the unit.

31

Security and operational risk

Security conditions vary substantially by city, district and street. They affect tenant demand, vacancy, insurance, staff costs, parking value and resale liquidity.

Foreign or absentee owners should budget for professional management, secure payment controls, documented maintenance approvals, inventory records and periodic physical inspection. Weak management can destroy the return on an otherwise good asset.

32

Potentially suitable investor profiles

  • Long-term BRL investors seeking rental income and inflation-linked repricing.
  • Foreign cash buyers who understand currency exposure and can hold through cycles.
  • Local-market specialists targeting specific employment or tourism nodes.
  • Buyers able to add value through legal regularization, renovation or operational improvement.
  • Developers with strong local planning, funding and execution capacity.
33

Potentially unsuitable investor profiles

  • Buyers expecting property ownership to produce automatic residency.
  • Highly leveraged investors whose debt cost exceeds realistic net yield.
  • Short-term speculators relying on continued double-digit appreciation.
  • Remote buyers unwilling to employ independent legal, tax and technical advisers.
  • Investors relying on short-term rentals without checking condominium rules.
  • Foreign buyers considering rural land without specialist eligibility analysis.
34

Opportunity factors

  • Asking rents are rising faster than consumer inflation and sale asking prices.
  • New-home demand remained positive in Q1 2026 despite restrictive rates.
  • Early-2026 mortgage finance volumes recovered from the prior year.
  • Large city and tourism markets offer diverse tenant bases.
  • Local market dispersion creates opportunities for research-driven selection.
  • Potential future rate declines could support affordability and transaction activity.
35

Risk factors

  • Selic remains high, raising financing cost and the return hurdle.
  • Currency depreciation can reduce foreign-investor returns.
  • Portal asking prices may overstate executable value.
  • Condominium fees and special assessments can destroy headline yield.
  • Documentation, unapproved construction and possession issues can impair resale.
  • Flood, landslide, coastal and building-condition risks are highly location-specific.
  • Supply can be excessive in a narrow segment even when national sales are strong.

37

Detailed buyer and investor due-diligence checklist

  • Define investment currency, required net return and holding period.
  • Obtain independent valuation using recent executable comparables.
  • Calculate all-in basis: price, ITBI, registry, deed, legal, FX and renovation.
  • Verify CPF, banking and source-of-funds route before signing.
  • Order complete matrícula and seller certificates.
  • Confirm marital, inheritance and corporate authority.
  • Reconcile registry, municipal cadastre, survey and physical area.
  • Check zoning, permits, occupancy and environmental restrictions.
  • Review condominium convention, rules, accounts and minutes.
  • Inspect structure, waterproofing, façade, roof, electrical and plumbing systems.
  • Check flood, landslide, coastal, heat and insurance risks.
  • Test long-term rent with current signed comparables, not only asking rents.
  • Model vacancy, management, tax, IPTU, condominium and repairs.
  • Confirm short-term-rental legality and building permission.
  • For off-plan: verify incorporation registration, financing and indexation.
  • For rural property: obtain specialist foreign-ownership eligibility opinion.
  • Model BRL appreciation and depreciation scenarios.
  • Plan tax compliance, income repatriation and eventual sale before purchase.
  • Reconfirm recipient bank details independently before each payment.
  • Register the transfer and retain complete closing evidence.
07 · FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

Brazil residential property questions

Are Brazilian residential prices rising in 2026?
Yes in nominal asking-price terms: FipeZAP rose 5.59% over the twelve months to June 2026. That was only moderately above 4.64% consumer inflation and does not represent every completed sale.
What is the average apartment asking price in Brazil?
The June 2026 FipeZAP composite was R$9,852.94 per m² for ready residential apartments in its city sample. Local averages ranged from below R$6,000 to above R$15,000 per m².
What rental yield can a buyer expect?
FipeZAP’s June composite implied roughly 6.13% gross annualized, while Global Property Guide’s Q1 2026 apartment sample averaged 5.71%. A realistic net yield can be materially lower after vacancy, condominium, tax, maintenance and management.
Can a foreigner buy an apartment in Brazil?
Generally yes for urban property, subject to CPF, documentation, banking, compliance and registration. Rural and special-zone properties are more restricted.
Does buying property provide Brazilian residence?
Not automatically. Property ownership and immigration status are separate. Obtain current immigration advice for any residence or investment route.
Is the market dependent on Minha Casa, Minha Vida?
The program is a major driver of the new-home market. CBIC reported that it represented about 49% of national Q1 2026 sales in its sample, so performance differs materially between subsidized housing and higher-end segments.
Is mortgage finance improving?
SBPE financing recovered strongly in January–May 2026, but the Selic rate remained 14.25%, so borrowing conditions were still restrictive and borrower-specific.
What are the main purchase costs?
Municipal ITBI, notarial deed, land registration, legal, translation, banking and currency-transfer costs. The total varies by municipality, value and structure; obtain written local estimates.
Can a condominium ban short-term rentals?
Building conventions, internal rules and condominium decisions can restrict short stays. Review the registered documents and meeting minutes before underwriting short-term-rental revenue.
What is the biggest due-diligence mistake?
Treating the online listing and signed purchase promise as proof of clean ownership. The exact matrícula, seller authority, debts, permits, physical area and registered transfer must all be independently verified.
