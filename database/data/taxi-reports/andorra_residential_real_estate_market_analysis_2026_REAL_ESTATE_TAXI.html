Andorra Residential Real Estate Market Analysis 2026

Andorra is a small, supply-constrained mountain market where premium international demand, limited developable land and a renter-heavy population continue to push prices and rents upward. The opportunity is real, but so are the legal, affordability and entry-price risks.

01 · EXECUTIVE INTELLIGENCE

A market supported by scarcity, but increasingly shaped by policy

Andorra’s residential market is not a conventional high-volume European housing market. Its physical geography sharply limits buildable land, its population and workforce create strong rental demand, and its tax and lifestyle appeal attracts affluent residents and cross-border capital. These forces have produced unusually persistent upward pressure on both sale prices and rents.

The central tension is that the same characteristics that support property values also create a severe affordability problem. Public policy is therefore moving in two directions at once: expanding affordable supply while restricting speculative and foreign demand. For buyers, this means that future performance will depend not only on location and asset quality, but also on permitted use, buyer status and regulatory fit.

Real Estate Taxi interpretation: Andorra remains a scarcity market rather than a volume-growth market. The strongest assets are likely to be legally simple, genuinely scarce, well located and suitable for long-term use—not purchases dependent on aggressive short-term-rental assumptions.
REAL ESTATE TAXI VERDICT

High-quality market, high entry discipline required

Andorra can suit capital-preservation buyers, lifestyle purchasers and long-term investors. It is less forgiving for buyers who overpay based on asking prices or assume yesterday’s foreign-investment rules still apply.

  • Structural supply is genuinely limited.
  • Rental demand is broad, but regulation is tenant-sensitive.
  • Premium parishes can command very large price differences.
  • Foreign-buyer legal screening must happen before negotiation becomes binding.

04 · SUPPLY

Geography creates a structural supply ceiling

Andorra had approximately 43,641 dwellings in 2023, excluding tourist apartments, plus about 3,149 units designated for tourist accommodation. The stock is concentrated in the capital and Escaldes-Engordany, while Ordino remains lower-density and more exclusive.

New construction has often favored higher-margin luxury product because scarce land, construction costs and affluent demand make affordable development less attractive. Public and public-private programs are attempting to increase regulated rental supply, including a target of roughly 500 affordable units by 2027.

The supply problem is not only “too few homes.” It is also a mismatch between the type of housing being built and the price level needed by residents and workers.
05 · DEMAND

2025 transactions surged—but the law-change effect matters

Residential transactions jumped to 1,630 in the first three quarters of 2025, an 86.5% annual increase. Part of that rebound likely reflects buyers completing transactions under transitional arrangements before or around the tightening of foreign-investment rules.

Demand is supported by residents, first-time buyers, affluent relocators, lifestyle buyers and international investors. Yet future foreign demand is likely to become more selective because permissible property quantity, permitted use and investment purpose now matter more.

Resident demandSupported by a tight labor market and high rental costs.
International demandStill relevant, but more regulated and legally segmented.
Luxury demandStrongest in prime, low-density and central premium areas.
First-time buyersSupported by a public guarantee and interest-subsidy program for eligible residents.
06 · RENTAL MARKET

Strong rents, broad tenant demand and limited available stock

Andorra is unusually renter-heavy: 64.9% of households rented their home in 2024. Geography, immigration-driven workforce needs and expensive ownership have combined to keep tenant demand high.

LANDLORD REALITY

Demand is attractive; regulation reduces flexibility

Andorra’s rental framework has shifted toward stronger tenant protection. Forced lease extensions and limits on rent adjustments have supported existing tenants but may discourage some owners from placing homes on the market.

  • Do not model unlimited annual rent increases.
  • Confirm current lease-extension rules before buying an occupied property.
  • Check whether tourist or short-term use is legally authorized and available to the buyer.
  • Model returns using a long-term rental case first, then treat other uses as optional upside.
07 · FOREIGN BUYER RULES

The 2025 Omnibus Law changed the investment framework

Foreign ownership has not been eliminated, but it is more constrained. The 2025 law introduced tighter limits, expanded the definition of foreign investor, strengthened authorization and monitoring, and directed foreign capital away from speculative and tourist-use housing.

Quantity limits

Foreign individuals and certain foreign-controlled entities are generally limited to one single-family property or up to two residential units over a ten-year period, subject to annex and entity conditions.

Tourist-use restriction

Foreign real-estate investment cannot generally be directed into tourist accommodation or homes authorized for short-term rental.

Development restrictions

Foreign-financed development for sale is generally restricted, with exceptions linked to long-term and affordable rental objectives or compliant planning areas.

Progressive foreign-investment tax

The framework gives more favorable treatment to investments aligned with primary residence and affordable long-term rental objectives.

This is a market-intelligence summary, not legal advice. The buyer’s residence status, ownership structure, property use, prior holdings and transaction date can change the applicable treatment.
PURCHASE PROCESS

Recommended buyer sequence

  1. Classify the buyer: resident, recent resident, non-resident or foreign-controlled entity.
  2. Confirm the property’s legal category, permitted use and foreign-buyer eligibility.
  3. Obtain foreign-investment authorization before creating an unconditional obligation.
  4. Verify title, charges, planning, occupancy, energy and rental documentation.
  5. Model acquisition tax, notary, legal, financing and resale costs.
  6. Only then finalize deposit, financing and closing documents.
08 · MORTGAGE MARKET

Borrowing activity accelerated as Euribor eased

Andorran mortgage rates rose from 1.21% in 2021 to 3.79% in 2024. By January 2026, advertised bank products indicated lower entry rates than the 2023–2024 peak, although many offers remained linked to 12-month Euribor.

In the first half of 2025, 426 new residential mortgages totaling €173.9 million were reported. That represented annual growth of 38.3% in volume and 82.0% in value, suggesting both stronger activity and larger average loans.

Financing risk is not only the starting rate. Buyers should stress-test the loan at a higher Euribor level and compare the mortgage payment with realistic long-term rent—not peak advertised rent.
09 · MACRO OVERLAY

Economic growth remains supportive, but is expected to moderate

The IMF’s May 2026 assessment estimated real GDP growth at 3.9% in 2025, driven by financial services, real estate and construction, and projected growth to slow to 2.1% in 2026. Inflation was expected near 3% in 2026 before moving back toward the euro-area target.

A near-full-employment labor market and strong service economy support housing demand. At the same time, housing shortages can become a constraint on business expansion because employers struggle to house workers.

SupportiveHigh employment, affluent residents, finance and tourism activity.
ConstrainingHousing affordability, limited labor accommodation and policy intervention.

11 · OPPORTUNITIES

Where value may still exist

  • Well-located existing homes with legal clarity and lower replacement risk.
  • Properties below the luxury threshold in parishes with strong resident demand.
  • Energy-efficient, parking-equipped homes suitable for year-round occupancy.
  • Assets in Encamp or Sant Julià de Lòria where entry prices may be lower than in prime central markets.
  • Long-term rental properties aligned with government housing objectives.
  • Rare premium homes in Ordino or Escaldes where location quality is difficult to replicate.
12 · RISKS

Main red flags before buying

  • Using national asking-price averages as a valuation.
  • Assuming foreign-buyer permission is automatic.
  • Paying a luxury premium for ordinary construction or a weak micro-location.
  • Relying on short-term rental income that is not legally available.
  • Underestimating tenant protection and lease-extension rules.
  • Ignoring parking, winter access, slope, sunlight and energy performance.
  • Using gross yield without tax, vacancy, repairs and financing.
  • Expecting recent double-digit price growth to continue indefinitely.
13 · SCENARIO ANALYSIS

Possible 2026–2027 market paths

Base case · Slower growth, firm prices

Limited stock, high replacement costs and resident demand keep values supported, but price growth moderates as regulation and affordability reduce transaction velocity.

Upside case · Scarcity dominates

New supply remains delayed, affluent demand persists and lower financing costs support another period of above-average price and rent growth in prime areas.

Downside case · Policy and affordability cool activity

Foreign-buyer restrictions, weaker confidence or higher financing costs reduce demand. Asking prices remain sticky, but transaction discounts widen and time on market increases.

14 · DUE DILIGENCE

Andorra property verification checklist

  • Foreign-investment eligibility and authorization route
  • Title, liens, easements and community debts
  • Planning compliance, occupancy permit and cadastral consistency
  • Authorized residential, tourist or rental use
  • Existing lease terms and tenant rights
  • Parking, storage and annex limits under the buyer’s authorization
  • Heating system, insulation, energy certificate and winter operating costs
  • Sun exposure, slope, access and snow-season practicality
  • Recent local transaction evidence—not only portal listings
  • Acquisition tax, foreign-investment tax, notary and legal costs
  • Mortgage stress test and early-repayment conditions
  • Exit liquidity for the same buyer category and permitted use
15 · OUTLOOK

Price pressure can persist without repeating the recent acceleration

Andorra’s fundamental scarcity is unlikely to disappear. New projects take time, developable land is limited and demand from residents and affluent international buyers remains meaningful. This supports values, particularly for property with strong location, legal clarity and year-round usability.

However, the market is entering a more regulated phase. The government is actively trying to redirect capital toward primary residence and affordable long-term rental while limiting speculative and tourist-use investment. That makes the market more asset-specific: legal fit and permitted use will matter increasingly alongside price per square meter.

Most likely direction: continued support for high-quality property, slower and more uneven price growth, stronger separation between legally useful homes and assets dependent on restricted investment strategies.
BOTTOM LINE

Buy the property—not the headline

A good Andorra purchase should remain sensible even if price growth slows. The strongest case combines a defensible micro-location, permitted use, sound building quality, realistic rent and a long holding period.

  • Value using comparable transactions.
  • Screen the buyer and use legally.
  • Stress-test financing and rent.
  • Prefer scarcity that is real, not merely marketed.
16 · FAQ

Andorra real estate questions

Are Andorra property prices still rising in 2026?

Available portal indicators show continued annual growth, but the pace differs depending on whether the dataset measures completed transactions, asking prices or an online market index. The correct conclusion is that price pressure remains positive—not that every property has risen by the headline rate.

Can foreigners still buy property in Andorra?

Yes, but purchases are subject to foreign-investment authorization, quantity limits, buyer-status rules and use restrictions. A legal opinion should be obtained before a reservation or deposit becomes unconditional.

Which parish is the most expensive?

Escaldes-Engordany showed the highest average apartment asking price per square meter in the Q3 2025 parish data, while Ordino was exceptionally expensive for both apartments and houses. Individual property quality can reverse the parish-level ranking.

What is the average rental yield?

A national gross average of 5.80% was reported for July 2025. Gross yield excludes acquisition costs, tax, repairs, management, vacancy, financing and regulatory restrictions, so net performance can be materially lower.

Is short-term rental a reliable investment strategy?

Not as a default assumption. Tourist and short-term rental use is regulated, and foreign investors face specific restrictions. The legal use of the exact unit must be confirmed before any income model is accepted.

Are asking prices negotiable?

Negotiability depends on parish, property quality, seller motivation and time on market. The wide difference between advertised and transaction-level datasets is a strong reason to request comparable completed sales before making an offer.

What purchase costs should a buyer expect?

Indicative secondary-market estimates suggest buyer costs can include transfer or registration tax, notary and legal fees. Foreign-investment tax may also apply. Exact costs must be calculated for the buyer, ownership structure, property type and current law.

What is the biggest hidden risk?

The biggest hidden risk is often legal-use mismatch: buying a property based on rental or ownership assumptions that are not permitted for that buyer or unit. The second is overvaluation based only on portal listings.
