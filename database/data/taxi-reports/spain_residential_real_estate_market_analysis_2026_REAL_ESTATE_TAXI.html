EXECUTIVE DECISION FRAME

Spain in one investment sentence

Spain entered mid-2026 with double-digit transaction-price growth, resilient mortgage demand and a structural shortage of homes in its largest cities, coastal corridors and island markets. The market is strong, but affordability pressure, regional tax differences, rental regulation and highly uneven micro-location liquidity make country-level averages insufficient for an actual purchase decision.

The strongest risk-adjusted opportunities are legally clean homes in markets with durable employment, population and transport demand, bought against verified comparable transactions and underwritten with realistic rent, vacancy, tax and exit assumptions. The weakest propositions are highly leveraged purchases, tourist-rental investments without confirmed licensing rights, and off-plan or resale assets whose registry, planning, community or occupancy documentation is incomplete.

01

2026 market snapshot

Spain’s National Statistics Institute reported that the national House Price Index rose 12.9% year-on-year in Q1 2026 and 3.5% quarter-on-quarter.

New-home prices increased 9.1% annually, while second-hand housing rose 13.5%. Both segments increased 3.5% from Q4 2025.

All autonomous communities recorded positive annual growth. Aragón and Murcia led at 15.6%, while the Basque Country recorded the lowest increase at 10.3%.

02

How to interpret Spain’s evidence

Official transaction-price evidence: INE’s House Price Index, based on notarized dwelling transactions.

Official registry evidence: Registradores de España transaction, mortgage and purchaser data.

Official appraisal and supply evidence: Ministry of Housing and Urban Agenda series and housing observatory publications.

Secondary asking evidence: listing-platform rents, prices and Global Property Guide yield estimates. Asking prices are not completed sale prices, and gross yields are not net returns.

PRICE CYCLE

From post-crisis recovery to structural undersupply

Spain’s pre-2008 housing cycle was driven by high construction and credit expansion. The subsequent correction reduced prices, transactions and development activity for years.

Since 2014, the market has moved through recovery, pandemic interruption and renewed expansion. The defining feature of the current cycle is not excessive national construction but insufficient delivery relative to household formation in the strongest urban, coastal and island markets.

That supply imbalance supports prices, but it does not eliminate local overvaluation, project risk or the possibility of slower liquidity when affordability becomes stretched.

03

Official INE House Price Index

The Q1 2026 annual increase of 12.9% was unchanged from Q4 2025, showing that Spain began 2026 at the same elevated national growth rate.

Second-hand housing represents the majority of the transaction market and rose faster than new housing, at 13.5% versus 9.1%. Investors should therefore avoid applying a single national growth number to both segments.

The INE series changed its reference base to 2025 from Q1 2026, but this rebasing did not alter previously published variation rates.

04

Regional price-growth map

Q1 2026 annual growth was broad: Aragón and Murcia reached 15.6%; Castilla y León and Ceuta 14.9%; Asturias, Valencia and La Rioja 14.3%; Madrid and the Balearic Islands 13.6%; Andalusia 13.3%; and Catalonia and Navarra 10.5%.

The highest annual growth does not automatically identify the best investment. Entry price, rent, tenant depth, future supply, tax and resale liquidity must be assessed together.

05

Registrars’ price evidence

Registradores de España reported that the average registered house price per square metre rose 8.9% year-on-year in Q1 2026 and 3.2% quarter-on-quarter.

The difference from the INE’s 12.9% annual index reflects methodology, transaction mix and statistical construction. Both are useful, but they should not be merged into one unsupported figure.

06

Transaction activity

Registradores recorded 178,096 home purchases in Q1 2026, only 0.1% below the previous quarter and still near the highest level since 2007.

New-home purchases reached 39,473 and increased 7.2% quarter-on-quarter. Used-home purchases reached 138,623 and declined 2.0%.

The rolling 12-month total was 701,828 purchases, indicating a high-volume market despite early-2026 monthly moderation.

07

Latest 2026 demand signal

The provisional Registradores advance for May 2026 showed more than 61,000 home purchases, 7.6% fewer than a year earlier. This was the fifth consecutive annual decline in monthly home sales.

The slowdown should be interpreted carefully: prices remained strong, housing availability was limited and the comparison followed an unusually active 2025 market.

A market can show falling transaction volume and rising prices when supply falls faster than demand.

08

National value map

Global Property Guide’s MIVAU-based Q4 2025 appraisal figures placed Madrid near EUR 3,902/m², the Balearic Islands EUR 3,810/m², Barcelona EUR 3,083/m², Málaga EUR 2,897/m², Las Palmas EUR 2,151/m², Alicante EUR 1,903/m², Valencia EUR 1,832/m² and Seville EUR 1,780/m².

These are provincial appraisal indicators, not asking prices for central-city apartments. A prime Madrid, Barcelona, Marbella, Ibiza or Palma property can trade far above its provincial average.

09

Madrid market intelligence

Madrid combines government, finance, technology, education, international business and deep domestic migration demand.

Salamanca, Chamberí, Retiro and Centro command premium pricing; Chamartín, Tetuán, Arganzuela and selected peripheral transport corridors can offer broader rental demand and different yield profiles.

Madrid remains one of Spain’s deepest resale markets, but investors must account for high entry prices, community charges, renovation costs and municipality-specific planning or short-stay rules.

10

Barcelona and Catalonia

Barcelona has deep international, professional, university and tourism demand, but it also has some of Spain’s most interventionist rental regulation.

The city’s investment case differs sharply between long-term residential letting, seasonal accommodation, student housing and licensed tourist use. The legality and permitted duration of the intended use must be confirmed before purchase.

Catalonia’s Q1 2026 annual INE price increase was 10.5%, below the national rate but still strong.

11

Valencia and Alicante corridor

Valencia combines population growth, logistics, services, universities, international residents and a large urban rental market.

Alicante province has one of Spain’s deepest foreign-buyer markets, with strong demand in Alicante city, Costa Blanca municipalities and retirement locations.

Investors should separate year-round employment markets from second-home and tourism markets, because seasonality, tenant turnover and resale demand differ materially.

12

Málaga and Costa del Sol

Málaga has evolved from a predominantly tourism market into a combined tourism, technology, professional and international-resident hub.

Marbella, Benahavís and Estepona serve premium international buyers, while Málaga city and surrounding municipalities have broader employment and rental demand.

High prices, community fees, short-stay regulation and new luxury supply make property-level underwriting essential.

13

Balearic Islands

The Balearic Islands combine extreme land scarcity, environmental constraints, tourism demand and international wealth.

Palma has the broadest year-round market; Ibiza and prime Mallorca locations are more exposed to luxury, seasonal and second-home demand.

Tourist-rental licensing, water availability, environmental restrictions, illegal extensions and rural land classification are critical due-diligence issues.

14

Canary Islands

The Canary Islands benefit from year-round tourism, international residents and a climate that reduces seasonality compared with mainland resort markets.

Las Palmas and Santa Cruz combine urban and tourism demand, while resort zones require separate operational analysis.

Island logistics, community rules, holiday-rental regulation and limited land can support value but also raise operating and renovation costs.

15

Murcia, inland Andalusia and lower-entry markets

Murcia led national annual house-price growth with Aragón in Q1 2026 at 15.6%, while still offering lower entry prices than Madrid, Barcelona, the Balearics or prime Costa del Sol.

Seville, Granada and Córdoba have strong urban, cultural, university and public-sector demand. Smaller coastal and inland locations can offer higher headline yields but thinner resale liquidity.

Lower purchase price does not compensate for weak tenant demand, car dependence, oversupply or unclear planning status.

16

Northern Spain and Galicia

Bilbao, San Sebastián, Santander, Oviedo, A Coruña, Vigo and Santiago offer urban and professional demand with different affordability and climate profiles.

Northern coastal markets may attract more lifestyle and climate-conscious second-home demand, but foreign-buyer depth remains lower than in the Mediterranean and islands.

Older building stock, façade obligations, lifts, damp, roofs and energy performance can materially affect net returns.

17

Foreign-buyer demand

Global Property Guide’s Registradores-based summary reports that foreign buyers represented about 13.82% of registered purchases on the rolling 12-month measure to Q4 2025.

Foreign demand is especially important in Alicante, the Balearic Islands, Málaga, the Canary Islands, Murcia, Girona and Almería.

Foreign-market depth can support resale and premium pricing, but it also increases sensitivity to exchange rates, visa rules, source-country economics and local political responses.

18

Mortgage rates and buyer leverage

Global Property Guide reported an average rate of 2.75% on new housing loans in February 2026 and 2.77% on outstanding housing loans.

Banco de España’s official January 2026 mortgage-market reference rate for loans over three years for free-market housing acquisition was 2.762%, while 12-month Euribor was 2.245%.

Actual borrower pricing depends on fixed or variable structure, term, income, age, loan-to-value, bank valuation and residency.

19

Mortgage activity in 2026

Registradores’ provisional May 2026 advance reported approximately 42,200 mortgages on homes, 0.5% fewer than a year earlier.

Earlier 2026 months had recorded stronger annual mortgage growth, showing that financing demand remains important even as home-sale volumes moderate.

A larger financed buyer pool can support transactions, but it also raises sensitivity to lending standards and valuation gaps.

20

Why this is not the 2007 credit cycle

Spain’s current expansion is supported more by household formation, employment, migration and insufficient supply than by the extreme credit and construction growth seen before 2008.

Household mortgage debt relative to GDP remains far below its previous peak, and a larger share of recent loans is fixed rate.

That reduces systemic vulnerability, but it does not protect an individual buyer from overpaying or taking excessive leverage.

21

Housing supply and completions

Global Property Guide’s MIVAU-based summary reports 80,792 free-market dwellings completed in 2025, 6.7% fewer than in 2024.

Free-market housing starts rose to approximately 121,827 units, up 8.6%, but the stronger pipeline has not yet solved the near-term shortage.

The gap is concentrated in Madrid, Barcelona, Valencia, Alicante, Murcia and other high-growth areas rather than evenly distributed across Spain.

22

Why supply remains slow

Planning and licensing delays, construction labour shortages, infrastructure constraints, land availability and development costs all slow delivery.

The Banco de España’s 2025 Annual Report identifies housing and real-estate constraints as a major structural issue.

An investor should inspect the local pipeline, not assume national undersupply guarantees the performance of every project.

23

New-build purchases

New homes offer energy efficiency, modern layouts, parking and lower immediate repair risk, but they require developer and completion due diligence.

The official buying guide recommends verifying that the developer exists, the land is registered in the developer’s name and the project documentation is consistent before signing.

Deposit guarantees, stage payments, specification, completion date, licences, first-occupancy documentation, community creation and defect liability should be reviewed by an independent lawyer.

24

Resale purchases

Resale homes provide immediate inspection and an established building history, but hidden liabilities can be substantial.

The registry nota simple should confirm the owner, mortgages, attachments, restrictions, special regimes, social-housing status and coordination with Catastro.

The buyer must also verify community debts, IBI, approved building works, illegal alterations, occupancy status and energy or technical inspection obligations.

25

Long-term rental market

Spain’s national rental market is highly segmented. Madrid, Barcelona, Málaga, Valencia, Palma and the islands have strong demand but high purchase prices and increasing regulation.

Global Property Guide reported nationwide median asking rent near EUR 15.0/m² in March 2026, with Madrid at EUR 21.3/m², Balearic Islands EUR 19.7/m², Barcelona EUR 19.2/m², Málaga EUR 16.8/m² and Valencia EUR 14.0/m².

These are advertised rents, not signed-contract results.

26

Rental regulation and stressed areas

Spain’s State Rental Price Reference System provides an indicative range and can set limits for certain new contracts in declared stressed residential markets.

The applicable rule depends on the autonomous community, municipality, landlord type, prior contract history and property characteristics.

A rental strategy must be legally underwritten for the exact address and intended contract type before purchase.

27

Tourist and seasonal rental risk

Tourist rental is regulated through autonomous-community and local rules, building-community restrictions and registration requirements.

A property marketed as suitable for holiday letting should not be valued on short-stay income unless the licence, registration, community rules and continuing legal use are independently confirmed.

Seasonal contracts are not a universal substitute for long-term leases and can face changing legal interpretation.

28

Gross rental yields

Global Property Guide estimated average Spanish gross residential yields near 5.45% in March 2026.

Its surveyed markets ranged from approximately 7.40% in Barcelona to 4.41% in Palma, but sample composition and regulation can strongly affect these figures.

Net yield is lower after vacancy, tax, community charges, insurance, management, maintenance, furnishing, utilities, legal costs and capital expenditure.

29

Purchase taxes

Spain’s official administration portal states that first deliveries of new property by a developer are subject to VAT. Ordinary residential VAT is generally 10%, with separate AJD rules and regional implementation.

Used-property purchases are normally subject to transfer tax, whose rate and reliefs depend on the autonomous community and buyer circumstances.

The correct all-in acquisition budget must be calculated for the exact region and property classification.

30

Foreign ownership and NIE

Spain generally allows foreigners to buy residential property, but ownership does not automatically grant immigration residence.

A foreign buyer normally requires a Spanish foreigner identification number, NIE, for tax and transaction purposes.

Residence, tax residence and property ownership are legally separate questions and should not be combined in marketing claims.

31

Transaction and registration process

A safe purchase typically involves an independent lawyer, reservation or deposit agreement with conditions, registry and cadastral review, financing approval, public deed before a notary, tax payment and registration.

Spain’s official portal states that registration is voluntary but strongly advisable because it protects the purchaser as the registered legal owner against hidden claims and disputes.

Mortgage borrowers also receive a mandatory prior notarial act explaining the loan conditions before completion.

32

Essential due-diligence checklist

Verify title, charges, cadastral reference, permitted use, planning status, first-occupancy licence, community statutes, community debts, IBI, utility debts, energy certificate and any protected-housing restrictions.

For rural or coastal property, verify land classification, environmental or coastal restrictions, legal access, water, boundaries, outbuildings and pools.

For tenanted property, review the lease, deposits, payment history, rent regulation, termination rights and tenant pre-emption rights where applicable.

33

Investment strategies that fit Spain

Major-city long-term rental: efficient homes near employment, transport, universities and healthcare.

Coastal international-demand strategy: legally clean property in a year-round market with conservative seasonality assumptions.

Value-add resale: older urban units where renovation improves layout and energy performance.

Regional yield strategy: lower-entry cities with durable employment and limited competing stock.

Luxury scarcity strategy: exceptional assets in Madrid, Barcelona, Marbella, Mallorca or Ibiza where provenance, privacy and location are independently verified.

34

Principal investor risks

Affordability risk: prices are rising faster than many household incomes.

Regulatory risk: rent controls, tourist-rental rules and community restrictions vary by location.

Supply risk: national shortage can coexist with local development concentration.

Legal risk: registry, planning, occupancy and cadastral discrepancies can block financing or resale.

Climate risk: heat, water scarcity, wildfire, flooding and coastal exposure affect insurance, maintenance and long-term demand.

Yield risk: purchase prices can rise faster than rents and compress returns.

2026 SCENARIOS

Base, upside and downside paths

Base case: national price growth remains positive but moderates from the Q1 pace as affordability limits sales volumes. Prime urban, coastal and island markets continue outperforming weaker inland areas.

Upside case: strong immigration, income growth, low financing costs and slow completions maintain double-digit pressure in the most undersupplied provinces.

Downside case: weaker European growth, higher rates, regulatory intervention or a tourism slowdown reduce transaction liquidity. Highly leveraged and licence-dependent investments are most exposed.

35

Who Spain fits best

Spain fits buyers seeking a large euro-denominated market with deep domestic demand, international lifestyle appeal and diverse urban, coastal, island and regional strategies.

It fits less well for investors requiring uniform national rules, guaranteed tourist licensing, high net yields in prime locations or a rapid exit without local legal and tax support.

36

Real Estate Taxi decision framework

Start with legal certainty and completed comparable evidence. Then calculate realistic rent, vacancy, financing, taxes, community charges, management, maintenance and exit costs.

Confirm the exact rental use permitted at the address before assigning any income value.

Reject any property whose ownership, planning, occupancy, community or licensing position cannot be independently verified.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

Spain residential real estate FAQ

Are Spanish house prices rising in 2026?
Yes. INE reported 12.9% annual and 3.5% quarterly growth in Q1 2026.

Which segment is rising faster?
Second-hand housing rose 13.5% annually, compared with 9.1% for new housing.

Can foreigners buy property in Spain?
Yes, but buyers normally require an NIE and ownership does not automatically provide immigration residence.

What mortgage rate is relevant?
Global Property Guide reported an average 2.75% rate on new housing loans in February 2026.

What tax applies to a purchase?
New developer property is generally subject to VAT and AJD, while used property is generally subject to regional transfer tax.

What gross yield is realistic?
Global Property Guide estimated an average 5.45% gross yield in March 2026, before all operating and tax costs.

What is the biggest tourism-rental risk?
Buying without confirming the exact licence, registration, community rules and continuing legal right to operate.
