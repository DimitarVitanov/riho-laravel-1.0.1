EXECUTIVE DECISION FRAME

Russia in one investment sentence

Russia enters the second half of 2026 with nominal housing prices still rising, but market liquidity remains heavily shaped by expensive credit, state-supported mortgage programs and a widening divide between premium metropolitan assets and ordinary regional stock.

The strongest risk-adjusted opportunities are generally assets with verifiable title, realistic local rents, low operating complexity and a clear domestic buyer pool. The weakest propositions are projects priced only on future appreciation, properties dependent on uncertain cross-border payment routes, and units where the buyer cannot independently verify cadastral, occupancy, encumbrance and developer documentation.

01

2026 market snapshot

Global Property Guide’s May 2026 house-price series, sourced from the Federal State Statistics Service, reports a national residential value of RUB 219,522 per square metre in Q1 2026. The index was 8.7% higher year-on-year and 7.6% higher in real terms.

This is a slower annual pace than the trailing four-quarter average because the market is normalizing after years of subsidized credit, construction-cost inflation and front-loaded demand. The central question for 2026 is therefore not whether nominal prices can rise, but whether transaction volumes and household affordability can recover enough to support those prices without further policy support.

02

How to read the numbers

Official transaction-price evidence: Rosstat house-price indices and regional average prices.

Official credit evidence: Bank of Russia mortgage and key-rate statistics.

Market asking evidence: Cian, Restate and Global Property Guide portal datasets.

Real Estate Taxi analysis: interpretation of liquidity, yield durability, risk and investor fit. Asking prices are not completed sale prices, and gross yields are not net returns.

PRICE CYCLE

From subsidized boom to slower growth

Russia’s housing market experienced several distinct phases: a rapid early-2000s expansion, the 2008–2009 correction, ruble devaluation and inflation shocks after 2014, and a major new-build boom after subsidized mortgages were introduced in 2020.

Between 2020 and 2022, primary-market apartment prices grew exceptionally quickly as low-cost credit pulled future demand forward. The broad subsidized program ended in July 2024, after which ordinary market-rate mortgages became much less affordable. The result is a lower-volume market in which developers protect headline prices through discounts, installment structures, smaller units and selective launches rather than broad nominal price reductions.

03

Primary-market pricing

In Q3 2025, Rosstat’s average new-housing price reached approximately RUB 208,268/m², up 18.96% year-on-year. New-build pricing remained supported by construction costs, expensive project finance, reduced launches and continued access to targeted subsidized mortgages.

Investors should compare the advertised price with the effective price after developer incentives, installment plans, fit-out status, completion timing and escrow conditions. A nominally stable price can hide weaker economics when completion is delayed or the unit requires substantial finishing expenditure.

04

Secondary-market pricing

The average secondary-market price was approximately RUB 129,074/m² in Q3 2025, up 16.01% year-on-year. The large national price gap between new and existing stock reflects both quality differences and the distortion created by subsidized financing concentrated in new housing.

Secondary housing may offer better immediate usability and rental visibility, but building condition, renovation liabilities, undocumented alterations, registered residents and historical ownership chains require deeper due diligence.

05

Moscow market intelligence

Moscow is Russia’s deepest and most internationally visible residential market, but it contains several distinct segments: mass-market peripheral districts, established inner-city apartments, business-class projects and trophy assets.

Rosstat data cited by Global Property Guide placed Q3 2025 primary-market pricing near RUB 414,371/m² and secondary-market pricing near RUB 369,497/m². Global Property Guide’s July 2026 portal sample shows a median asking price of approximately USD 448,000 for a two-bedroom dwelling.

Liquidity is strongest where transport, building quality, legal cleanliness and realistic pricing align. Luxury listing prices can remain visible for long periods without producing representative transactions.

06

St. Petersburg market intelligence

St. Petersburg combines a major employment base, tourism demand, historic housing, suburban construction and a substantial new-build pipeline. Q3 2025 average prices cited by Global Property Guide were about RUB 282,215/m² in the primary market and RUB 274,427/m² in the secondary market.

Historic-center apartments can command premiums, but technical condition, protected-building restrictions, communal-apartment history and renovation permissions may materially affect operating costs and resale liquidity.

07

Regional market segmentation

Russia’s national average conceals extreme regional variation. Stronger construction and demand clusters include Moscow and Moscow Region, St. Petersburg and Leningrad Region, Krasnodar Krai, Sverdlovsk Region, Tyumen Region, Novosibirsk Region, Rostov Region and Bashkortostan.

Regional investment should be based on local employment, migration, new supply, household incomes and rental depth. A high nominal yield in a shrinking city may compensate poorly for vacancy, maintenance and resale risk.

08

New-build demand reset

Rosreestr data compiled by DOM.RF showed approximately 148,000 shared-equity participation contracts in Q3 2025, up 29% year-on-year, while the first nine months totaled around 380,000 contracts, still 12% below the prior-year period.

The quarterly rebound therefore represented stabilization from a weak base rather than a return to the previous subsidized-credit boom. Demand remains highly sensitive to eligibility rules and the spread between subsidized and market mortgage rates.

09

Where new-build sales recovered

Primary sales in Q3 2025 reached about 6.32 million m², 17% above the same quarter a year earlier. The first nine months totaled roughly 16.54 million m², 14% lower year-on-year.

Among large construction regions, quarterly gains were reported in Rostov Region and Tyumen Region, while the year-to-date picture remained weaker in many major markets. Investors should distinguish a quarter-on-quarter recovery from a full-cycle demand recovery.

SUPPLY AND DEVELOPMENT

Construction remains large, but new launches are becoming more selective

Russia retains a substantial residential construction pipeline. However, expensive developer financing and weaker market-rate mortgage demand encourage developers to phase launches, reduce unit sizes, offer installment plans and concentrate on projects that qualify for supported buyer demand.

Supply risk is local. A city can show healthy national price growth while an individual district faces several years of competing completions. Underwriting should include all projects under construction within the relevant catchment area, not only currently completed listings.

10

Buying housing under construction

Shared-equity participation agreements are a standard route for purchasing housing before completion. The practical investor review should include the developer, project declaration, construction permit, escrow-bank structure, expected handover, permitted use, exact apartment specification and delay remedies.

The most important distinction is between marketing promises and legally enforceable obligations. Furniture packages, rental guarantees, views, access roads and completion dates should be confirmed in signed documents.

11

Mortgage-market conditions

The Bank of Russia’s mortgage-market portal publishes monthly national and regional statistics. At the latest available policy date before this report, the Bank’s key rate was 14.25% on 16 July 2026, after a 25-basis-point cut in June.

Although policy easing has begun, ordinary mortgage affordability remains restrictive. A meaningful secondary-market recovery normally requires market mortgage rates to fall much further than the policy rate alone suggests.

12

Subsidized mortgage dependence

Targeted state-backed programs remain central to new-build demand. Global Property Guide’s synthesis of official and industry data indicated that subsidized loans represented more than four-fifths of new mortgage value during the first ten months of 2025.

This creates a policy concentration risk: changes in eligibility, regional limits, family status or loan caps can rapidly change demand for specific unit types and developments.

13

Interest-rate sensitivity

High deposit yields compete directly with investment property. A landlord must compare net rental yield, vacancy and resale risk against relatively high returns available on ruble savings instruments.

As rates fall, some deposits may migrate into property and secondary-market transactions may recover. Yet easing can also weaken the ruble or revive inflation, so a lower nominal rate does not automatically improve foreign-currency returns.

14

Long-term rental market

Rental growth moderated during 2025 after the sharp increases of 2023–2024. Global Property Guide, citing DOM.RF and Cian, reported Q3 2025 average asking rents near RUB 114,800 per month in Moscow and RUB 57,300 in St. Petersburg.

Across other million-plus cities, average asking rent was around RUB 39,000, while cities with populations between 500,000 and one million averaged around RUB 35,600. These are asking levels and must be adjusted for negotiated rent, vacancy, utilities, agent costs and furnishing.

15

Rental yields and net-income reality

DOM.RF estimates cited by Global Property Guide placed indicative gross apartment yields around 6.4% in Moscow and 6.0% in St. Petersburg in Q3 2025.

Net yield can be materially lower after vacancy, repairs, common-area charges, management, tenant turnover, taxation and currency movement. A high gross yield should not compensate for unclear title or weak resale demand.

16

Short-term rental suitability

Short-term rentals may work in selected central, tourism and business locations, but revenue is more operationally intensive than long-term leasing. Building rules, neighbor complaints, local enforcement, registration requirements, seasonality and payment-platform access should be reviewed before acquisition.

A conservative model should be able to function as a normal long-term rental if short-term demand or operating channels weaken.

FOREIGN BUYER REALITY

Ownership may be possible, but execution is the central issue

Foreign buyers can in principle acquire many apartments and buildings, but land ownership is restricted in designated border and other specially regulated territories. Article 15 of the Land Code prohibits foreign ownership of land in listed border territories.

Cross-border payment, banking, sanctions, currency-control and counterparty restrictions can be more decisive than the property law itself. The Federal Tax Service states that real-estate settlements involving non-residents should be conducted through authorized banks rather than cash. Every foreign buyer should obtain written confirmation from a qualified Russian lawyer and the participating banks before signing or transferring funds.

17

Title and registry due diligence

Obtain and independently review an up-to-date extract from the Unified State Register of Real Estate, the seller’s identity and authority, acquisition documents, encumbrances, mortgages, arrests, easements, shared ownership and pending disputes.

For apartments, check registered occupants, minors’ rights, matrimonial consent, privatization history, inheritance risk and whether alterations are documented. For buildings and land, confirm boundaries, permitted use, access and cadastral consistency.

18

Technical and building review

Technical due diligence should cover structural condition, facade and roof obligations, utilities, heating, lifts, fire safety, common-area liabilities, reserve funding and planned major repairs.

Older central-city buildings can offer strong location value while carrying unpredictable capital expenditure. New projects can reduce technical risk but introduce completion, snagging and developer-performance risk.

19

Indicative purchase process

  1. Identify the property and verify seller authority.
  2. Obtain registry, cadastral, title and occupancy documents.
  3. Complete legal, technical and sanctions/payment checks.
  4. Agree the sale agreement, price, currency and settlement mechanism.
  5. Use an authorized bank or compliant settlement structure.
  6. Register the transfer and confirm the updated registry record.
  7. Complete tax, utilities, insurance and management setup.

Sequence and documentation differ for new-build participation agreements, completed apartments, corporate sellers, inherited property and properties involving minors or shared owners.

20

Transaction-cost framework

Costs may include legal review, notarial services where required, state registration, bank settlement fees, brokerage, translation, technical inspection and taxes. The effective cost depends more on the transaction structure than on a single national percentage.

Foreign buyers should obtain a written closing-cost schedule before paying a reservation deposit, because bank and compliance costs can be significant.

21

Annual property taxation

Individual property tax is generally calculated from cadastral value under federal and municipal rules, with rates and reliefs varying by property type and locality. High-value property can face higher maximum rates.

The Federal Tax Service’s 2026 guidance should be checked together with the municipality’s current rate and the specific cadastral value shown for the asset.

22

Rental-income taxation

The Federal Tax Service states that Russian tax residents’ rental income is subject to the progressive personal-income-tax scale, while individuals present in Russia for fewer than 183 days are generally taxed at 30% on such Russian-source income.

Entity ownership, individual-entrepreneur status and special regimes can change the outcome. The legal owner, tax residence and management arrangement should be modeled before purchase.

23

Tax on sale and holding period

For Russian tax residents, gains from property sales are generally subject to 13%/15% rates unless an exemption applies after the minimum ownership period. The usual minimum period is five years, with three-year exceptions for specified cases such as inheritance, qualifying gifts, privatization and certain sole-residence situations.

Non-residents are generally subject to a 30% rate. Current exemptions, deductions and cadastral-value floors should be confirmed for the seller’s exact status and acquisition date.

24

Ruble and foreign-currency risk

Most local prices, rents, taxes and operating expenses are in rubles. A property can appreciate in ruble terms while losing value when measured in euros or dollars.

Foreign-currency investors should run at least three exchange-rate cases and separate the return generated by the property from the return generated by currency movement.

25

Sanctions and payment-channel risk

Sanctions and counter-sanctions can affect banks, transfers, insurance, professional services, buyer eligibility and exit routes. The compliance position can change faster than property-market fundamentals.

No purchase should proceed based only on an agent’s assurance that funds can be transferred. Both sending and receiving banks should approve the exact parties, currency, account route and purpose of payment before contract signing.

INVESTMENT STRATEGIES

Which approaches fit the 2026 environment?

Income-first apartment: established district, proven long-term rent, modest refurbishment, broad domestic tenant pool.

Discounted secondary acquisition: motivated seller, clean title, immediate occupancy and pricing below comparable completed transactions.

Selective new build: strong developer, escrow protection, realistic completion date, limited competing supply and a purchase price that survives the end of incentives.

Capital-preservation asset: prime location and scarce building quality, but only where liquidity and legal execution are demonstrably strong.

Avoid: speculative remote projects, guaranteed-yield marketing, unclear land rights, informal payment arrangements and assets whose exit depends on one foreign-buyer niche.

26

Risk matrix

High: cross-border payment execution, sanctions exposure, currency volatility, ordinary mortgage affordability.

Medium-high: developer completion, subsidy-policy dependence, title history in complex secondary assets.

Medium: rental vacancy in major cities, operating costs, regional supply concentration.

Lower where verified: completed apartments in liquid districts with clean registry records and broad domestic demand.

27

Liquidity and exit planning

Exit liquidity should be tested before entry. Identify the likely domestic buyer, realistic mortgage eligibility, competing supply and average marketing period.

A property priced for a narrow luxury or foreign audience may have a high headline valuation but a weak executable exit. Underwrite the price at which a normal local buyer can transact.

28

Base-case outlook: 2026–2027

The base case is continued nominal price growth at a slower pace, with greater differentiation between new and existing housing. Gradual monetary easing may improve secondary-market activity, while developers continue to defend new-build prices through controlled supply and incentives.

Real returns will depend on inflation, the ruble, net rent and transaction execution. National nominal growth alone is not sufficient evidence of an attractive investment.

29

Upside scenario

Faster disinflation, deeper key-rate cuts, improving mortgage affordability and stable policy support could release deposit savings into housing and strengthen secondary-market transactions.

The main beneficiaries would likely be liquid urban apartments and completed projects with immediate occupancy rather than highly speculative developments.

30

Downside scenario

Persistent inflation, renewed rate pressure, weaker household incomes, tighter subsidy rules or more restrictive payment channels could reduce transaction volumes and increase effective developer discounts.

In a downside case, nominal ruble prices may remain sticky while time-to-sale lengthens and foreign-currency performance deteriorates.

INVESTOR CHECKLIST

Minimum checks before committing capital

  • Current registry extract and complete ownership chain.
  • Seller identity, authority, marital status and beneficial ownership.
  • Encumbrances, arrests, mortgages, registered occupants and minors’ rights.
  • Legal status of alterations, land, access and permitted use.
  • Building condition, major-repair obligations and utility arrears.
  • Bank-approved payment route and sanctions screening.
  • Independent comparable sales and rent evidence.
  • Base, upside and downside ruble/euro or ruble/dollar models.
  • Tax memo for ownership, rent and exit.
  • Written exit plan identifying the likely local buyer.
FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

Russia residential real estate FAQ

Are Russian house prices still rising in 2026?

Yes. The Rosstat-based national series reported 8.7% year-on-year nominal growth in Q1 2026, but the pace is slowing and varies materially by segment and region.

What is the average national residential price?

The Q1 2026 national series reported RUB 219,522 per square metre. This is a statistical average, not a substitute for city, district, building and unit comparables.

Is Moscow still the most expensive market?

Moscow remains the country’s deepest high-price market. Q3 2025 averages were about RUB 414,371/m² for new housing and RUB 369,497/m² for existing housing.

Can foreigners buy apartments in Russia?

Foreign buyers can in principle acquire many apartments, but land, banking, sanctions and payment restrictions may apply. Border-territory land ownership is restricted, and every transaction requires current legal and bank confirmation.

What are typical gross rental yields?

Indicative Q3 2025 estimates were around 6.4% in Moscow and 6.0% in St. Petersburg. Net yields are lower after vacancy, management, tax, repairs and utilities.

Why is new housing much more expensive than secondary housing?

New-build demand has been supported by subsidized mortgages, while construction and finance costs remain high. Secondary buyers rely more heavily on expensive market-rate mortgages.

What is the main 2026 investment risk?

Execution risk: title, payment channels, sanctions, currency movement and the ability to resell. These can matter more than the national price index.

Is a high-rent listing enough to prove yield?

No. Asking rent must be adjusted for negotiation, vacancy, furnishing, common charges, management, maintenance and tax.

Should investors wait for lower interest rates?

Lower rates may improve liquidity, but timing is uncertain. A purchase should work under conservative current financing and rental assumptions, not only under a future easing scenario.

What document is central to title verification?

An up-to-date extract from the Unified State Register of Real Estate is central, but it must be reviewed together with acquisition documents, occupancy, marital, inheritance and litigation checks.
