Sweden Residential Real Estate Market Analysis 2026

EXECUTIVE DECISION FRAME

Sweden in one investment sentence

Sweden entered mid-2026 with modest national house-price growth, lower mortgage rates and a gradual revival in housing construction, while household indebtedness, regulated rents and the special legal structure of tenant-owned apartments remain central investment constraints.

The strongest risk-adjusted opportunities are usually well-managed tenant-owned apartments or legally clean houses in deep employment markets, bought after detailed association or property review and underwritten with realistic rent and exit assumptions. The weakest propositions are highly leveraged purchases, apartments in financially weak housing cooperatives, and rental strategies that assume unrestricted subletting or market-level rent without prior approval.

01

2026 market snapshot

Statistics Sweden reported that the national Real Estate Price Index for one- or two-dwelling buildings was 1% higher year-on-year in Q1 2026. The average national purchase price was approximately SEK 4.2 million.

Average Q1 prices ranged from about SEK 5.3 million in Greater Malmö to SEK 7.2 million in Greater Stockholm.

This detached-house indicator does not measure tenant-owned apartments, which form a separate and very important Swedish market.

02

How to interpret Sweden’s evidence

Official house evidence: Statistics Sweden Real Estate Price Index and registration-of-title data for one- or two-dwelling buildings.

Official apartment evidence: Statistics Sweden sales and turnover statistics for tenant-owned flats.

Official finance evidence: Statistics Sweden mortgage rates and lending growth, produced under Riksbank responsibility.

Secondary market evidence: Global Property Guide asking prices, rents and gross-yield estimates. Asking evidence is not completed sale evidence, and gross yield is not net return.

MARKET STRUCTURE

Sweden is not one single ownership market

Almost all directly owned residential property is found in one- or two-dwelling houses. Most apartments are either rental units or tenant-owned rights known as bostadsrätter.

A tenant-owner does not directly own the apartment as an individual piece of immovable property. The buyer acquires membership in a tenant-owner association, a right to use the apartment and a proportional economic exposure to the association.

That distinction affects financing, taxation, stamp duty, subletting, due diligence and the buyer’s exposure to association debt.

03

Detached-house price cycle

Global Property Guide’s SCB-based series reported only 0.86% nominal annual growth in Q4 2025, after a deeper correction during the 2022–2023 interest-rate shock.

The Q1 2026 official SCB house index showed more than 1% annual growth and a quarterly increase, indicating stabilization rather than a rapid national boom.

The recovery remains sensitive to mortgage pricing because Swedish households commonly use short fixation periods.

04

Tenant-owned flat market

SCB reported 102,062 tenant-owned flats sold in 2025 for a total value of approximately SEK 295 billion.

The national average price increased about 2%, from SEK 2.829 million in 2024 to SEK 2.888 million in 2025, while the number of sales was slightly lower.

Average-price changes must be treated cautiously because the mix of location, size and quality can change from year to year.

05

Metropolitan transaction concentration

Greater Stockholm, Greater Gothenburg and Greater Malmö accounted for more than 78% of the total 2025 sales value of tenant-owned flats.

Greater Stockholm alone represented more than 58% of national turnover value and 39% of all reported tenant-owned-flat sales.

This concentration gives the three metropolitan regions deeper comparable evidence and generally stronger resale liquidity than smaller markets.

06

Stockholm market intelligence

Stockholm has Sweden’s deepest apartment market and the broadest professional, technology, financial, government and international demand.

Inner-city districts command premium pricing, while suburban municipalities connected by metro or commuter rail can offer lower entry prices and broader family demand.

The investment must include a review of the housing cooperative’s debt per square metre, interest fixation, maintenance plan, land ownership, monthly fee history and planned capital works.

07

Gothenburg market intelligence

Gothenburg combines manufacturing, logistics, technology, universities, healthcare and a large regional labour market.

Central and waterfront districts command strong prices, but many investors find better income-to-price relationships in established tram-connected neighborhoods.

Association finances and building-envelope risks are especially important in older coastal and exposed properties.

08

Malmö and Lund corridor

Greater Malmö combines lower entry prices than Stockholm, cross-border access to Copenhagen, universities, logistics and a diverse tenant base.

Lund has concentrated university, research and life-science demand, while Malmö offers a larger and more varied urban rental market.

The area can provide stronger headline yields, but neighborhood selection, tenant demand and association finances remain decisive.

09

Uppsala market intelligence

Uppsala is supported by universities, healthcare, life sciences, government employment and commuter links to Stockholm.

Smaller apartments near universities and rail connections can have durable demand, but student turnover, association approval and furnishing costs must be considered.

Global Property Guide’s secondary sample suggests some of Sweden’s stronger gross yields in Uppsala, but net results depend heavily on permitted rent and subletting.

10

Regional cities and industrial corridors

Linköping, Västerås, Örebro, Jönköping, Umeå and other regional centers can offer lower entry prices and demand linked to universities, industry, defence, logistics and public services.

Smaller markets have fewer comparable sales and can be more exposed to one major employer or project.

A regional purchase should be underwritten with a longer exit period and a larger vacancy margin than a comparable Stockholm asset.

11

Northern Sweden and project-driven demand

Parts of northern Sweden have experienced housing pressure linked to mining, energy, battery, infrastructure and defence investment.

Project-driven demand can produce strong rents and rapid repricing, but it can also reverse if construction, financing or employment plans change.

The investor should separate permanent household formation from temporary contractor accommodation.

12

Holiday homes and secondary-use property

SCB reported that prices for seasonal and secondary-use property fell almost 4% quarter-on-quarter in Q1 2026.

Holiday-home markets are highly local and depend on accessibility, water, wastewater systems, winter usability, shoreline rules, maintenance and local short-stay demand.

A countryside discount may reflect weak liquidity, costly infrastructure or limited year-round access rather than an investment opportunity.

13

Mortgage rates in May 2026

SCB reported an average rate of 2.83% on households’ new housing-loan agreements in May 2026.

The average floating mortgage rate was 2.78%, while loans fixed for more than five years averaged 2.96%.

Housing-loan growth reached 3.2% year-on-year, indicating a gradual recovery in credit demand.

14

Riksbank policy rate

The Riksbank’s policy rate was 1.75% from 24 June 2026.

The central bank left the rate unchanged in June but stated that the probability of a later increase had risen because of external inflation risks.

Sweden’s short mortgage-fixation culture means policy-rate expectations can affect household cash flow and buyer capacity relatively quickly.

15

New mortgage law from April 2026

From 1 April 2026, Sweden’s new law on limits for housing credit replaced Finansinspektionen’s earlier regulations.

The additional 1% amortisation requirement for borrowers whose mortgages exceeded 4.5 times annual income was removed.

Borrowers above 70% loan-to-value must still amortise at least 2% annually, while borrowers above 50% and up to 70% must amortise at least 1%.

16

Higher mortgage cap and buyer leverage

The new 2026 framework increased the upper mortgage-cap interval to 90% loan-to-value, reducing the potential minimum cash contribution from 15% to 10% for qualifying borrowers.

This can expand buyer capacity and support prices, but bank credit assessment remains independent and can be stricter than the legal ceiling.

Higher leverage also increases sensitivity to interest rates, monthly association fees and valuation declines.

17

Household-debt sensitivity

Swedish households remain highly indebted by international standards, and a large share of mortgage debt is on floating or short fixation.

SCB reported that 76% of outstanding housing loans had a floating rate in April 2026.

An investment model should therefore test higher interest costs even when the current mortgage rate appears affordable.

18

Q1 2026 housing starts

Preliminary SCB figures show that construction started on approximately 6,800 dwellings in Q1 2026, 4% more than one year earlier.

About 5,200 were ordinary dwellings in multi-dwelling buildings, 550 were special housing and 1,050 were in one- or two-dwelling buildings.

The recovery is positive, but the level remains below the strong construction years before the rate shock.

19

New-supply tenure mix

Among started dwellings in multi-dwelling buildings in Q1 2026, SCB reported a national split of approximately 50% rental dwellings and 48% tenant-owned dwellings.

In Greater Stockholm, tenant-owned dwellings represented about 57% of the started multi-dwelling pipeline; in Greater Gothenburg the share was around 63%.

The tenure mix matters because rental supply and owner-occupier supply compete differently for land, financing and household demand.

20

Existing housing stock

Sweden had 5,291,493 dwellings at the end of 2025.

One- or two-dwelling buildings accounted for 41%, multi-dwelling buildings 52%, special housing 6% and other buildings 1%.

In multi-dwelling buildings, 58% of units were rented and 42% were tenant-owned.

21

Official rental-market evidence

SCB reported that average rents in rental dwellings increased 4.6% in 2025.

The national average rent for a three-room apartment was SEK 9,118 per month, including heating and water but excluding electricity and paid parking.

Greater Stockholm averaged SEK 10,139 for a three-room apartment, Greater Gothenburg SEK 8,907 and Greater Malmö SEK 10,432.

22

Rent setting is not a free market

Swedish rents are influenced by collective negotiation, utility-value principles and tenancy law rather than unrestricted market pricing.

Official rental-stock averages are therefore lower than many advertised second-hand rents.

An investor should not use an online asking rent without confirming the permitted rent under the applicable legal structure.

23

Subletting a tenant-owned apartment

A bostadsrätt owner may sublet only with the housing cooperative’s approval or permission from the Rent Tribunal.

Unauthorized second-hand letting can place the tenant-owner right at risk.

The investment case should therefore never assume unrestricted, permanent subletting before the association’s rules and likely approval are reviewed.

24

Private-home rental rules

Sweden’s private-home rental law can apply when an individual rents out a house, owner-occupied apartment or tenant-owned apartment outside a rental business.

The tenant and landlord can ask the Rent Tribunal to review the terms, and the tenant generally lacks a right to extension when this specific law applies.

The exact permitted rent and termination rules depend on the property, contract and number of units rented.

25

Gross rental yields

Global Property Guide estimated an average Swedish gross residential yield of 5.75% in Q2 2026.

Its sampled city averages were approximately 4.67% in Stockholm, with higher figures in some Gothenburg, Malmö and Uppsala segments.

These are secondary estimates based on asking prices and rents. Net yield can be materially lower after association fees, vacancy, tax, maintenance, furnishings and management.

26

Housing-cooperative financial risk

A bostadsrätt buyer must underwrite both the apartment and the association.

Critical indicators include association debt per square metre, loan maturity, fixed-rate profile, operating result, cash reserves, maintenance plan, land ownership, ground rent, energy costs and planned projects.

A low apartment price can be offset by a high monthly fee or future fee increases caused by association refinancing.

27

Buying a house or other real property

A buyer of immovable property must apply for registration of ownership with Lantmäteriet within three months of acquisition.

From 1 July 2026, a foreign natural-person applicant needs a Swedish personal identity number or coordination number; legal entities face Swedish registration and beneficial-ownership documentation requirements.

The purchase document, property designation, seller authority and any conditions must be legally correct before title registration.

28

Stamp duty and mortgage certificates

For immovable property, Lantmäteriet states that private individuals pay 1.5% stamp duty on the higher of the purchase price or prior-year assessment value, plus an SEK 825 processing fee.

Legal entities generally pay 4.25%. A new mortgage certificate attracts 2% stamp duty on the mortgage amount plus an SEK 375 fee.

These land-registration charges do not apply to an ordinary tenant-owned apartment in the same way because a bostadsrätt is not directly owned immovable property.

29

Recurring property charge

For income year 2026, the municipal property charge for a completed residential house is 0.75% of assessment value, capped at SEK 10,425 per residential building.

Newly built residential houses and owner-occupied apartments can qualify for a 15-year exemption under the applicable rules.

For a tenant-owned apartment, the association normally bears property-level charges and passes its costs indirectly through monthly fees.

30

Tax on private rental income

The Swedish Tax Agency states that surplus rental income from a private residence is taxed at 30%.

A general deduction of SEK 40,000 per property is available, with further deductions depending on whether the property is a house, owner-occupied apartment, rental apartment or tenant-owned apartment.

Cross-border owners must coordinate Swedish tax obligations with their country of tax residence.

31

Capital-gains taxation

For a private cooperative flat, 22/30 of the gain is taxed at 30%, producing an effective tax rate of 22% before any qualifying deferral.

Allowable deductions can include acquisition cost, broker fees, qualifying improvements and capital contributions to the association.

House sales follow a related but separate calculation and should be reviewed for the exact ownership and residence situation.

32

Foreign-buyer practical issues

Sweden generally does not impose a nationality-based ban on ordinary residential purchases, but identification, financing, source-of-funds, tax and registration requirements can be significant.

Foreign buyers may find mortgage access more restrictive without Swedish income, credit history, personal identity number or long-term residence.

A coordination number supports registration processes but does not guarantee bank financing or membership approval in a housing cooperative.

33

Essential due diligence

For a house, verify title, mortgages, easements, joint facilities, boundaries, planning, permits, water and wastewater, radon, moisture, energy use, inspection results and tax assessment.

For a tenant-owned apartment, verify membership eligibility, annual report, association debt, monthly fee, renovation plan, ground lease, subletting policy, pledged status and apartment-area records.

For all property types, verify the seller, purchase document, financing conditions, insurance and exact legal right being acquired.

34

Investment strategies that fit Sweden

Metropolitan owner-occupier resale: efficient apartments in strong associations near rail and employment.

Approved second-hand rental: only where association permission, permitted rent and tax treatment are verified.

Regional yield strategy: lower-cost markets with durable universities, public employment or diversified industry.

House and holiday-home strategy: legally clean properties with year-round access, infrastructure and conservative maintenance costs.

Association-arbitrage strategy: selecting apartments where the association’s finances are stronger than competing buildings.

35

Principal investor risks

Interest-rate risk: short mortgage fixation transmits policy changes quickly.

Association risk: hidden cooperative debt can increase monthly fees and reduce resale value.

Subletting risk: an investment model may fail if the association or tribunal refuses permission.

Rent risk: legal rent can be lower than advertised second-hand rent.

Liquidity risk: smaller towns and specialized holiday homes can take longer to sell.

Currency risk: a foreign investor may earn and report returns in another currency while the asset is priced in SEK.

2026 SCENARIOS

Base, upside and downside paths

Base case: prices rise modestly as lower mortgage rates and the April credit reform support demand, while construction recovers gradually.

Upside case: stronger real incomes, easier credit and limited supply accelerate metropolitan apartment and house prices.

Downside case: higher inflation or Riksbank tightening increases monthly mortgage and association costs. Highly leveraged households and debt-heavy housing cooperatives are most exposed.

36

Who Sweden fits best

Sweden fits buyers seeking a transparent Nordic market, strong institutions, deep metropolitan liquidity and a wide choice between houses and tenant-owned apartments.

It fits less well for investors who require unrestricted rental rights, low leverage risk, high prime-city net yields or simple apartment ownership without association exposure.

37

Real Estate Taxi decision framework

First identify what is actually being purchased: immovable property, owner-occupied apartment or tenant-owner right.

Then test title or association quality, financing, monthly fees, permitted rent, vacancy, tax, maintenance and exit liquidity.

Reject any purchase whose legal structure, association finances, registration status or intended rental use cannot be independently verified.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

Sweden residential real estate FAQ

Are Swedish house prices rising in 2026?
SCB reported that one- or two-dwelling prices were more than 1% higher year-on-year in Q1 2026.

What is a bostadsrätt?
It is a tenant-owner right: membership in an association plus a right to use an apartment, not direct ownership of a separately registered apartment property.

Can a bostadsrätt be rented out freely?
No. The housing cooperative must approve the sublet, or permission must be obtained from the Rent Tribunal.

What mortgage rate is relevant?
SCB reported an average 2.83% rate on new housing-loan agreements in May 2026.

What gross yield is realistic?
Global Property Guide estimated an average 5.75% gross yield in Q2 2026, before fees, tax, vacancy and maintenance.

What stamp duty applies?
Private individuals generally pay 1.5% when buying immovable property. An ordinary bostadsrätt is transferred under a different legal structure.

What is the biggest apartment due-diligence risk?
A financially weak housing cooperative with high debt, rising interest costs or major unfunded maintenance.
