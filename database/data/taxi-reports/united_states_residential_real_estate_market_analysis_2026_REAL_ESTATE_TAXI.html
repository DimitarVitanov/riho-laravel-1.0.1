02 · DIRECT ANALYSIS ANSWER

A more balanced market, but not a cheap one

The 2026 U.S. housing market is no longer defined by one national direction. Transaction activity has improved from the tightest post-rate-shock conditions, inventory is rebuilding, and official price growth has slowed to low single digits. Yet affordability remains strained because mortgage rates are still near 6.5%, insurance and property taxes have risen in many markets, and the national median existing-home price reached a new June high.

For investors, the strongest opportunities are increasingly local and operational rather than purely cyclical: metros with durable employment, realistic price-to-rent ratios, manageable taxes and insurance, landlord-compatible rules, and enough liquidity to exit. Real Estate Taxi does not treat national median figures as a substitute for property-level underwriting.

DATA CLASSIFICATION

What each number actually represents

Official indexes, closed-sale statistics, listing data and analytical scenarios measure different things and must not be mixed.

01

1. Data hierarchy and interpretation

Official repeat-sales index: FHFA tracks price change for properties financed with conforming mortgages; it is an index, not a median dollar price.

Closed-sale data: NAR reports existing-home transactions and median sale prices; Census and HUD report new single-family sales estimates.

Asking-price and portal data: useful for current listing conditions, but asking prices are not completed transaction prices and portal methodologies differ.

Real Estate Taxi analysis: all forecasts, risk scoring, yield ranges and investor conclusions on this page are analytical interpretations, not government statistics or guarantees.

02

2. Official national price direction

FHFA reported that U.S. house prices increased 1.7% from 2025 Q1 to 2026 Q1 and 0.5% from 2025 Q4 to 2026 Q1. The seasonally adjusted monthly index rose 0.1% in March from February.

This is clear evidence of national nominal appreciation, but at a much slower rate than the pandemic-era surge. Inflation-adjusted performance may be flat or negative depending on the exact consumer-price measure and comparison month.

03

3. Existing-home sales and liquidity

NAR reported 4.09 million existing-home sales at a seasonally adjusted annual rate in June 2026, down 2.4% from May but up 2.8% from June 2025. The median price was $440,600, up 1.8% year over year, with 4.6 months of inventory.

Liquidity is better than in the most constrained phase of 2023–2025, but turnover remains sensitive to mortgage rates and the willingness of existing owners to give up low-rate loans.

04

4. New-home market

The U.S. Census Bureau estimated the May 2026 median new-home sale price at $424,900, virtually unchanged from May 2025. The average was $540,600. New-home data are estimates and carry sampling uncertainty.

Builders can use rate buydowns, closing-cost incentives and smaller floor plans, so a headline new-home price comparison does not capture the full economic package.

05

5. Supply, starts and construction pipeline

Census estimated May 2026 single-family housing starts at an annual rate of 882,000, 1.9% below revised April. Construction is highly regional and constrained by land, labor, infrastructure, zoning and financing.

Investors should distinguish permits, starts, completions and available finished inventory. A metro can show high construction while still having a shortage in the specific price band or submarket being targeted.

07

7. Houses versus apartments and condos

Single-family homes usually offer land exposure, broader owner-occupier resale demand and direct control, but maintenance and insurance sit with the owner. Condominiums can lower entry cost and simplify exterior maintenance, yet HOA fees, assessments, reserves, litigation, insurance deductibles and rental caps can dominate economics.

Multifamily apartments require a different underwriting framework based on net operating income, local rent regulation, cap rates, financing terms and professional management.

08

8. Existing versus new-build property

Existing homes provide observable neighborhood history and often mature locations, but may carry deferred maintenance, unpermitted work, lead paint, asbestos, sewer, roof or foundation issues. New builds reduce some condition risk but add builder quality, warranty, completion, appraisal and development-phase risks.

A lower advertised new-build mortgage rate may be temporary or funded through price and incentive structures; compare total cash cost and permanent debt service.

09

9. Rental market and vacancy logic

There is no single national rent that is useful for underwriting. Rent growth differs sharply by metro, unit type and vintage. Markets with heavy apartment completions may offer tenant concessions even while national rents remain high.

Use signed comparable leases, not only online asks. Include economic vacancy, concessions, bad debt, turnover, leasing fees and downtime. Short-term rental income must be evaluated separately from long-term rent because regulation, seasonality and operating costs differ.

11

11. Mortgage rates and lending conditions

Freddie Mac reported the average 30-year fixed mortgage at 6.49% for the week ending July 9, 2026 and the 15-year rate at 5.82%. These survey rates are not quotes for every borrower.

Investor, second-home, jumbo and foreign-national loans may require larger down payments and carry higher rates or fees. Debt-service coverage, reserves, credit history, property type and state law influence approval.

12

12. Affordability

Affordability remains the central constraint. Even modest price growth can raise required cash and monthly payments when mortgage rates stay high. Insurance, taxes and HOA charges are increasingly important components of the monthly ownership cost.

A buyer should calculate the fully loaded monthly cost at the actual rate, not compare only the purchase price with an earlier market period.

13

13. Acquisition costs and closing structure

  • Earnest-money deposit and due-diligence deadlines vary by contract and state.
  • Buyer costs may include lender fees, appraisal, inspection, survey, title search, title insurance, escrow, recording and transfer taxes.
  • Seller-paid commissions or concessions are negotiable and market-specific; do not assume a fixed national structure.
  • Property-tax proration and HOA transfer or capital-contribution charges can materially affect cash at closing.

Use a local attorney or qualified closing professional where appropriate and review the final settlement statement before signing.

14

14. Annual ownership costs

  • Property tax, often reassessed after sale depending on state and locality.
  • Homeowner, landlord, flood, windstorm, earthquake or excess-liability insurance.
  • HOA or condominium fees and special assessments.
  • Repairs, preventive maintenance, landscaping, utilities and pest control.
  • Property management, leasing, accounting, licensing and legal compliance.
  • Capital reserves for roof, HVAC, exterior, appliances, plumbing and structural work.
15

15. Rental-income taxation and depreciation

Rental income is generally reportable for U.S. federal tax purposes, with eligible expenses potentially deductible. IRS guidance states that residential rental property under the General Depreciation System is generally depreciated over 27.5 years, excluding land.

Passive-activity rules, depreciation recapture, state income tax, local tax, entity structure and treaty treatment can materially change results. This report is not tax advice.

16

16. Capital gains and FIRPTA for foreign owners

Foreign owners can be subject to U.S. federal and state tax on rental income and disposal. Under FIRPTA, a buyer or settlement agent may need to withhold part of the amount realized when a foreign person sells U.S. real property, subject to exceptions and procedures.

Withholding is not necessarily the final tax. Obtain U.S. and home-country advice before acquisition so ownership, financing, estate planning and future exit are structured coherently.

17

17. Foreign-buyer rules

The United States has no broad federal ban on foreign buyers purchasing ordinary residential property. However, federal sanctions, anti-money-laundering requirements, tax identification, source-of-funds checks, state restrictions on certain foreign persons or land types, and local disclosure rules may apply.

Buying property does not itself provide immigration status. Visa and residency programs are legally separate from ordinary property ownership.

19

19. Title, survey, zoning and permit risk

U.S. title systems are state-based. A title commitment and owner’s title insurance can reduce certain risks but do not replace a survey, physical inspection or zoning review. Easements, boundary issues, liens, probate, trusts, unrecorded interests and municipal violations can delay or impair use.

Verify permitted use, legal unit count, short-term rental status, accessory dwelling units, additions, pools, septic systems and certificates of occupancy.

20

20. HOA and condominium risk

Read the declaration, bylaws, rules, budgets, reserve studies, insurance, meeting minutes, pending litigation and assessment history. A low monthly fee is not automatically positive if reserves are inadequate.

Confirm rental caps, minimum lease terms, pet rules, parking rights, renovation approvals and any structural or insurance issue affecting financeability.

21

21. Climate, catastrophe and insurance

Wildfire, hurricane, flood, tornado, hail, earthquake, extreme heat and coastal exposure are property-specific. Insurance availability and deductibles can change quickly, especially in Florida, California, Louisiana, Texas and other exposed regions.

Obtain bindable insurance quotations during the contingency period, review FEMA flood mapping and local hazard data, and inspect roof age, drainage, defensible space, elevation and prior claims.

22

22. Short-term rental market

Short-term rentals can produce higher gross revenue but operate more like hospitality. City or county licensing, zoning, occupancy taxes, primary-residence rules, HOA bans and caps may apply. Rules can change after purchase.

Underwrite platform fees, cleaning, furnishings, utilities, guest damage, management, seasonality, local competition and regulatory downtime. Never value a property solely on an unverified host revenue screenshot.

24. Opportunity map

  • Inventory rebuilding can improve buyer negotiating power in selected metros.
  • Builder incentives may reduce effective financing cost on new homes.
  • Markets with job growth but controlled new supply may offer resilient demand.
  • Well-located older homes can support value-add strategies where permitting is practical.
  • Small multifamily and accessory dwelling opportunities may improve income where zoning allows.
25

25. Key risk factors

  • Mortgage-rate persistence and payment shock.
  • Local price correction after rapid pandemic-era appreciation.
  • Insurance non-renewal, exclusions or large deductibles.
  • Property-tax reassessment and special district charges.
  • HOA underfunding, structural repairs or litigation.
  • Rent regulation, eviction procedure and short-term rental restrictions.
  • Construction oversupply in selected apartment or subdivision markets.
  • Weak property-level liquidity despite strong national headlines.

27. Property-level due-diligence checklist

  • Independent general inspection plus roof, sewer, structural, HVAC, pool, septic or environmental specialists as relevant.
  • Title commitment, owner’s title policy, survey and review of all exceptions.
  • Permit history, code compliance, zoning, legal unit count and occupancy authorization.
  • Insurance quotations and hazard review before contingency expiry.
  • Property-tax history, reassessment rules, special assessments and utility obligations.
  • HOA financials, reserves, litigation, insurance and rental restrictions.
  • Signed lease audit, deposits, tenant estoppels and rent-regulation status.
  • Comparable closed sales and signed rent evidence; reconcile concessions.
  • Full operating budget including capital expenditure and management.
  • Entity, financing, tax, FIRPTA, estate and banking advice for foreign buyers.
28

28. Negotiation intelligence

Use evidence that changes property economics: inspection findings, insurance cost, tax reassessment, HOA liabilities, days on market, price reductions, builder incentives, appraisal risk and comparable closed sales. Generic claims that the national market is weak are less persuasive than documented property-specific issues.

Protect leverage through well-drafted contingencies and deadlines. A low price with waived due diligence can create a worse investment than a higher price with verified condition and clean title.

29

29. Real Estate Taxi investment conclusion

The United States remains one of the world’s deepest and most liquid residential property markets, but national scale can obscure extreme local variation. In 2026, the best risk-adjusted acquisitions are likely to be properties where the price is supported by closed comparables, rent is supported by signed leases, insurance is actually available, taxes are modeled after reassessment, and the exit buyer pool is broad.

The least attractive deals are those relying on rapid rate cuts, unverified short-term rental income, unusually low operating expenses or the assumption that all U.S. housing appreciates at the same rate.

FAQ

United States residential real estate questions

31

31. Are U.S. home prices rising in 2026?

Official FHFA data show national house prices up 1.7% year over year in the first quarter of 2026, while NAR reported the June 2026 median existing-home sale price at $440,600, up 1.8% year over year. Conditions vary materially by state and metro.

32

32. Can foreigners buy residential property in the United States?

Generally yes. The United States has no broad federal prohibition on foreign ownership of ordinary residential property, but state-level restrictions, tax reporting, sanctions, financing and entity rules must be checked.

33

33. What was the typical U.S. mortgage rate in mid-July 2026?

Freddie Mac reported a 6.49% average 30-year fixed mortgage rate for the week ending July 9, 2026. Borrower-specific rates depend on credit, leverage, points, occupancy and loan type.

34

34. Is U.S. residential property a high-yield investment?

Not automatically. Gross yields can look attractive in lower-cost metros, but taxes, insurance, maintenance, vacancy, management, leasing and capital expenditure can materially reduce net yield.

35

35. What is the largest risk for buyers in 2026?

The largest national risk is paying a strong nominal price while financing remains expensive. Local insurance availability, property taxes, HOA liabilities and climate exposure can be equally important.

36

36. Does buying U.S. property provide residency?

No. Buying real estate alone does not provide a U.S. visa, green card or immigration status.

37

37. How are foreign sellers taxed?

FIRPTA can require withholding when a foreign person disposes of U.S. real property. The withholding is not necessarily the final tax, and treaty, filing and state-tax issues require specialist advice.

38

38. What should be verified before closing?

Title, survey, liens, permits, zoning, inspection, insurance quotes, property-tax history, HOA documents, rental restrictions, flood and hazard exposure, leases and the closing statement should all be checked before funds become non-refundable.
