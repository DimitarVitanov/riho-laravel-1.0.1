EXECUTIVE DECISION FRAME

Ukraine in one investment sentence

Ukraine’s residential market entered 2026 with rising nominal prices, improving transaction activity and attractive advertised rental yields in relatively safer cities, but every conventional real-estate risk is secondary to security, destruction, energy, legal-access, insurance and liquidity risk.

The strongest risk-adjusted opportunities are legally registered, physically inspected homes in functioning, comparatively resilient urban markets, bought without excessive leverage and at a price that remains defensible under prolonged-war and delayed-recovery scenarios. The weakest propositions are undocumented construction, assets near active hostilities, purchases dependent on uninterrupted utilities or short-term resale, and properties whose registry, seller authority, sanctions status, damage history or land rights cannot be independently verified.

01

2026 market snapshot

Global Property Guide’s April 2026 market analysis, citing Ukraine’s State Statistics Service, reports that the national House Price Index increased 13.2% year-on-year in Q4 2025. Primary-market prices rose 13.1%, while secondary-market prices rose 13.3%.

This official national indicator contrasts with the older Kyiv-specific S&V Development series, which recorded only 2.16% nominal annual growth in December 2025 and a 5.36% inflation-adjusted decline.

The difference is not an error: the series measure different geographies and methodologies. Ukraine must be analysed city by city rather than through one blended headline.

02

How to interpret Ukraine’s evidence

Official national evidence: State Statistics Service house-price and rent-inflation indicators.

Official transaction evidence: Ministry of Justice and notarial registration data.

Official financial evidence: National Bank of Ukraine policy rates, bank statistics and Financial Stability Reports.

Secondary market evidence: LUN and Global Property Guide asking prices, asking rents and yield estimates. Advertised values are not notarized sale prices.

WARTIME MARKET STRUCTURE

This is not a normal national housing cycle

Ukraine’s housing market is geographically fragmented by security conditions, population displacement, infrastructure damage, business relocation, local employment and access to finance.

Relatively safer western and central cities have absorbed households and businesses, while markets exposed to repeated attacks can trade at substantial discounts and experience abrupt changes in demand.

Any investment conclusion must remain conditional. A strong yield or low price does not compensate for an asset that cannot be safely accessed, insured, occupied, heated, registered or resold.

03

Official national price signal

The reported 13.2% annual Q4 2025 House Price Index increase reflects nominal hryvnia price movement across the national market.

Inflation, exchange rates, regional transaction mix and the small number of transactions in some areas can materially affect interpretation.

Foreign investors should model returns in both hryvnia and their reporting currency, because nominal local appreciation can coexist with weaker real or foreign-currency performance.

04

Kyiv long-term price series

Global Property Guide’s Kyiv-specific S&V Development series recorded a December 2025 index value of 1,180, nominal annual growth of 2.16% and real annual decline of 5.36%.

The series remained approximately 67.5% below its September 2008 nominal peak, illustrating the combined effect of the global financial crisis, currency depreciation, political shocks and full-scale war.

Historical peak comparisons are informative but do not prove that present values are cheap, because the currency, economy, building stock and risk environment have fundamentally changed.

05

Five-year and long-term context

Global Property Guide’s harmonized data place Ukraine’s five-year nominal residential change at approximately 11.32% through 2026.

Its ten-year nominal measure was close to flat, while the fifteen-year measure remained deeply negative.

Ukraine is therefore a recovery and repricing market rather than a conventional steady-appreciation market.

06

Residential transaction activity

The 2025 notarial system recorded approximately 205,296 apartment and detached-house sale agreements, around 15.05% more than in 2024 but still roughly 36.9% below 2021.

Kyiv recorded about 34,836 residential sale contracts in 2025, while Kyiv region recorded approximately 17,362.

The recovery is meaningful, but the National Bank of Ukraine stated in its December 2025 Financial Stability Report that demand still showed no return to pre-war depth.

07

Regional market map

Kyiv: largest and deepest market, but exposed to security events, energy disruption and district-level differences in resilience.

Lviv: high western-region prices, international links, education, IT and strong inward-migration demand.

Uzhhorod: border access, limited supply and high rents, but a smaller and less liquid market.

Ivano-Frankivsk: strong post-2021 repricing and development activity with lower entry prices than Lviv.

Odesa and Dnipro: large urban markets with attractive yields but materially higher security and infrastructure risk.

Kharkiv, Zaporizhzhia, Kherson and frontline areas: extreme risk, limited conventional finance and potentially impaired marketability.

08

Kyiv market intelligence

LUN data cited by Global Property Guide placed the average advertised primary-market price in Kyiv near USD 1,340/m² in March 2026, up 4.69% year-on-year.

Advertised secondary-market prices were approximately USD 70,000 for a one-bedroom apartment, USD 110,000 for a two-bedroom and USD 160,000 for a three-bedroom.

The most defensible properties combine functioning utilities, shelter access, transport, employment demand, recent construction or well-maintained older buildings and clear digital registry records.

09

Lviv market intelligence

Lviv had the highest sampled primary-market asking price among the selected cities at approximately USD 1,360/m² in March 2026.

One-bedroom secondary apartments were advertised near USD 72,000, slightly above Kyiv in the same sample.

Demand is supported by migration, universities, IT, tourism, logistics and proximity to the EU, but high prices and a growing construction pipeline require careful project-level comparison.

10

Uzhhorod market intelligence

Uzhhorod’s relative safety, border proximity and limited stock have supported both rents and purchase prices.

March 2026 advertised primary prices were around USD 1,100/m², while one-bedroom secondary apartments were near USD 69,000.

The market is much smaller than Kyiv or Lviv, so resale liquidity and comparable evidence can weaken quickly when demand changes.

11

Ivano-Frankivsk market intelligence

Ivano-Frankivsk recorded the strongest sampled four-year primary-market price increase, rising approximately 113.95% from March 2021 to March 2026.

The March 2026 primary asking price was about USD 920/m², while a one-bedroom secondary apartment was around USD 42,000.

The lower entry cost and strong migration demand can support yields, but investors must review the volume of unfinished and newly delivered supply.

12

Odesa market intelligence

Odesa primary asking prices reached approximately USD 1,170/m² in March 2026, up 15.84% year-on-year in the sampled evidence.

Its port, tourism, education and large population support long-term demand, but security, infrastructure, insurance and seasonal factors remain critical.

A headline discount or yield must be weighed against access interruptions, property damage and slower buyer liquidity.

13

Dnipro market intelligence

Dnipro remains a major industrial, logistics, medical and administrative center with a large rental pool.

March 2026 primary asking prices were around USD 1,080/m², while one-bedroom secondary apartments were near USD 34,000.

Global Property Guide estimated Dnipro’s gross residential yield at approximately 9.76%, the highest of its sampled cities, but this premium directly reflects higher risk.

14

Primary-market and developer risk

New construction is exposed to financing, labour, material, energy, logistics, permit, completion and security risk.

A buyer should verify the land right, urban-planning conditions, building permit, developer and contractor identity, construction-finance structure, completion schedule, utility connection, commissioning and future registration route.

Payments for property rights in an unfinished project require substantially more legal review than a completed, registered apartment.

15

Secondary-market advantages and risks

Completed apartments can be inspected for physical condition, utilities, shelter access, energy resilience and building management.

Important legal risks include old BTI-only records, inheritance disputes, co-owner or spouse rights, registered occupants, mortgages, arrest or enforcement notes, illegal alterations and damaged documentation.

The State Register of Rights and mortgage registry information should be checked immediately before notarization.

16

Rental-market structure

State Statistics Service rent inflation slowed to approximately 6.6% year-on-year in December 2025, below the broader 8.0% consumer inflation rate.

The market remains geographically divided: Kyiv advertised rents softened, while Lviv, Ivano-Frankivsk and Uzhhorod recorded stronger demand.

Tenant demand increasingly rewards autonomous heating, backup power, water storage, communications resilience and proximity to shelters.

17

Advertised city rents

LUN data cited by Global Property Guide placed Kyiv one-bedroom asking rent near UAH 16,500 per month in March 2026, down 8.33% year-on-year.

Uzhhorod recorded the highest selected one-bedroom asking rent at approximately UAH 22,200 and two-bedroom rent at UAH 31,100.

These are advertised rents and should be reduced for negotiation, vacancy, agency fees, utilities, furnishing and security-related interruptions.

18

Gross rental yields

Global Property Guide estimated an average Ukrainian gross residential yield of 7.55% in March 2026.

Its city estimates included Dnipro at 9.76%, Ivano-Frankivsk at 8.15%, Kyiv at 7.27%, Lviv at 7.17%, Kharkiv at 6.97% and Odesa at 5.99%.

These yields are compensation for risk, not free excess return. Net income can fall sharply after vacancy, repairs, management, currency movement and damage.

19

Energy and utility resilience

Repeated attacks on energy infrastructure make heating, electricity, water, lifts and communications part of the investment thesis.

A resilient building should be reviewed for heating type, generator or battery capability, water pressure, floor level, lift dependence, shelter, fire safety and local repair capacity.

A high-floor apartment with weak backup systems can experience severe usability and rental problems during outages.

20

Security and physical-damage risk

No city in Ukraine should be described as risk-free. Relative safety changes over time and can change quickly.

Due diligence should include recent strike history, proximity to military or infrastructure targets, building damage, official inspection records and neighbourhood access.

Conventional property insurance may exclude or limit war damage. Coverage must be read rather than assumed.

21

NBU policy rate

The National Bank of Ukraine cut its key policy rate from 15.5% to 15.0% effective 30 January 2026.

On 19 March 2026, the NBU kept the rate unchanged at 15% because inflation expectations and external risks had worsened.

The rate supports hryvnia stability and inflation control but keeps conventional market financing expensive.

22

Market mortgage rates

Global Property Guide reported an average mortgage rate near 8.66% in December 2025.

The available mortgage market is heavily influenced by government support, bank risk limits, security geography and property eligibility.

The headline rate does not show the borrower’s down payment, fees, insurance, property-age restrictions or subsidy conditions.

23

yeOselia affordable mortgage programme

The official Ukrainian Financial Housing Company states that eligible categories receive yeOselia loans at subsidized rates of 3% or 7%, rising to 6% or 10% after the first ten years under the programme structure.

The maximum term is 240 months. Standard participation generally requires Ukrainian citizenship, solvency and housing-need conditions.

The programme is a demand and construction support mechanism; it is not a general foreign-investor mortgage product.

24

yeOselia transaction-cost example

The official programme example uses a 20% down payment, a one-time bank fee of 1% of the loan, a 1% pension-insurance contribution, property insurance, valuation and notary costs.

Actual requirements vary by borrower category, age, building age, city, partner bank and property value.

Property in active-hostility or temporarily occupied areas is excluded from programme eligibility calculations and collateral selection.

25

State registration of ownership

Ukraine’s Diia service allows owners to register rights that were acquired or documented before the digital State Register of Rights was created in 2013.

A standard online registration request can take up to five working days, but an old BTI record can require an additional verification request and extend the process.

Before purchase, the buyer should verify that the seller’s right appears correctly in the current electronic register.

26

Registry and mortgage checks

Diia provides access to information from the State Register of Rights to Immovable Property, the Property Rights Register and the State Mortgage Register.

The extract should be obtained close to completion and compared with the notary’s checks, seller documents, cadastral data and physical apartment.

A historical paper title without digital registration requires additional caution and time.

27

Foreign-buyer ownership position

Foreign individuals can generally acquire apartments and other residential buildings, subject to identity, source-of-funds, sanctions, notarization and registration requirements.

The Land Code permits foreign nationals to own non-agricultural land within settlements and non-agricultural land outside settlements where their owned real estate is located.

Agricultural land remains a separate restricted category. A house purchase must therefore include legal review of the underlying land.

28

Sanctions and financial-monitoring risk

Ukrainian banks, notaries and programme operators apply anti-money-laundering, counter-terrorist-financing and sanctions checks.

Foreign funding routes, beneficial ownership and source of wealth should be prepared before the transaction.

A buyer should never rely on informal cash, nominee or undocumented payment structures.

29

Purchase taxes and transaction costs

Transaction costs can include notary services, registration, valuation, bank fees, a pension-insurance contribution and taxes determined by the seller’s ownership period and number of sales.

The exact tax result depends on residence, property history, relationship between parties, sale frequency and transaction structure.

Because wartime legislation and administrative practice can change, a Ukrainian tax adviser and notary should provide a written cost calculation immediately before signing.

30

Damage history and compensation status

A buyer should verify whether the property has been reported as damaged or destroyed, whether compensation or a housing certificate has been claimed, and whether repair documentation exists.

Physical repair does not automatically prove structural safety or remove administrative claims.

Engineer inspection, municipal records and seller declarations should be incorporated into the contract.

31

Occupancy, family and co-owner rights

The buyer should verify ownership shares, spouse consent, minor interests, inheritance, powers of attorney and persons registered at the address.

A notarized sale does not eliminate every practical occupancy dispute if information supplied to the notary was incomplete or fraudulent.

Remote sellers and powers of attorney require heightened identity and authority verification.

32

Essential due-diligence checklist

Verify electronic title, seller identity, sanctions status, mortgages, arrests, court disputes, spouse and co-owner rights, cadastral data, permitted use and registered occupants.

Inspect structural damage, façade, roof, utilities, heating, lift, shelter, backup power, water, fire safety and building management.

For new construction, verify developer land rights, permits, financing, stage of work, utility connection, completion and future ownership registration.

33

Investment strategies that fit Ukraine

Resilient-city long-term rental: completed apartments in Kyiv, Lviv, Uzhhorod or Ivano-Frankivsk with utility resilience and durable tenant demand.

Discounted secondary-market strategy: only legally clean, undamaged property where the discount is larger than verified repair and liquidity risk.

State-supported domestic-buyer strategy: properties eligible for yeOselia demand in qualifying locations and building-age categories.

Post-war recovery strategy: long-duration capital that can tolerate uncertainty, delayed liquidity and reconstruction risk.

Operational housing strategy: professionally managed accommodation for workers, relocated businesses or reconstruction teams, subject to legal and security review.

34

Principal investor risks

Security risk: death, injury, destruction and loss of access.

Infrastructure risk: prolonged power, heat, water or communications disruption.

Legal risk: incomplete registry, disputed authority, sanctions or land restrictions.

Completion risk: unfinished projects can be delayed or abandoned.

Currency risk: hryvnia income can weaken against the investor’s reporting currency.

Liquidity risk: transaction activity can stop abruptly after security events.

Insurance risk: war damage may be excluded.

Data risk: asking prices and small transaction samples can misrepresent realizable value.

2026 SCENARIOS

Prolonged-war, stabilization and recovery paths

Base case: the market remains active but regionally divided. Safer western and central cities maintain nominal price support, while security-exposed markets remain discounted and illiquid.

Positive stabilization case: improved security, external financing, reconstruction and returning households increase transactions, mortgage activity and construction. Legally clean urban housing outperforms incomplete projects.

Adverse case: intensified attacks, energy disruption, reduced external financing or currency pressure weaken demand and increase vacancy, discounts and construction failures.

Every acquisition should remain financially survivable in the adverse case. A post-war upside should be treated as optional, not required.

35

Who Ukraine fits best

Ukraine fits highly risk-tolerant buyers with local legal, technical, security and property-management capability, patient capital and no need for rapid liquidity.

It does not fit buyers seeking capital preservation, reliable insurance, predictable utilities, conventional leverage, stable foreign-currency income or a passive cross-border investment.

36

Real Estate Taxi decision framework

First assess security and physical usability. Then verify ownership, seller authority, land rights, sanctions, damage status and digital registration.

Only after those checks should the buyer model price, rent, financing, vacancy and recovery upside.

Reject any property that fails the adverse-case test or cannot be independently inspected, registered and safely completed.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

Ukraine residential real estate FAQ

Are Ukrainian house prices rising in 2026?
State Statistics Service data cited by Global Property Guide showed 13.2% annual national growth in Q4 2025, but city performance is highly uneven.

Which cities have the strongest prices?
Kyiv, Lviv and Uzhhorod are among the most expensive sampled markets, while Ivano-Frankivsk recorded exceptionally strong multi-year growth.

What gross yield is realistic?
Global Property Guide estimated a national average near 7.55% in March 2026, before security, vacancy, damage, currency and operating costs.

What is the NBU policy rate?
The National Bank of Ukraine kept the key policy rate at 15% in March 2026.

Can foreigners buy apartments?
Generally yes, subject to sanctions, source-of-funds, notarization and registration. Agricultural land is separately restricted.

Is yeOselia available to foreign investors?
No. The official programme is for qualifying Ukrainian citizens and specific housing categories.

What is the biggest investment risk?
Security and destruction risk, followed by loss of access, utilities, insurance, legal defects and liquidity.
