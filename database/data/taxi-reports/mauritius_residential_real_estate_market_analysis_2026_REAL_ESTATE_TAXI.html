DIRECT ANSWER

Prices are still positive nationally, but the market has shifted from rapid appreciation to selective, evidence-based buying.

Mauritius combines a strong lifestyle proposition, authorised foreign ownership and tourism demand with low average gross yields, a 10% covered non-citizen buyer duty from 1 July 2026, thin luxury resale evidence and material climate exposure. The best opportunities are more likely to come from property selection and negotiation than from buying the market indiscriminately.

01
MAURITIUS 2026

Executive market verdict

Mauritius is no longer a simple “rapid appreciation” story. The official residential index still increased in the first quarter of 2026, but only by 1.0% year on year. That is a major cooling from the unusually strong index advances recorded earlier in the cycle. At the same time, foreign-eligible coastal developments remain a separate, higher-priced market driven by lifestyle demand, residence options, tourism, foreign currency and scarcity of prime sites.

The core investment challenge is yield. An average gross long-term yield around 3.22% leaves limited room after vacancy, management, maintenance, insurance, copropriété charges, furnishing and tax. Buyers therefore need a stronger reason than “property always rises”: exceptional micro-location, a credible rental operation, development value, a residence objective, long holding period or a defensible discount to comparable completed sales.

Real Estate Taxi conclusion: Mauritius can suit capital-preservation and lifestyle investors, but a low-yield premium project bought at full developer pricing is highly sensitive to tax, resale liquidity, currency and operating-cost assumptions.

MAURITIUS 2026

How to read the data without mixing incompatible evidence

This report keeps four evidence layers separate because they answer different questions:

  • Official transaction data: the Statistics Mauritius Residential Property Price Index, based on registered deeds. This is the best national direction indicator.
  • Official macro, tourism and policy data: Bank of Mauritius, Statistics Mauritius, EDB, legislation and budget documents.
  • Asking-price and asking-rent evidence: portal and broker observations compiled by Global Property Guide. These measure expectations and listings, not closed prices.
  • Real Estate Taxi analysis: yield adjustments, risk interpretation, investor suitability and 2026–2028 scenarios. These are reasoned inferences, not official forecasts or appraisals.
Do not compare a national deed-based index directly with one luxury portal average and call the difference a “market error.” The datasets cover different buyer pools, property quality, regions and pricing stages.

04
MAURITIUS 2026

Nominal versus inflation-adjusted performance

A nominal price increase is not the same as a gain in purchasing power. Global Property Guide calculated the Q1 2026 annual change at approximately −1.77% in real terms, compared with +0.96% to +1.0% nominally. Meanwhile, Statistics Mauritius reported that the CPI rose 2.4% between March and June 2026, from 109.6 to 112.2, confirming that inflation remained relevant after the property index reference quarter.

For a foreign buyer, there is a third layer: currency. A property can rise in Mauritian rupees while producing a lower return in euros, pounds or dollars if the rupee weakens. The opposite can also occur. Every investment model should therefore show:

  • MUR property return
  • inflation-adjusted MUR return
  • home-currency return after FX movement and conversion costs

07
MAURITIUS 2026

Houses versus apartments

Apartments generally offer lower maintenance complexity and can be easier to rent, but they expose the owner to syndic governance, service charges, sinking-fund quality and rules on short-term letting. Houses and villas provide land, privacy and stronger lifestyle appeal, but require greater spending on roofs, façades, pools, gardens, pumps, gates, drainage and security.

The 2025 asking-price direction—apartments down while houses rose—does not prove that every house outperformed. It may reflect different listing composition, luxury-house scarcity or developer stock. For apartments, compare net internal area, terraces, parking, storerooms and common-area allocation separately. For villas, obtain a current survey and a five-year capex plan.

08
MAURITIUS 2026

New-build, completed resale and off-plan pricing

New schemes often sell a package: modern construction, amenities, management, foreign-buyer eligibility and sometimes residence potential. That package can justify a premium, but it can also hide three margins—land/developer margin, marketing commission and legal-access premium.

  • Completed resale: easier to inspect, verify operating costs and test actual rent; resale liquidity may still be thin.
  • Completed developer unit: lower construction risk, but check unsold inventory and discounting.
  • Off-plan/VEFA: staged payments and potential early pricing, but higher completion, specification, financing and delivery risk.
Ask for the project’s full price history, number of units reserved, completed and resold, and the price of the last genuine arm’s-length resale—not only the current developer price list.
09
MAURITIUS 2026

Transaction volume and market liquidity

The Q1 2026 official RPPI release gives index movement and methodology but does not provide a complete current national transaction-count or days-on-market series. Therefore this report does not invent a liquidity figure.

Practical liquidity should be measured at project and micro-market level:

  • registered resales in the last 24–36 months
  • number of competing listings and months of inventory
  • discount from original ask to deed price
  • time needed for EDB approval and notarial completion
  • buyer pool: citizen-only, foreign-eligible or residence-linked
  • developer right of first refusal or resale restrictions

A high-end scheme can look active because of new launches while having very few secondary-market exits. That distinction is essential.

10
MAURITIUS 2026

Supply, permits, construction and vacancy

Global Property Guide’s compilation of official data reports 7,264 residential building permits in 2024, up 9.12% from 2023, while approved floor area fell 14.29% to about 1.55 million square metres. That combination suggests more approvals but a smaller average scale or changed project mix.

The same compilation cites 2022 housing stock of approximately 410,221 dwellings, with 86.1% occupied and 13.9% vacant. Vacancy does not equal available supply: some units are second homes, incomplete, unsuitable, held off-market or located away from demand nodes.

Residential gross fixed capital formation reached about MUR 35.56 billion in the first three quarters of 2025, 5.36% higher year on year. Buyers should therefore test whether their submarket has genuine scarcity or simply a scarcity narrative alongside a substantial pipeline.

11
MAURITIUS 2026

Domestic demand, foreign demand and tourism

Demand has three engines: local households, foreign residents/investors and tourism-linked buyers. The foreign-eligible segment is supported by authorised ownership schemes and the attraction of a stable island jurisdiction, but it is also more exposed to international wealth cycles, tax changes, aviation access and currency.

Tourism remained supportive. Official data show 1,436,250 tourist arrivals in 2025, up 3.9%, with tourism earnings of MUR 103.351 billion, up 10.4%. In Q1 2026 arrivals increased another 6.8% year on year to 348,445, while tourism earnings rose 28.0% to MUR 30.190 billion.

Tourism growth supports accommodation demand, but it does not guarantee profitability for every villa. Legal rental rights, seasonality, air connectivity, reviews, management quality and the number of competing units determine property-level revenue.

The average gross yield estimate of 3.22% is a useful starting point, not a return promise. Applying a simple 25%–35% operating leakage to gross rent produces an indicative 2.1%–2.4% net yield before financing, income tax and major capex.

A low gross yield is not automatically a bad investment, but it means the buyer is relying more heavily on capital appreciation, lifestyle use, residence value or currency protection.
15
MAURITIUS 2026

Mortgage rates, lending conditions and leverage

The Bank of Mauritius showed a 4.75% Key Rate on 20 May 2026. Central-bank commentary reported a weighted average rate on new housing loans around 5.49% in late-2025 data, while the broader range of new housing-loan rates was wide.

A leveraged investor buying an asset with a 3.22% gross yield may have negative cash carry before principal repayment. Financing analysis should include:

  • loan currency versus rental currency
  • fixed, variable and repricing terms
  • deposit and debt-to-income assessment
  • life and property insurance requirements
  • bank valuation below purchase price
  • prepayment, arrangement and conversion fees

Non-citizen financing also interacts with EDB rules on foreign-currency funding. The PDS guidelines require outside funds and specify conditions where local-currency borrowing is used.

16
MAURITIUS 2026

Affordability and currency risk

Local affordability is constrained when sale prices rise faster than household income and mortgage costs. The national index’s rapid increase since 2019 does not mean local wages followed the same path. This helps explain why the citizen market, centre-region market and foreign-eligible coastal market can move differently.

For international buyers, the Mauritian rupee can create both opportunity and risk. Model at least three FX cases over the holding period: unchanged, 10% weaker and 10% stronger against the buyer’s home currency. Apply the same FX assumptions to purchase costs, rent, resale proceeds and debt service.

A “cheap” MUR price after currency depreciation can be offset by imported construction costs, higher maintenance inputs and a lower home-currency resale value.
17
MAURITIUS 2026

Purchase taxes and transaction costs from July 2026

For a covered residential transfer to a non-citizen on or after 1 July 2026, current legislation and budget implementation materials indicate a 10% buyer registration duty, replacing the earlier 5% rate for relevant EDB property-scheme and approved G+2 transactions.

Seller-side land transfer taxation also changed. For certain non-citizen resales, official budget material describes the higher of 10% of property value or 30% of the gain. The exact rule depends on the deed, seller status, property route and transitional provisions.

  • Request a written notarial closing statement before paying a reservation deposit.
  • Confirm whether duty is based on price, market value or another statutory valuation.
  • Add notary, valuation, bank, EDB, due-diligence and translation costs.
  • Check whether a reservation signed before a policy date qualifies for transitional treatment.
A 10% entry tax materially raises the break-even appreciation required for a short holding period. It also makes the difference between developer list price and genuine resale value more important.
18
MAURITIUS 2026

Annual ownership, rental-income and exit taxation

Annual cost varies by property and ownership structure. A proper budget should include local authority charges where applicable, copropriété or scheme service charges, sinking fund, building and contents insurance, cyclone excess, pool/garden, pest treatment, security, utilities, management, accounting and maintenance reserve.

Rental income arising in Mauritius is taxable under Mauritian rules. The effective result depends on taxpayer residence, ownership vehicle, allowable expenses, other income and current Mauritius Revenue Authority provisions. This report does not assume a universal flat rate.

Mauritius is often described as having no separate general capital-gains tax, but property exits can still trigger substantial land transfer tax, including gain-based rules for some non-citizen resales. “No capital gains tax” should never be treated as “no exit tax.”

19
MAURITIUS 2026

Foreign-buyer routes and residence relevance

Non-citizens cannot treat the whole island as an unrestricted property market. Typical authorised routes include approved PDS, Smart City, Invest Hotel and other legally specified categories. Each route has its own certificate, property type, approval and operating conditions.

  • PDS minimum: official PDS guidelines state no universal minimum purchase price under the scheme itself.
  • Residence threshold: acquisition of qualifying residential property of at least USD 375,000 can support a residence permit while the property is held.
  • Separate acquisition route: EDB information also describes a USD 500,000 minimum route for certain property acquisitions; do not confuse it with the PDS rule.
  • State land/G+2 changes: 2026/27 policy restricts new authorisations for sale to non-citizens on State Land or Pas Géométriques, subject to stated prior-approval and existing-owner exceptions.

Residence is an immigration benefit, not an investment return. Verify spouse/dependent eligibility, tax residence, minimum physical presence, resale consequences and what happens to permits when the property is transferred.

MAURITIUS 2026

Standard buying procedure

  1. Choose the legal acquisition route and verify buyer/entity eligibility.
  2. Appoint an independent Mauritian notary and, for complex deals, independent legal/tax advisers.
  3. Obtain title, transcription, encumbrance, survey, planning, permit, EDB and scheme documents.
  4. Agree a conditional reservation or preliminary contract with clear refund triggers.
  5. Complete source-of-funds, bank, KYC and EDB/PAMS application requirements.
  6. For off-plan property, review VEFA contract, completion guarantee and payment certification.
  7. Sign the authentic deed before the notary, pay duties and register/transcribe the deed.
  8. Obtain keys, snagging record, insurance, utilities, syndic records and rental approvals.

Under the PDS process, EDB authorisation is normally time-limited. Do not let the purchase contract become unconditional before required approvals and due diligence are complete.

21
MAURITIUS 2026

Title, cadastral, zoning and permit risks

  • Confirm freehold versus leasehold, State Land or Pas Géométriques status and remaining lease term.
  • Verify the deed chain, registered mortgages, servitudes, rights of way, coastal access and boundary survey.
  • Match cadastral/survey plans to the walls, pool, parking, access road and actual occupation.
  • Check land-use, building, subdivision, environmental and occupancy permits.
  • For apartments, inspect copropriété rules, unit entitlement, common property and voting rights.
  • Confirm that extensions, terraces, pools, gazebos and seawalls were authorised.
  • Review disputes, unpaid syndic charges, contractor claims and tax liabilities.
A beautiful coastal villa can be impaired by a short lease, unregistered access, illegal seawall, encroachment or building outside the approved footprint. These are valuation issues, not only legal issues.
22
MAURITIUS 2026

Off-plan and VEFA due diligence

PDS units may be sold during construction under VEFA or sale-for-future-delivery structures. The PDS guidelines describe a standard VEFA payment sequence of 25% on deed, 10% after foundations, 35% at roofed-in stage, 25% on completion and 5% on availability.

Do not rely only on the statutory structure. Verify:

  • land ownership and developer encumbrances
  • bank completion or financial guarantee and its calling conditions
  • quantity-surveyor certification for each stage
  • specification, tolerances, substitution rights and area measurement
  • long-stop completion date and buyer remedies
  • escrow controls and refund rights
  • one-, two- and ten-year defect/structural insurance evidence described in the scheme guidelines
23
MAURITIUS 2026

Climate, insurance and building-condition risks

World Bank climate analysis identifies sea-level rise, tropical cyclones, storm surge, coastal flooding and flash floods among Mauritius’s major physical risks. Property-level exposure varies sharply over short distances.

  • Obtain flood and cyclone claims history for the building and surrounding road.
  • Check elevation, drainage, retaining walls, roof fixing, shutters and emergency power/water.
  • Inspect salt corrosion in reinforcement, metalwork, air-conditioning and electrical systems.
  • Review coastal setback, erosion trend, seawall responsibility and beach-access changes.
  • Get a binding insurance quotation before the purchase becomes unconditional.
  • Read exclusions, cyclone deductibles, underinsurance clauses and loss-of-rent cover.

Climate risk should affect the discount rate, maintenance reserve, insurance assumption and resale scenario—not sit in a generic disclaimer.

MAURITIUS 2026

Who the market may and may not suit

Potentially suitable for:

  • long-hold lifestyle buyers who value personal use
  • foreign investors seeking an authorised ownership/residence route
  • cash buyers targeting scarce, completed prime assets at evidence-based prices
  • operators with a proven short-term rental and management strategy
  • development investors with strong local legal, planning and construction capability

Potentially unsuitable for:

  • high-leverage investors needing immediate positive cash flow
  • short-term speculators facing 10% entry duty and thin resale liquidity
  • buyers relying on developer appreciation projections
  • investors who cannot tolerate cyclone, currency or operating-cost volatility
  • remote owners without independent property oversight

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

Mauritius real estate FAQ

Answers distinguish current official rules from market estimates and analytical assumptions.

Are Mauritius home prices still rising in 2026?
Official transaction data show the Residential Property Price Index at 240.9 in the first quarter of 2026, up 1.3% from the previous quarter and 1.0% from a year earlier. That is positive nominal growth, but much slower than the market's earlier surge, and inflation-adjusted performance was negative in the Global Property Guide calculation.
What does the official Mauritius property index actually measure?
Statistics Mauritius compiles a transaction-based national index from deeds registered with the Registrar-General. It covers houses and apartments purchased by domestic and foreign buyers across four broad regions. It does not publish a city-level index and the latest observations are provisional.
Can foreigners buy residential property in Mauritius?
Yes, but generally only through authorised routes such as approved Property Development Scheme, Smart City, Invest Hotel or other specifically permitted property categories. The exact route, approval, land tenure and buyer entity must be checked before signing.
Is USD 375,000 the minimum price for every foreign buyer?
No. The Property Development Scheme guidelines state that PDS itself has no universal minimum acquisition price. USD 375,000 is the threshold commonly relevant to a residence permit linked to qualifying residential property. Separate routes and projects can have different minimums or conditions.
What purchase tax applies to a non-citizen from July 2026?
For covered foreign-eligible residential transfers on or after 1 July 2026, the buyer registration duty generally rises to 10%. The seller-side land transfer tax rules also changed. A Mauritian notary should confirm the exact deed, category, valuation base and transitional treatment.
What rental yield is realistic in Mauritius?
The January 2026 asking-rent compilation cited by Global Property Guide indicates an average gross yield of about 3.22%. A simple operating-cost adjustment can reduce a long-term rental to roughly 2.1%–2.4% net before financing, income tax and major capital expenditure. Short-term rentals can generate higher gross revenue but also carry higher management, utility, furnishing, vacancy and regulatory costs.
Which areas are strongest for rental demand?
The north and west have the deepest foreign-buyer and holiday-rental ecosystems, while Black River, Rivière du Rempart and Pamplemousses show larger short-term rental inventories. The centre is typically cheaper and more locally driven. Micro-location, beach access, employment nodes, schools and legal rental eligibility matter more than region labels alone.
Are asking prices reliable evidence of value?
No. Asking prices show seller expectations and market segmentation, not completed transaction values. Foreign-eligible listings can be materially more expensive than local-only stock. A buyer should compare registered deeds, recent project resales, land tenure, net usable area, condition and realistic rent before relying on a portal average.
What are the main off-plan risks?
Developer solvency, construction delay, specification changes, completion guarantees, title and encumbrances, escrow controls, staged-payment certification, snagging and warranty enforcement are central risks. PDS sales during construction normally use VEFA or sale-for-future-delivery structures, but the contract and guarantees still require independent review.
What property risks are specific to Mauritius?
Cyclone wind, flash flooding, storm surge, coastal erosion, salt-air corrosion, humidity, termites, water storage, drainage and insurance exclusions can materially affect costs and resale. Coastal and leasehold properties require especially careful boundary, access, erosion-setback and tenure review.
