DIRECT MARKET VERDICT

Cambodia offers negotiating opportunity, but not low-risk simplicity.

The central investment fact is a continuing correction rather than a confirmed recovery. Official price indicators remain negative, Phnom Penh is weaker than the provinces, mortgage demand is cautious, and the banking system is still working through property-related asset-quality pressure. At the same time, approvals and planned residential investment rose in 2025, creating a possible future-supply mismatch if absorption remains weak.

That combination can create discounts and motivated sellers, especially in investor-oriented condominiums. It also increases the importance of unit-level quality, title certainty, completed-building performance, realistic rent evidence and exit liquidity. A nominal bargain can become expensive if ownership rights, construction delivery, management, vacancy or resale are misunderstood.

EVIDENCE FRAMEWORK

What the numbers mean—and what they do not mean

A serious market report must keep transaction evidence separate from advertised prices, sales material and analytical inference.

A

Official and regulatory evidence

National Bank of Cambodia price indices, credit data, project approvals and banking-sector information provide the strongest national trend signals used here. They describe the market at an aggregate level; they do not establish the fair value of a specific unit.

B

Asking-price and broker evidence

Advertised sale prices, advertised rents, developer price lists and broker supply estimates are useful for current market positioning. They can overstate achieved prices, occupancy and yield. Every such figure in this report is labeled accordingly.

C

Real Estate Taxi analysis

Scenario ranges, risk interpretation, buyer suitability and due-diligence priorities are analytical conclusions, not official forecasts. They are designed to make the evidence decision-useful without pretending that uncertain outcomes are facts.

MARKET STRUCTURE

Prices, supply, liquidity and buyer demand

The national correction is real, but Cambodia is not one uniform market. Phnom Penh condominiums, landed housing, provincial homes and resort-linked properties respond to different demand pools.

01

National price history and current direction

The nationwide Residential Property Price Index was reported down 3.67% year-on-year in January 2026, following a 3.8% decline in 2025. Earlier annual readings were approximately +0.45% in 2024 and −2.95% in 2023. Adjusted for inflation, the January 2026 decline was approximately 4.87%.

The pattern indicates a multi-year correction, not a single weak month. The official 2025 review said the decline moderated during part of the second half but softened again late in the year. That matters because a slower fall is not the same as a durable turn upward.

Real Estate Taxi interpretation: buyers should underwrite flat-to-soft nominal prices as the base case until there is broader evidence of improved mortgage demand, stronger completed-unit absorption and sustained index stabilization.

Evidence class: National Bank of Cambodia index as reported in the official Financial Stability Review and current market compilation. Individual properties can diverge substantially.

02

Nominal versus real performance

A nominal price decline already reduces value in money terms. When consumer-price inflation is also positive, the inflation-adjusted loss is larger. In January 2026, the reported nationwide real decline was about 4.87% year-on-year.

For a USD-based foreign buyer, the practical result also depends on the actual transaction currency, any KHR-denominated costs, financing currency and the buyer's home currency. Cambodia is highly dollarized, but “priced in dollars” does not eliminate local economic or liquidity risk.

A seller holding the nominal asking price unchanged may still be conceding value in real terms—but only if the asset can actually be sold and rented at the assumed price.

05

Apartments versus houses; new-build versus existing

Foreign buyers are structurally concentrated in eligible condominium units because direct land ownership is restricted. This can create a foreign-buyer market that behaves differently from local landed housing.

  • Completed condominium: permits, common-area condition, occupancy, service charges, actual rents and management quality can be inspected.
  • Off-plan condominium: may offer staged payments or incentives, but adds completion, financing, specification, registration and handover risk.
  • Existing landed home: may serve local owner-occupier demand better, but land-title and foreign-ownership structure are central issues.
  • New landed development: can benefit from infrastructure and amenities, while exposing buyers to developer, access-road, utilities and title-delivery risk.
There is no dependable nationwide public series that cleanly separates achieved price changes for houses versus apartments and new versus existing homes. Any precise universal premium should be treated skeptically.
06

Transaction volume and market liquidity

Current national information is stronger on price indices, approvals, lending and project supply than on a comprehensive public count of completed arm's-length residential transactions. Broker-reported sales rates for monitored projects have been very low, around 3%–4%, but that is not an official national transaction rate.

Liquidity risk is therefore best tested directly: days on market for comparable units, discounts from initial asking price, number of genuine competing listings, cash-buyer depth, mortgage eligibility and completed resales in the same building.

Do not model a fast exit simply because many listings exist. High listing volume can indicate supply, not liquidity.
07

Supply, approvals, completions and vacancy

The official 2025 review recorded 55 approved residential projects, up from 40 in 2024. Housing approvals increased from 27 to 41, while condominium approvals edged down from 12 to 11. Approved residential development capital rose 22.1% to US$982.4 million, led by housing.

Broker estimates place total Phnom Penh condominium stock near 80,000 units in 2025, and one broker reported roughly 1,770 high-end units handed over in the second half. These are market estimates, not an official census of occupied or rentable units.

Nationally standardized residential vacancy is not readily available. A buyer should instead calculate economic vacancy at building level: physically empty units, dark units held by investors, units listed for rent, developer inventory, unpaid service charges and operational amenities.

Supply risk: rising approvals while prices and mortgage demand remain soft can prolong buyer leverage and delay broad price recovery if projects proceed faster than real occupancy.
08

Demand and foreign-buyer demand

Domestic demand is constrained by cautious lending, weaker mortgage growth and affordability pressure. Foreign demand is more selective than during earlier development booms and often concentrates in completed, legally eligible condominium units with professional management.

Demand sources include local households, Cambodian diaspora, expatriate tenants and buyers, regional investors and tourism-linked purchasers. Each group has different unit-size, financing and resale preferences.

  • Local owner-occupiers often prioritize access, schools, parking and landed formats.
  • Foreign investors prioritize legal eligibility, USD income, management and exitability.
  • Tourism buyers prioritize destination demand, but face higher income volatility.
INCOME & FINANCING

Rental performance, mortgages and affordability

Advertised gross yields can look attractive. The investable result depends on achieved rent, occupancy, operating cost, legal use and a realistic exit assumption.

09

Long-term rents and gross rental yields

A July 2026 Global Property Guide dataset based on asking prices and asking rents indicated an average gross yield of 6.64% in Phnom Penh. Its example units were:

These are advertised-market ratios, not audited achieved returns. Asking rent can be discounted; furniture and fit-out may be owner-funded; and vacant periods, leasing fees, management, repairs, property tax, insurance, service charges and utility leakage reduce net income.

Practical underwriting: use a verified signed lease or three recently achieved comparable rents, deduct at least one conservative vacancy period, and model a full replacement reserve. A general market estimate suggests net yields may run 1.5–2 percentage points below gross, but building-specific costs can make the gap larger.

11

Short-term rental market

Phnom Penh, Siem Reap, coastal destinations and island markets can attract short-stay demand, but revenue patterns differ. Business, government and expatriate demand can support Phnom Penh; leisure travel dominates Siem Reap and resort areas.

Short-term underwriting should include platform commission, professional management, cleaning, linen, electricity, internet, consumables, guest damage, maintenance, seasonal vacancy, licensing and building rules. Confirm that the title, building management and local authorities permit the intended use.

A property that works only under an aggressive nightly-rate assumption is speculation on operating performance, not a stable real-estate income asset.
12

Tourism sensitivity

Tourism can support rental demand, but it also increases exposure to flights, border conditions, global travel, destination reputation and competing hotel supply. The World Bank's June 2026 outlook described a difficult domestic backdrop with weaker consumption, remittances and credit, while expecting gradual tourism recovery in its baseline.

Resort-linked buyers should compare three income cases: long-term tenancy, professionally managed short-stay use and owner-use with no income. The investment should remain acceptable under the least optimistic legally viable case.

13

Mortgage rates and lending conditions

Deposit-taking institutions' mortgage credit contracted 5.4% in 2025, even as credit to real-estate activities excluding mortgages rose. At December 2025, reported new term-loan rates were approximately 8.76% in KHR and 8.61% in USD.

For property demand, the direction of credit is as important as the quoted rate. A market can have available loans but still experience weak absorption if underwriting tightens, down-payments rise, household income is stressed or borrowers avoid leverage.

  • Confirm whether the quoted rate is fixed, floating or promotional.
  • Model fees, insurance, currency and early-repayment terms.
  • Do not assume foreign buyers receive the same products as local borrowers.
  • Stress-test debt service with lower rent and longer vacancy.
14

Affordability and banking stress

The World Bank projected 2026 real GDP growth of 3.9% and reported that non-performing loans rose to 8.9% in 2025. The property correction, weak credit and construction downturn remain part of the macro-financial risk picture.

Affordability is not captured by sale price alone. Buyers face down-payment requirements, relatively high lending rates, household income constraints, service charges and transaction taxes. A property can become “cheaper” while still remaining difficult to finance.

For investors, banking stress can create motivated sellers—but it can also reduce buyer financing at exit.
OWNERSHIP, TAX & TRANSACTION

Foreign buyer rules, costs and the buying procedure

Cambodia's investability depends heavily on acquiring the right legal interest, registering it correctly and avoiding structures that create hidden control or enforcement risk.

15

Foreign ownership: condominium rights versus land

Official Cambodian investment guidance states that foreigners cannot directly own land; land ownership is reserved for Khmer citizens and qualifying Khmer legal entities. A company is treated as Khmer for landholding purposes only when Cambodian ownership meets the legal threshold, commonly at least 51%.

Under the law governing private units in co-owned buildings, legally qualified foreigners may own eligible private units from the first floor upward. Ground and underground floors are excluded. Foreign ownership does not include the underlying land, and registration is required.

A commonly cited project-level restriction limits foreign ownership to 70% of the private units in a co-owned building. The exact foreign quota, co-owned-building status and remaining eligibility must be confirmed from official registration records for the specific project.

  • Verify that the building is legally registered as co-owned.
  • Verify the unit's floor and private-unit title eligibility.
  • Confirm the remaining foreign quota before paying.
  • Confirm that the seller can transfer clean registered title.
  • Do not assume a long lease, nominee or company interest equals freehold ownership.
16

Residency and investment-program relevance

Property ownership should not be treated as automatically granting permanent residence, citizenship or unrestricted immigration status. Cambodia has offered investment-related pathways and visa options over time, but eligibility, qualifying investment, documentation and continuation can change.

Separate the decisions:

  • Is the property legally and financially attractive?
  • Does the buyer independently qualify for the desired visa or residence status?
  • Would the investment remain acceptable if immigration policy changed?
Never pay a property premium based only on an agent's verbal immigration promise. Obtain current written advice from licensed immigration and legal professionals.
17

Taxes and purchase costs

A commonly referenced acquisition transfer or stamp-duty rate is 4%. Total buyer-side acquisition cost is often estimated near 4%–5% before financing-specific costs, while a full buy-and-sell round trip can be materially higher. These are planning estimates, not a transaction quote.

Policy support has included stamp-duty relief through 2026 for qualifying registered borey and condominium purchases. The official 2025 Financial Stability Review described a full exemption for first-time buyers of eligible units up to US$210,000 and partial relief in other qualifying cases.

18

Annual ownership, rental income and capital gains

Commonly cited annual property tax is 0.1% on the portion of assessed property value above KHR 100 million. Nonresident rental income is commonly cited as subject to 20% tax. The exact taxpayer status, deductible costs, withholding, filing and treaty treatment require current professional advice.

The introduction of the 20% capital-gains tax on real estate has been postponed through the end of 2026 according to the official 2025 financial-stability review. A buyer should not assume this postponement will continue beyond the stated period.

  • Budget service charges and sinking-fund contributions.
  • Confirm tax registration and filing responsibility.
  • Retain invoices for basis and deductible expenditure.
  • Model capital-gains tax under the law expected at exit, not only at purchase.

RISK, OPPORTUNITY & OUTLOOK

Due diligence, investor fit and scenarios through 2028

The best opportunities are likely to be specific assets purchased with legal clarity and a genuine income or use case—not generic exposure to “Cambodia property.”

20

Title, cadastral, zoning and permit risks

  • Title type: identify hard title, soft title, strata/private-unit title, lease or contractual interest.
  • Boundary: compare cadastral plan, survey, physical occupation, access and neighboring use.
  • Encumbrance: search mortgages, charges, court disputes, claims and developer financing.
  • Planning: confirm permitted use, height, density, setbacks and future infrastructure impact.
  • Construction: verify approvals, completion status, occupancy permission and deviations.
  • Utilities: legal and reliable electricity, water, sewage, drainage, road and fire access.
  • Seller authority: spouse, shareholders, directors, attorney and beneficial owner.
A physical apartment is not enough. The buyer needs a legally transferable, registrable and enforceable interest matching the commercial promise.

22

Climate, insurance and building-condition risks

Flooding, intense rainfall, heat and drainage stress are material considerations in Cambodia. Risk varies dramatically by elevation, local drainage, road level, river proximity, fill quality, basement design and maintenance.

  • Inspect the site during or immediately after heavy rain where possible.
  • Request historical flood and water-ingress evidence from management and neighbors.
  • Check basement pumps, backup power, drainage, waterproofing and mold.
  • Inspect façade, roof, lifts, fire systems, generators and water storage.
  • Obtain an insurance quote before purchase and read flood exclusions.
  • Model higher cooling, façade and waterproofing maintenance costs.
A unit on a high floor can still suffer investment loss if common areas, access roads, electrical systems or the building's reputation are damaged by flooding.
23

Border and location-specific security risk

Border-area conditions and travel advice can change quickly. Property near international borders requires current verification of access, crossings, trade flows, insurance, tenant demand and security conditions—not only historic tourism or land-value assumptions.

For every location, verify:

  • current government travel and security advice;
  • road and border-crossing status;
  • effect on tourism and local employment;
  • insurance availability and exclusions;
  • liquidity under a prolonged disruption case.
24

Who the market may suit

  • Cash-rich buyers able to negotiate completed units without depending on rapid appreciation.
  • Investors with Cambodia operating knowledge and strong local legal/management support.
  • Long-horizon buyers seeking USD-linked rent and willing to accept liquidity risk.
  • Owner-users who value the property independently of investment return.
  • Specialists able to rehabilitate under-managed or distressed assets after full title review.
Best fit: buyers who treat legal structure, building operation and exit depth as core investment variables—not administrative details.
25

Who should be cautious

  • Buyers needing a quick, predictable resale.
  • Highly leveraged investors relying on full occupancy.
  • Foreign buyers who want direct land ownership without complex structuring.
  • Remote investors without independent management and legal oversight.
  • Buyers relying on guaranteed rent, citizenship or appreciation claims.
  • Anyone unable to absorb construction delay, vacancy or policy change.
A high advertised yield does not compensate for an ownership structure the buyer does not understand or an asset that cannot be exited.

Detailed buyer and investor due-diligence checklist

  • Identify the exact legal interest being sold and whether it is registrable.
  • Use an independent Cambodian lawyer with property experience.
  • Verify seller identity, marital status, company authority and beneficial owner.
  • Obtain a current official title and encumbrance search.
  • Confirm cadastral boundaries, access and physical occupation.
  • For foreigners, confirm co-owned-building status, eligible floor and remaining quota.
  • Verify project, construction, completion, occupancy and fire approvals.
  • Compare approved drawings with the delivered unit and common areas.
  • Inspect structure, façade, roof, waterproofing, lifts, generators and fire systems.
  • Review flood history, drainage, elevation, water ingress and mold.
  • Confirm legal utility connections and service reliability.
  • Review service-charge budget, arrears, sinking fund and major contracts.
  • Review owners' association rules, voting rights and management term.
  • Confirm whether short-term rental is legally and contractually permitted.
  • Verify achieved rent using signed leases or independent tenant evidence.
  • Calculate net yield after vacancy, management, repairs, tax and replacement reserve.
  • Inspect developer inventory and competing units in the same building.
  • Establish real resale evidence, not only active asking prices.
  • For off-plan, verify land ownership, project funding and lender security.
  • Require objective milestones, long-stop date and delay remedies.
  • Verify how buyer instalments are held and refunded if conditions fail.
  • Translate and reconcile all contract versions; identify which language prevails.
  • Obtain written tax and closing-cost calculations.
  • Confirm bank account ownership and payment instructions independently.
  • Use conditions precedent before a deposit becomes non-refundable.
  • Arrange insurance quotation and review exclusions before completion.
  • Stress-test price, rent, vacancy, interest and exit period.
  • Document handover, defects, warranties, keys, meters and inventories.
  • Complete registration and retain official originals after closing.
  • Review the investment annually against actual income, supply and legal changes.
FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

Cambodia residential property FAQ

Are Cambodia residential prices rising or falling in 2026?
The latest clearly reported nationwide figure available for this report is a 3.67% year-on-year decline in January 2026. Phnom Penh was weaker at −4.52%, while the provinces were down 3.49%. These are index movements, not a guarantee that every unit or district changed by the same amount.
Can a foreigner own a condominium in Cambodia?
A legally qualified foreigner may own a private unit in a registered co-owned building from the first floor upward. Ground and underground floors are excluded. The land beneath the building is not foreign-owned, and project-level foreign ownership limits and registration conditions must be checked before purchase.
Can a foreigner own land in Cambodia?
Direct land ownership is generally reserved for Khmer citizens and eligible Khmer legal entities. Foreign buyers should not treat nominee structures as equivalent to secure direct title. Long leases, properly structured local companies, or other legal arrangements require independent Cambodian legal advice and careful beneficial-ownership review.
What gross rental yield is realistic in Phnom Penh?
Portal-based asking-price and asking-rent data published in July 2026 indicated a Phnom Penh average gross yield of about 6.64%, with sample unit yields ranging roughly from 5.45% to 7.40%. Achieved net yield can be materially lower after vacancy, management, furnishing, repairs, tax, service charges and leasing commissions.
Is Cambodia currently a buyer's market?
For many urban condominium and developer-held segments, subdued demand, price-index declines, cautious mortgage lending and substantial competing supply improve buyer negotiating power. However, bargaining strength varies sharply by title quality, developer liquidity, completed versus off-plan status, building management and micro-location.
What is the biggest legal risk for a foreign buyer?
The largest risk is not merely price; it is acquiring an interest that does not provide the ownership, registration priority, land rights or exit protection the buyer assumed. Independent title verification, co-owned-building registration, foreign quota confirmation, developer authority, encumbrance searches and contract enforceability are essential.
Should an investor buy off-plan in Cambodia in 2026?
Only with a risk-adjusted discount, strong evidence of construction funding, clear escrow or payment protection, verified approvals, realistic completion milestones, enforceable delay remedies and a credible operating plan. A glossy launch price or guaranteed-rent promise is not a substitute for bankability and delivery evidence.
What taxes and purchase costs should be budgeted?
A commonly referenced transfer or stamp-duty rate is 4%, but exemptions and relief can apply to qualifying purchases and policy has changed over time. Buyers should obtain a transaction-specific written calculation from a Cambodian tax adviser and lawyer, including registration, legal, valuation, financing and agent costs.
Is short-term rental income dependable?
No. Tourism-linked revenue is seasonal and sensitive to airline capacity, destination demand, platform rules, property management, competition and local enforcement. A serious underwriting should use conservative occupancy, include replacement and operating costs, and remain viable under a long-term-rental fallback.
What should be checked immediately before paying a deposit?
Confirm the seller's identity and authority, title type, encumbrances, cadastral match, building and unit registration, foreign-ownership eligibility, permits, taxes, service-charge arrears, actual unit condition, utility access, management records, contract language, payment destination and refund conditions.

FINAL INVESTMENT RULE

Buy the verified legal and operating asset—not the brochure.

Cambodia can reward disciplined, locally supported buyers who negotiate from evidence. It can punish investors who confuse a low asking price, foreign-buyer marketing or projected yield with secure ownership, delivered quality and resale liquidity.
