Bulgaria Residential Real Estate Market Analysis 2026

Bulgaria began its first year inside the euro area with residential prices still rising rapidly, mortgage rates remaining unusually low and buyers becoming more selective after the pre-euro rush. The headline market is strong, but it is not uniform: Sofia, Plovdiv, Varna, Burgas, secondary cities, Black Sea resorts, ski locations and depopulating rural areas have fundamentally different liquidity, supply, tenant demand and resale logic.

EXECUTIVE INTERPRETATION

A strong market entering a more analytical phase

Bulgaria’s national price index accelerated again in Q1 2026, but the market underneath that figure has already changed. The urgency connected with anticipated euro adoption moved part of future demand into 2024 and 2025. By late 2025, transaction volumes were falling even while values remained comparatively resilient, indicating fewer deals at higher average ticket sizes rather than an immediate price collapse.

The next phase is likely to reward property quality more than simple market exposure. Buyers are increasingly separating completed, energy-efficient and well-connected homes from compromised projects, difficult titles, weak building management and locations dependent on one seasonal demand source.

Core reading: Bulgaria remains a growth market, but 2026 is less suitable for indiscriminate buying. The difference between a liquid city apartment and a nominally cheap but difficult-to-resell unit is becoming more important.
REAL ESTATE TAXI VERDICT

Positive fundamentals, rising execution risk

Low mortgage pricing, accumulated savings, urban concentration and euro adoption support demand. Rapid prior appreciation, affordability pressure, record completions, demographic decline and a large vacant stock argue against assuming that every segment can continue rising at double-digit rates.

03 · TRANSACTIONS AND BUYER BEHAVIOUR

Fewer transactions do not yet equal collapsing demand

NSI transaction indicators show that the number of dwellings transacted declined by 2.5% in 2025, with a much sharper 22.5% annual drop in Q4. Existing-home transactions fell more heavily than new-build transactions. Transaction value declined less than transaction count, showing that the market was concentrating in more expensive or higher-quality properties.

In the six principal city series, Plovdiv was the exception, recording annual growth in both transaction number and value in Q4 2025. Sofia, Varna, Burgas, Ruse and Stara Zagora recorded falling volumes. This supports a rebalancing interpretation: buyers are taking longer, comparing projects and negotiating instead of buying primarily from fear of future price increases.

WHAT STILL SUPPORTS DEMAND
  • Low mortgage interest rates and strong bank liquidity
  • Rising urban incomes and internal migration
  • Housing used as a savings and inflation hedge
  • Limited developed capital-market alternatives
  • Demand for completed, efficient and functional homes
  • Euro-area integration and lower currency friction
04 · DEVELOPMENT PIPELINE

Record completions were followed by a sharp quarterly drop, while permits and starts recovered

2025 completions

37,571 newly completed dwellings, up 78.9%. This was an exceptional delivery year containing projects launched earlier in the cycle.

Q1 2026 completions

4,151 completed dwellings, down 48.7% year-on-year against an unusually high comparison base.

Q1 2026 permits

Permits covered 13,567 dwellings, 28.3% above the same quarter of 2025.

Q1 2026 starts

Construction started on 10,193 dwellings, up 67.4% year-on-year after a weak 2025 starts profile.

Approved supply is not the same as delivered supply. Project delays, land cost, labour shortages, infrastructure capacity, permit timing, developer financing and sales absorption determine how much of the pipeline becomes usable housing.

Buyer implication: compare the specific project’s legal and construction stage with the wider local pipeline. A unit bought early may face future competition from several nearby deliveries.
05 · RENTAL MARKET

Rents are still growing faster than general inflation, but purchase prices have compressed yields

Bulgaria has a high owner-occupation rate and a comparatively small formal rental sector. Actual-rent inflation slowed from a peak of 15.7% in August 2025 to 8.1% in April 2026, still above general inflation. Sofia rents reportedly rose around 20% in 2025, with Varna and Burgas closer to 10–15%.

Quality-apartment survey data for Sofia indicated approximately €650 per month for one bedroom, €900 for two bedrooms and €1,300 for three bedrooms in 2025. Wider market asking rents vary substantially by city, furnishing, location, building age and whether the unit serves long-term residents or seasonal guests.

06 · MORTGAGES

Cheap credit remains one of the market’s strongest supports

BNB data placed the average rate on new housing loans at 2.45% in April 2026 and the rate on outstanding housing loans at 2.68%. New housing lending reached about €2.3 billion in the first four months of 2026, 16% above the corresponding 2025 period, while outstanding housing loans reached approximately €17.9 billion.

Credit is growing faster than the historical mortgage base, but household mortgage penetration remains low by many European standards. The risk is therefore less a highly leveraged national stock than a marginal buyer who has stretched to meet rapidly rising prices.

STRESS TEST

Do not underwrite at today’s payment only

  • Test at least a 2–3 percentage-point rate increase
  • Include insurance and all bank charges
  • Allow for income or vacancy interruption
  • Do not use short-term rental income at 100% occupancy
  • Keep a repair and common-area reserve
07 · EURO ADOPTION

The euro changed transaction mechanics more than Bulgaria’s underlying currency risk

Bulgaria joined the euro area on 1 January 2026 at the fixed conversion rate of BGN 1.95583 per euro. Because the lev had long been fixed to the euro, the changeover did not represent a sudden currency revaluation. Its main property effects are simpler euro-denominated pricing, lower conversion friction, integration with euro-area monetary policy and a possible perception of lower cross-border transaction risk.

Market expectations had already influenced behaviour before adoption. The more useful 2026 question is not whether the euro will automatically lift every price, but whether a specific property remains affordable, financeable and competitive after the anticipation premium has been absorbed.

Euro adoption can improve market visibility and confidence, but it cannot repair a weak title, poor building, seasonal location or overpriced unit.
08 · FOREIGN OWNERSHIP

Apartments are usually simpler than transactions involving land

Foreign buyers can participate in Bulgaria’s residential market, but the correct ownership route depends on nationality and the property being acquired. EU and EEA nationals and companies have broader rights to acquire land under Bulgarian and EU-accession rules. Other foreign persons can generally acquire apartments, buildings and limited rights in rem, while direct acquisition of the underlying land may be restricted unless treaty or statutory conditions are met.

A common structure for land-connected purchases is a Bulgarian company, but company ownership creates accounting, tax, compliance and beneficial-ownership obligations. It should never be formed merely because an agent says it is standard.

VERIFY BEFORE DEPOSIT
  • Nationality and EU/EEA status
  • Apartment-only or land-inclusive title
  • Agricultural, forest or regulated building land
  • Personal ownership or Bulgarian company
  • Residence, tax and reporting consequences
  • Whether a power of attorney is properly limited
09 · PURCHASE AND OWNERSHIP COSTS

Local tax, notary, registration and advisory costs must be calculated property by property

Local acquisition tax

Set by the municipality and applied to the higher relevant taxable or transaction basis under local rules. Confirm the exact rate for the municipality before signing.

Notary and registration

A notarial deed and registration in the Property Register are central transaction steps. Fees depend on value and the official tariff.

Annual property costs

Owners may face municipal real-estate tax, waste fee, building charges, insurance and maintenance. Resort management fees can be material.

Rental income

Bulgarian personal-income taxation generally uses a 10% rate on the relevant taxable base, but deductions, residence status and business activity can change the calculation.

Planning allowance: a cautious buyer can initially reserve roughly 4–8% above the purchase price for acquisition-related costs when agency fees and professional advice are included, then replace that allowance with exact written quotations.
10 · LEGAL AND TITLE SYSTEM

The deed is only one part of the verification process

The Bulgarian Property Register records acts that recognize, transfer, amend or terminate ownership and other real rights, together with mortgages and foreclosures. The system should be checked alongside cadastral records, planning and construction documents, because a registered seller does not automatically prove that every physical extension or use is legal.

  • Registered owner and acquisition history
  • Mortgages, foreclosures, claims and rights of use
  • Cadastral identifier, floor and area
  • Construction permit and Act 16/use authorization
  • Common-area shares and storage/parking title
  • Spousal, inheritance and company authority issues

12 · AFFORDABILITY, VACANCY AND HOUSING QUALITY

Strong city demand coexists with national depopulation and a large unused stock

The European Commission estimated signs of national price overvaluation of around 19% in 2025, but also reported that the price-to-income ratio remained below its long-term average because income growth had been strong. These indicators are not contradictory: prices can appear stretched by one model while improving relative to rapidly rising incomes.

A more structural concern is allocation. The Commission reports that 38.9% of Bulgaria’s housing stock was vacant in 2021, with more than 25% vacancy even in Sofia, while strong demand persists for the right homes. Much of the unused stock is in the wrong location, tied up by ownership issues, technically obsolete or held as an investment rather than offered to tenants.

Investment lesson: national dwelling quantity does not equal usable local supply. Renovation need, fragmented ownership, poor energy performance and location mismatch can keep effective supply tight even when headline vacancy is high.

14 · 2026–2028 SCENARIO FRAMEWORK

Three plausible paths—not a single guaranteed forecast

BASE CASEModerating growth

Price growth moves toward mid-to-high single digits as mortgage availability, income growth and urban demand offset improved supply and lower buyer urgency.

UPSIDE CASEEuro confidence extends

Foreign and domestic demand remains strong, credit stays cheap and delivery bottlenecks preserve double-digit growth in the best Sofia and coastal submarkets.

DOWNSIDE CASEAffordability and supply bite

Higher euro-area rates, slower income growth, geopolitical shock or concentrated new supply produces flat prices or localized nominal corrections.

BEST FIT

Who may find Bulgaria attractive?

  • Buyers seeking lower nominal entry prices than mature Western markets
  • Investors comfortable with active legal and technical due diligence
  • Long-term buyers focused on Sofia, Plovdiv, Varna or Burgas fundamentals
  • Euro-based buyers who value the removal of currency-conversion friction
  • Owner-users combining lifestyle use with conservative rental assumptions
WEAKER FIT

Who should be more cautious?

  • Buyers expecting another automatic year of 15–20% appreciation
  • Investors who cannot supervise a remote renovation or resort operator
  • Highly leveraged buyers without a rate or vacancy buffer
  • Buyers selecting solely by low price per square metre

16 · FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

Bulgaria residential property FAQ

Are Bulgarian prices still rising rapidly in 2026?

Yes at the national index level: NSI reported +14.8% year-on-year in Q1 2026. However, transaction activity had already slowed, and city-level quarterly movements ranged from strong increases to declines.

Is Sofia always the best investment market?

Sofia has the deepest economy and resale market, but the entry price is highest and indicative gross yield is lower than in some secondary or coastal cities. The best market depends on hold period, tenant target, financing and exit strategy.

Did the euro remove exchange-rate risk?

For euro-based buyers it removed the need to convert between euro and lev after 1 January 2026. The lev had already been fixed to the euro, so the underlying monetary link was not new.

Can a non-EU citizen buy a house with land?

Land rules can restrict direct foreign ownership. A Bulgarian company or another lawful structure may be required, but the exact route must be confirmed by a lawyer for the buyer’s nationality and property category.

Are resort apartments good rental investments?

Some can work, but annual performance depends on season length, operator terms, common charges, travel demand, maintenance and resale liquidity. Peak weekly rates should never be treated as year-round income.

What is Act 16?

It is the commonly used market term for the authorization placing a completed building into use. Buyers should confirm the exact legal document and project status rather than relying only on an agent’s label.

How much should be reserved above the purchase price?

Until exact quotations are obtained, a conservative initial reserve of about 4–8% can cover local acquisition tax, notary and registration charges, legal work and possible agency costs. The final amount varies.

What is the biggest mistake foreign buyers make?

Paying a deposit before independent title, land, permit, building and contract verification. A desirable view or discount does not compensate for a legally or technically defective asset.
