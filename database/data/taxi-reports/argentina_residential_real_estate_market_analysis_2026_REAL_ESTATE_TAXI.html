02 · DIRECT MARKET ANSWER

A recovering transaction market — but still a high-complexity investment environment

Argentina’s residential market entered mid-2026 with a firmer Buenos Aires asking-price signal, deed activity holding near the prior year, improving construction activity and a wider recovery in household credit. However, the recovery is not the same as a low-risk property cycle. Inflation remained high, mortgage-backed purchases lost momentum in April, and data quality varies sharply outside major cities.

The market is most suitable for buyers who can underwrite in US dollars, tolerate policy and currency changes, use independent local legal and tax advisers, and treat each asset as a separate title, building and cash-flow investigation. It is less suitable for highly leveraged investors who need predictable local-currency financing or who assume advertised yields can be converted directly into stable hard-currency returns.

03 · DATA HIERARCHY

What each dataset can — and cannot — prove

Argentina does not offer one single, current, nationwide residential transaction-price index with uniform coverage. A serious report therefore uses an evidence hierarchy.

01

Official transaction activity

Notarial deed counts from the Colegio de Escribanos show completed legal acts for properties in CABA. They are the strongest current liquidity indicator used here, but they do not provide a quality-adjusted price index.

Use deed counts to judge market turnover and mortgage participation — not to infer that every neighborhood or property type moved by the same percentage.
02

Asking-price indicators

Buenos Aires price-per-square-metre figures published by market data providers are based on listings or asking prices. They are useful for direction, neighborhood screening and seller expectations.

Asking prices can differ materially from signed reservation prices, deed values and the economic value of payment terms.
03

Real Estate Taxi inference

Scenario ranges, suitability judgments and net-yield logic in this report are analytical conclusions, not official forecasts. They are explicitly identified and should be recalculated for the individual property.

No national average substitutes for unit-level comparable sales, building expenses, legal status and realistic rent evidence.
04

Price direction and the inflation problem

Global Property Guide’s Q1 2026 dashboard reports a Buenos Aires average apartment asking-price indicator of US$2,111 per m², up 1.46% year on year. Its earlier detailed Q2 2025 series showed used two-bedroom asking prices at US$2,310 per m², up 5.27% nominally in US-dollar terms but down 24.48% after its inflation adjustment.

The apparent contradiction is important: a property can rise in advertised US-dollar price while still losing purchasing power under a particular real-price methodology. Argentina’s exchange-rate regime, inflation base, price currency and timing can radically affect the result.

  • Always identify whether the series is in pesos, official-rate dollars, market-rate dollars or constant prices.
  • Do not compare a Q1 average-all-apartment indicator directly with a Q2 used-two-bedroom series.
  • For an acquisition decision, prioritize recent closed comparables with matching payment terms.

06

Transactions and market liquidity

CABA recorded 5,472 sale deeds in April 2026, almost exactly matching 5,471 a year earlier. Activity was 2.1% below March 2026, and the first four months of 2026 were broadly level with the same period of 2025.

The total April deed amount was ARS 861.110 billion. The average act was ARS 157.37 million, or US$111,857 using the average official exchange rate cited by the Colegio; the US-dollar average was 2.5% lower year on year.

Liquidity stabilized after the sharp 2024–2025 rebound. That supports market functionality, but it does not prove continued acceleration.
07

Mortgage participation weakened

Only 609 CABA sale deeds were formalized with a mortgage in April 2026. That was 11.1% of total deeds and 48.9% below April 2025.

BCRA’s second-half 2025 financial-inclusion report still showed a broader recovery in long-term household credit: 20.3 million individuals had financing at December 2025, population coverage reached 54.7%, and mortgage and auto credit added borrowers and balances.

The structural credit recovery and the April mortgage slowdown can both be true. Buyers should underwrite the actual bank offer available at reservation date, not rely on a national narrative.
08

Supply, construction and new-build pipeline

INDEC’s synthetic construction activity indicator (ISAC) increased by 2.1% cumulatively in January–April 2026 compared with the same period of 2025. This indicates an improving construction backdrop, but it is not a direct count of residential completions.

A non-official Buenos Aires market monitor reported 70,142 used apartments for sale at end-2024, 5.39% fewer than a year earlier. Because that is portal inventory, it can be affected by duplicate listings, stale advertisements and delistings.

  • Check municipal permits and approved plans for each new project.
  • Confirm whether construction costs or installment balances are indexed.
  • Model delivery delays, incomplete common areas and developer solvency.
09

Apartments, houses, new and existing stock

Apartment data are much more visible in CABA than national house data. Houses in suburban Buenos Aires, Córdoba, Mendoza, Patagonia and coastal markets require local evidence because land value, infrastructure, security, water, access and construction quality drive the result.

10

Long-term rental market

Contract economics depend on the agreed currency, adjustment mechanism, deposit, guarantee, term, expense allocation and local enforcement practice. A rent quoted in pesos can produce a very different hard-currency return depending on adjustment timing and exchange-rate movement.

Law 27,802 changed the national income-tax framework from fiscal years beginning 1 January 2026 by exempting qualifying income from residential rentals. The precise benefit depends on taxpayer status, use, implementation and other taxes; it should not be assumed to eliminate every tax or reporting obligation.

Underwrite the lease itself, not only the headline rent: index clause, payment currency, recoverable expenses, vacancy and collection quality drive the net outcome.
11

Gross and realistic net rental yields

Global Property Guide estimated an average gross residential yield of 5.98% nationwide in Q2 2025, with city averages of 7.30% for Buenos Aires, 6.45% for Córdoba and 4.19% for Rosario. These are portal-based gross estimates, not audited landlord returns.

12

Short-term rental potential

Short-term rentals can work in Buenos Aires tourism and business districts, wine regions, ski markets and Patagonia, but performance is property-specific and seasonal. Building bylaws, city registration, safety rules, hospitality taxes, platform reporting and neighbor restrictions must be checked before purchase.

  • Use verified occupancy and achieved nightly rates, not host calendar assumptions.
  • Budget cleaning, linen, utilities, platform fees, guest support and furnishing replacement.
  • Check whether the building’s co-ownership rules permit temporary accommodation.
  • Stress low season, regulatory change and a stronger or weaker local currency.
13

Affordability and currency mismatch

Argentina’s national CPI rose 1.9% in June 2026, 16.8% from December and 33.5% year on year. The EMAE activity indicator for April 2026 was 1.6% above a year earlier but 1.5% below March on a seasonally adjusted basis.

Property sales are frequently discussed in US dollars while household incomes, taxes and many operating costs are in pesos. That mismatch can support cash buyers with hard currency but constrain local affordability.

The same exchange-rate move can improve a foreign buyer’s entry price and weaken local tenant affordability.
04 · BUYING, TAX AND LEGAL STRUCTURE

Foreign access is possible — but urban apartments, rural land and special zones are not governed by one identical process

14

Foreign-buyer identification and access

A foreign individual without CUIT or CUIL can request a CDI when the identification is needed to buy registrable assets or operate in the financial system. The official process accepts a passport for non-residents and is free.

Urban residential ownership is generally accessible to foreigners, subject to normal legal, tax and compliance checks. A buyer should confirm whether a local bank account, power of attorney, certified translations, apostilles or representation are required for the chosen closing structure.

A CDI identifies the person for specified operations; the official guidance states that it does not itself identify a taxpayer for every fiscal or social-security obligation.
15

Rural land restrictions

Foreign purchase of rural land is subject to Law 26,737 and prior certification. Official rules include a maximum 15% foreign-ownership share at national, provincial and departmental levels, a 30% limit for any one nationality within that foreign share, and buyer-specific hectare-equivalence limits that vary by location.

Additional water-authority and border-zone approvals may apply. The authorization fee is stated as 0.1% of the deed value, and non-compliant acts can be null.

Never assume that a vineyard, estancia, farm, forest parcel or peri-urban development land follows the same foreign-buyer process as a CABA apartment.
16

Standard buying procedure

  1. Define the payment structure: price currency, exchange-rate basis, bank or escrow route, timing and source-of-funds evidence.
  2. Appoint an independent escribano and lawyer: confirm who acts for whom and what is included.
  3. Review title and parties: ownership, liens, inhibitions, marital or corporate authority and succession issues.
  4. Review the property: cadastral plan, municipal approvals, zoning, improvements, occupancy, building rules and debts.
  5. Negotiate reservation / boleto terms: deposit, conditions precedent, default, possession, tax allocation and refund triggers.
  6. Execute the escritura: complete payment, tax/fee withholding, notarization, possession and registry presentation.
  7. Complete post-closing changes: utilities, consortium, insurance, tax records, rental registration and accounting.

Exact sequencing differs by province, property type, seller status and whether the buyer is present or represented.

17

Taxes and transaction costs

There is no safe single national closing-cost percentage because stamp duty, registration, professional fees, tax status and negotiated allocation vary by jurisdiction and transaction.

For CABA in 2026, the city maintained a 100% stamp-duty exemption for a unique family permanent residence below ARS 226.1 million. For second or additional homes below that threshold, the announced rate was reduced from 3.5% to 2.7%. Stamp duty on qualifying unique-home mortgages was reduced from 1% to zero.

Obtain a written closing statement before signing: price, stamp duty, registry, notary, legal, brokerage, certificates, tax withholdings, banking and foreign-exchange costs.
18

Annual ownership and income taxes

Recurring costs may include local property charges, municipal services, provincial taxes, consortium expenses, insurance, maintenance and national tax obligations depending on residency and asset profile. ARCA’s personal-assets regime generally evaluates taxable assets held at 31 December, subject to applicable thresholds, exemptions and valuation rules.

Law 27,802 provides, from fiscal years beginning 1 January 2026, an income-tax exemption for qualifying residential rental income and an exemption for certain property disposals governed by Article 99, subject to regulation and taxpayer circumstances.

This is a major 2026 legal change, but it is not a universal statement that every owner, company, non-resident, short-term rental or sale is tax-free.
19

Title, cadastral and building due diligence

  • Registry title, liens, mortgages, embargoes and personal inhibitions.
  • Seller identity, authority, marital property, company powers and succession status.
  • Cadastral plan versus actual walls, terraces, enclosed balconies and extensions.
  • Municipal permits, zoning, heritage restrictions and final approvals.
  • Consortium minutes, expensas, extraordinary assessments, litigation and reserve fund.
  • Tax, utility and building debts; occupancy and tenant rights.
  • Structural, electrical, gas, water, moisture, façade, lift and roof condition.
An attractive renovated unit can still contain unapproved works or inherit a building-level capital expenditure problem.
20

New-development and off-plan risk

Off-plan projects may offer installments and early pricing, but the buyer can carry developer, construction, inflation-indexation, delivery, specification and title-structure risk.

  • Identify the legal vehicle, landowner and developer guarantees.
  • Verify approved plans, work permits, insurance and construction progress.
  • Understand the adjustment index and which balance is indexed.
  • Check the consequences of late delivery, material substitution and area variance.
  • Confirm when separate title, possession and utilities will be available.
  • Do not pay substantial funds without independent review of the trust or contract structure.
21

AML and payment-route risk

Notaries, banks and other obligated parties can require identification, beneficial-ownership and source-of-funds documentation. A legitimate buyer can still face delay if the payment route, currency conversion or wealth trail is prepared late.

Prepare early
Tax returns, bank statements, sale contracts, corporate documents and translations.
Define route
Origin bank, intermediary, recipient, currency, reference and closing timing.
Avoid ambiguity
Document every exchange-rate and settlement assumption in writing.
22

Climate, insurance and physical risks

Argentina’s size creates different hazard profiles: urban flood and storm-water exposure in parts of Buenos Aires, river flooding, wildfire in some dry and forested regions, seismic design considerations in western provinces, snow and access constraints in mountain markets, and wind or coastal exposure in Patagonia and Atlantic locations.

  • Obtain parcel- and building-specific insurance quotations before commitment.
  • Inspect basements, ground floors, drainage, pumps and historical water marks.
  • Check seismic and structural compliance where relevant.
  • For remote assets, verify year-round road, water, power, communications and emergency access.

05 · OPPORTUNITY, RISK AND 2026–2028 SCENARIOS

The next two years will depend more on inflation, credit and policy credibility than on one headline price number

24

Opportunity factors

  • Buenos Aires asking prices have returned to positive year-on-year movement.
  • CABA transaction activity remains functional after the strong 2024 recovery.
  • Construction activity improved in early 2026.
  • Hard-currency buyers may find motivated sellers or renovation opportunities.
  • Some central urban submarkets have deep rental demand and international recognition.
  • Tax changes may improve qualifying residential-rental and disposal economics.
  • Lifestyle markets can combine personal use with rental demand when operated professionally.
25

Risk factors

  • Inflation and exchange-rate volatility can distort both prices and net returns.
  • Mortgage participation can reverse quickly as lending conditions change.
  • National data are incomplete and heavily Buenos Aires-weighted.
  • Advertised properties may be duplicated, stale or priced above executable levels.
  • Legal and tax rules can change, and provincial treatment differs.
  • Building expenses, special assessments and unapproved works can destroy yield.
  • Repatriation, banking and source-of-funds friction can delay transactions.
  • Short-term rental regulations and building rules may constrain use.

27

Detailed buyer and investor due-diligence checklist

  • Independent written valuation using recent executable comparables.
  • Seller identity, authority, civil status and beneficial ownership.
  • Registry title, liens, mortgages, embargoes and inhibitions.
  • Cadastral plan, measured area and actual configuration reconciliation.
  • Zoning, approved plans, permits, final completion and heritage status.
  • Consortium bylaws, minutes, ordinary and extraordinary expensas.
  • Pending façade, lift, roof, gas, electrical or structural works.
  • Tax, utility, building and municipal debt certificates.
  • Tenant, occupant, caretaker or possession status and handover plan.
  • Physical inspection: structure, moisture, roof, services and equipment.
  • Flood, seismic, wildfire, wind, coastal and access exposure.
  • Insurance availability, exclusions, deductibles and replacement value.
  • Reservation and boleto refund triggers and default provisions.
  • Price currency, exchange-rate source, payment route and settlement evidence.
  • CDI/CUIT, bank account, powers, apostilles and translations.
  • AML/source-of-funds pack accepted by notary and receiving bank.
  • Complete closing-cost statement by party and jurisdiction.
  • Rental legality, building restrictions, registration and tax treatment.
  • Verified rent roll, vacancy, arrears and achieved short-term rates.
  • Net-yield model including capex, management and currency conversion.
  • Exit taxes, brokerage, liquidity and realistic sale period.
  • For development: land title, vehicle, permits, guarantees and indexation.
  • For rural land: foreign-ownership certificate and special approvals.
  • Post-closing registration, utilities, insurance and accounting setup.
06 · FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

Argentina residential property questions

Is Argentina residential property priced in pesos or US dollars?
Many urban sale listings and negotiations are referenced in US dollars, while taxes, fees, financing and legal accounting may be denominated in pesos. The deed, payment path and exchange-rate basis must be documented precisely.
Can a foreign non-resident buy an apartment in Argentina?
Generally, foreign buyers can acquire urban residential property, but they normally need identification such as a CDI and must satisfy source-of-funds and compliance requirements. Rural land and some border or strategic zones are subject to additional restrictions and approvals.
What is the latest Buenos Aires price signal?
Global Property Guide reports an average Buenos Aires apartment asking-price indicator of US$2,111 per square metre in Q1 2026, up 1.46% year on year. This is an asking-price indicator, not a national transaction-price index.
How liquid is the Buenos Aires market in 2026?
The Colegio de Escribanos recorded 5,472 CABA sale deeds in April 2026, essentially unchanged from April 2025. The first four months of 2026 were also broadly level with the same period of 2025.
Are mortgages driving the market?
Mortgage-backed deeds represented 609 transactions, or 11.1% of CABA deeds, in April 2026 and were 48.9% below the same month of 2025. The market therefore remained materially dependent on cash and non-mortgage financing.
What rental yield should an investor underwrite?
Portal-based gross yields can be useful as a screening tool but are not net returns. A buyer should deduct vacancy, building charges, maintenance, management, insurance, furnishing, taxes, legal compliance and currency-conversion effects.
What changed in property taxation in 2026?
Argentina Law 27,802 introduced income-tax exemptions from fiscal years beginning 1 January 2026 for qualifying residential rental income and for certain property disposals covered by Article 99, subject to scope, taxpayer status and implementing regulation. Transaction-specific tax advice is essential.
Is rural land treated the same as an urban apartment?
No. Foreign acquisition of rural land is subject to statutory concentration limits, personal hectare-equivalence limits and a prior authorization certificate. Urban residential property should not be analyzed under the same rule set.
What is the biggest practical risk for a foreign buyer?
The biggest recurring risks are incomplete title or cadastral review, unapproved works, condominium debts, unclear payment and exchange-rate mechanics, weak source-of-funds documentation, and assuming that portal prices or advertised yields equal completed-deal economics.
What should be verified before paying a reservation or deposit?
Verify the seller and title, registry inhibitions and liens, cadastral plan, zoning and permits, tax and building-charge debts, occupancy status, contract conditions, refund triggers, currency and payment route, notary responsibilities, and the exact closing-cost allocation.
