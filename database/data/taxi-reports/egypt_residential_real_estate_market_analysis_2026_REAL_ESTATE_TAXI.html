REAL ESTATE TAXI DIRECT VIEW

Egypt is a high-inflation, high-dispersion property market—not one uniform housing market

Nominal Egyptian-pound price growth can be impressive while real purchasing-power returns and foreign-currency returns are weak. The early-2026 portal index reversal, policy rates near 20%, wide district-level price gaps and a large off-plan market mean that location, legal status, payment currency, delivery risk and exit liquidity matter more than the national headline. Buyers should underwrite the asset twice: once in Egyptian pounds and once in the currency in which their wealth and future liabilities are measured.

03
COUNTRY MARKET INTELLIGENCE

Currency and inflation can reverse the investment result

An EGP-denominated property may rise in local currency while losing value against the US dollar or euro. Egypt's repeated exchange-rate adjustments changed replacement costs, imported finishing costs and developer pricing, but they also reduced the foreign-currency value of existing local-currency assets.

  • Calculate entry price, rental income, operating costs and exit value in both EGP and the investor's base currency.
  • Identify whether the sale contract, instalments, rent and resale market are EGP-linked, USD-linked or contractually indexed.
  • Do not describe an inflationary price increase as a real capital gain without a CPI and currency adjustment.

Real Estate Taxi interpretation: the March 2026 real decline indicates that the inflation hedge worked unevenly. New-build launch prices and prime districts can behave very differently from older resale stock.

COUNTRY MARKET INTELLIGENCE

Apartments versus villas

Villa asking prices in the cited Greater Cairo districts are generally above apartment prices per square metre, but the premium is not uniform. Land share, compound services, plot title, finishing, private garden, pool, security and developer reputation can dominate the comparison.

  • Apartments: larger tenant pool, lower absolute ticket, easier maintenance, but greater exposure to building management and service-charge disputes.
  • Villas: land and privacy premium, higher upkeep and vacancy cost, more complex structural and utility due diligence.
  • Older central stock: may offer scarcity and established neighborhoods but requires deeper building-condition, elevator, parking, title and rent-law review.

04 · LIQUIDITY & SUPPLY

Transactions, market liquidity and construction

Published asking prices do not reveal how quickly assets sell, at what discount, or whether a completed title can be transferred.

08
COUNTRY MARKET INTELLIGENCE

Transaction volume and liquidity—what is known and what is missing

A current, comprehensive national transaction-volume series with standardized residential prices, buyer nationality, property type and district coverage was not identified in the reviewed public sources. This is a material data gap.

  • Ask for verified recent transactions in the same project, phase, building and unit type—not only active listings.
  • Record days on market, price reductions, assignment fees and the percentage of cash versus financed buyers.
  • Check whether the unit is registrable now, only through court-validity proceedings, or dependent on future developer/master-title steps.

Real Estate Taxi inference: liquidity is likely strongest in completed, occupied, legally clear, mid-ticket units in established Cairo compounds and recognized resort projects. Large luxury, unfinished or legally incomplete units can have a much thinner buyer pool.

09
COUNTRY MARKET INTELLIGENCE

Official and broker supply indicators

CAPMAS published its latest Bulletin of Housing in Egypt 2023/2024 on 14 December 2025. The bulletin covers housing implementation by sector and governorate, but national construction totals should not be confused with immediately investable, completed, serviced and legally transferable stock. [2]

For the tracked formal Cairo market, JLL data cited by Global Property Guide recorded approximately 7,500 residential completions in Q3 2025 and a tracked stock of around 317,000 units. Full-year tracked completions were 24,000 in 2024, 23,000 in 2023 and 18,000 in 2022. [1]

JLL's tracked stock is a broker-defined formal market universe, not the entire Cairo housing stock.

10
COUNTRY MARKET INTELLIGENCE

Vacancy and occupancy must be measured project by project

Reliable national vacancy data for investable residential units was not found in the reviewed sources. Egypt can simultaneously have a housing need, vacant investment units and shortages of affordable, correctly located homes.

  • Inspect evening occupancy, utility-meter use and building services.
  • Ask the compound management for occupied-unit and arrears data.
  • Check whether surrounding roads, retail, schools and public transport are operational rather than planned.
  • For the New Administrative Capital and new districts, distinguish handover from practical habitability.
11
COUNTRY MARKET INTELLIGENCE

Demand drivers and foreign-buyer demand

Structural demand is supported by population growth, household formation, urban congestion and the cultural use of real estate as a store of value. Demand is also segmented by expatriate Egyptians, Gulf capital, tourism buyers and local upper-middle-income purchasers.

The IMF's July 2026 country page projected 4.6% real GDP growth and 13.2% consumer-price inflation for 2026. Population was listed at 110.058 million. [6]

Demand for an asset does not guarantee liquidity at the investor's price. The most important question is which buyer cohort can purchase the exact property at exit—and in which currency.

05 · RENTAL MARKET

Long-term rents, short-term demand and realistic yields

Gross yield is only the first line of an investment model. Currency, vacancy, furnishing, service charges and collection risk determine the result.

12
COUNTRY MARKET INTELLIGENCE

Long-term rental direction

JLL figures cited by Global Property Guide showed Q3 2025 apartment rents rising 17.6% year-on-year in 6th of October and 16.9% in New Cairo. Prime rents may be quoted or negotiated in hard currency, especially for expatriate-oriented units. [1]

  • Verify whether rent is legally payable in EGP, contractually linked to foreign currency or simply marketed in USD.
  • Test tenant demand for the exact furnishing level, school commute, parking, security and utility reliability.
  • Underwrite collection and turnover, not just advertised monthly rent.

14
COUNTRY MARKET INTELLIGENCE

Short-term rental market

Cairo business and medical demand, Red Sea tourism and Mediterranean summer demand can support short stays, but performance is seasonal and operationally intensive.

  • Confirm building, compound, tourism and local licensing rules before advertising.
  • Model platform commission, utilities, cleaning, linen, guest damage, local management and empty periods.
  • Use conservative annual occupancy rather than peak-week rates.
  • Check whether foreign owners can open required accounts, receive platform payouts and document taxable income.

A resort unit with a high nightly rate can still produce a weak annual net yield if it has long low-season vacancy and high service charges.

06 · FINANCE & AFFORDABILITY

Mortgage market, interest rates and purchasing power

High policy rates make conventional market-rate leverage expensive and reinforce the importance of developer instalment structures and subsidized programs.

15
COUNTRY MARKET INTELLIGENCE

Policy rates and mortgage conditions

On 9 July 2026, the Central Bank of Egypt kept the overnight deposit rate at 19.0%, the overnight lending rate at 20.0% and the main-operation rate at 19.5%. Annual urban headline inflation was 14.3% in June 2026. [3] [4]

These rates do not equal a retail mortgage offer, but they indicate a high-cost financing environment. Subsidized housing programs operate under separate eligibility rules and should not be assumed available to an investment or foreign buyer.

Developer instalments can appear cheaper than bank debt, yet the cash price versus instalment price often reveals a substantial embedded financing cost.

16
COUNTRY MARKET INTELLIGENCE

Official non-bank mortgage activity

The Financial Regulatory Authority reported that by the end of 2025, 25 licensed mortgage-finance companies had extended approximately EGP 42.7 billion to 115,000 clients, up 67.5% from EGP 25.5 billion in 2024. [5]

This growth is significant but remains small relative to Egypt's population and housing stock. A buyer should obtain a written credit decision before relying on finance and verify valuation methodology, eligible title, down payment, term, insurance and early-settlement cost.

17
COUNTRY MARKET INTELLIGENCE

Affordability and payment-plan risk

Affordability is constrained by the gap between wages and formal new-build prices. Long instalment plans reduce the initial payment but may shift risk to later years.

  • Stress-test instalments after a 20%–30% income or exchange-rate shock.
  • Compare total nominal payments with the cash price and present value.
  • Check whether missed instalments trigger cancellation, penalties or forfeiture.
  • Confirm whether maintenance deposits, club fees, parking and fit-out are excluded from the advertised price.
07 · TAX, OWNERSHIP & PURCHASE PROCESS

Taxes, foreign-buyer rules, residency and legal transfer

Legal status is asset-specific. A valid sale contract is not the same thing as registered title, development approval or an immediately transferable freehold interest.

18
COUNTRY MARKET INTELLIGENCE

Taxes and acquisition / ownership costs

Annual real estate tax
10% of assessed rental value, with statutory deductions and exemptions depending on classification
Transfer tax on disposal
2.5% of the total disposal value for built real estate or building land, subject to the law's scope
VAT
Standard rate 14%; application depends on the transaction and services
Other costs
Legal review, registration/notarization, translations, powers of attorney, brokerage, developer assignment, service charges and utility deposits

PwC's Egypt tax summary, reviewed 4 February 2026, states the 10% real-estate tax basis and the 2.5% transfer tax. Tax allocation in the contract does not necessarily change who is legally liable. [7]

Obtain transaction-specific advice. Rental income, corporate ownership, furnished hospitality services and non-resident status can produce different outcomes.

19
COUNTRY MARKET INTELLIGENCE

Foreign ownership—official position requires property-specific confirmation

GAFI's official foreign-investment summary states that non-Egyptians may own constructed property or vacant land under Law No. 230 of 1996, traditionally subject to a maximum of two residential properties nationwide, 4,000 m² per property and antiquities restrictions, with possible exceptions. The same official document notes that an amendment allowing unlimited ownership subject to foreign-currency payment had been under review. [8]

Special rules and security approvals can apply in tourist areas, new urban communities, Sinai and strategic or border zones. GAFI separately states that foreign investors in qualifying investment projects may receive residence for the project term. [9]

Do not rely on a developer's phrase “foreigners can buy.” A lawyer must identify the precise legal interest—registered freehold, usufruct, long lease, allocation right, developer contract or shareholding structure.

20
COUNTRY MARKET INTELLIGENCE

Residency and investment-program relevance

Property purchase, investor residence and citizenship-by-investment are separate legal routes. GAFI's investment-law pages address residence for non-Egyptian investors in qualifying projects; they do not mean that every apartment purchase automatically grants residence. [9]

  • Confirm current thresholds, payment currency and source-of-funds rules directly with the competent authority.
  • Verify whether the chosen property and ownership form qualifies.
  • Do not value a property premium on immigration benefits until written eligibility is confirmed.
21
COUNTRY MARKET INTELLIGENCE

Standard buying procedure—safer sequence

  1. Appoint an independent Egyptian property lawyer; do not use only the seller's or developer's representative.
  2. Identify seller, legal capacity, title chain, land owner/allocation and encumbrances.
  3. Verify foreign-buyer eligibility, security approvals and geographic restrictions.
  4. Check planning, building permit, approved drawings, unit number and common-area rights.
  5. Reconcile cash price, instalment price, assignment fee, maintenance deposit and all closing costs.
  6. Use a conditional reservation or preliminary contract with document and refund protections.
  7. Complete notarization, signature validation, registration or the legally appropriate court/registry process.
  8. Transfer utilities, management account, possession record and original documents.
22
COUNTRY MARKET INTELLIGENCE

Title, cadastral, zoning and permit risks

  • Unregistered or partially registered title and reliance on private contracts.
  • Mismatch between master land title, developer allocation and unit sold.
  • Unauthorized floor, extension, roof use, garden use or subdivision.
  • Outstanding mortgages, liens, taxes, service charges or developer balances.
  • Multiple sales, conflicting powers of attorney or incomplete inheritance documentation.
  • Property on state, military, tourism-development or protected land with special transfer rules.

The legal objective is not merely to prove that a building exists. It is to prove that the seller owns the exact transferable interest, that the unit was lawfully created and that the buyer can register or enforce it.

23
COUNTRY MARKET INTELLIGENCE

Off-plan and new-development risks

  • Land allocation may be conditional on development milestones.
  • Delivery date can differ from practical occupancy date because utilities and surrounding infrastructure lag.
  • Contract may permit broad changes to area, view, finishing or common facilities.
  • Assignment or resale before delivery may require developer consent and a fee.
  • Maintenance and club charges may begin before the buyer can use the unit.
  • Buyer payments may not be ring-fenced in a project escrow structure comparable with stronger regulated markets.

Require evidence of land rights, approvals, construction progress, completed prior phases, audited developer position where available, payment protection and enforceable delay remedies.

24
COUNTRY MARKET INTELLIGENCE

Climate, insurance and building-condition risks

Risk varies sharply between dense Cairo, Mediterranean coast, Red Sea resorts and desert new cities.

  • Heat and water: air-conditioning capacity, insulation, water pressure, storage and utility continuity.
  • Coastal exposure: salt corrosion, humidity, façade and mechanical-system deterioration.
  • Flooding: local drainage and low-lying access roads during intense rainfall.
  • Seismic and structural: engineer review of older, altered or visibly distressed buildings.
  • Insurance: confirm insured perils, rebuilding value, common-area policy, exclusions and claims process.
08 · INVESTMENT DECISION

Buyer fit, opportunities, risks and the next two years

A good Egyptian property is one whose legal clarity, micro-location and income or use case are strong enough to compensate for macro and execution risk.

25
COUNTRY MARKET INTELLIGENCE

Who the market may suit

  • Egyptians abroad who understand local procedures and want a long-term family or inflation-hedge asset.
  • Cash buyers able to hold through currency and liquidity cycles.
  • Investors with strong local legal, property-management and tenant networks.
  • Buyers targeting completed, high-demand units with a clear title route and realistic rent.
  • Lifestyle buyers who value use of the property independently of short-term return.

Best fit is generally a patient investor who can verify documents locally and does not depend on a rapid resale.

26
COUNTRY MARKET INTELLIGENCE

Who should be cautious or avoid the market

  • Highly leveraged buyers exposed to market-rate debt.
  • Investors requiring stable euro or dollar cash flow without currency risk.
  • Short-term flippers relying only on a developer's projected appreciation.
  • Remote buyers unable to inspect, manage and enforce locally.
  • Buyers who need fully transparent transaction comparables and rapid registry transfer.

Principal risk factors

  • Further EGP depreciation or inflation resurgence.
  • High financing costs and reduced buyer affordability.
  • Portal asking prices diverging from achievable resale prices.
  • Developer delay, specification changes and incomplete infrastructure.
  • Title, allocation, permit or foreign-ownership problems.
  • Thin resale market for large luxury and unfinished units.
  • Regional security and travel risk affecting tourism locations.
  • Rent regulation changes and legacy old-rent occupancy issues in older stock.

COUNTRY MARKET INTELLIGENCE

Detailed due-diligence checklist

Identity, title and legal interest
  • Seller ID, marital/inheritance status, company authority and power of attorney.
  • Original title/allocation documents, complete transfer chain and encumbrance search.
  • Exact unit, floor, area, parking, garden, roof and common-area rights.
  • Foreign ownership eligibility and all security/ministerial approvals.
Planning, building and condition
  • Land-use designation, master plan, building permit and approved drawings.
  • Engineer measurement and structural, MEP, moisture, corrosion and façade inspection.
  • Utility connections, meters, road access, fire safety and elevator documentation.
Financial and operational
  • Cash and instalment price reconciliation; all fees, deposits and taxes.
  • Service-charge budget, arrears, reserve fund and management contract.
  • Verified rents, actual occupancy, net-income model and currency assumptions.
  • Exit buyer profile, transfer procedure, assignment restrictions and comparable closed deals.
Contract protections
  • Clear condition precedent for legal approval and document verification.
  • Delivery specification, long-stop date, delay remedy and refund right.
  • No uncontrolled unilateral area, design, view or facility changes.
  • Dispute forum, governing law, Arabic/English consistency and enforceability.
09 · FAQ

Egypt residential real estate FAQ

Are Egyptian house-price figures transaction prices?
Usually not. Egypt does not yet provide a comprehensive, frequently updated, public national transaction-price index comparable with the strongest registry-based systems. The most current market direction in this report relies mainly on the Aqarmap portal index and broker research, while official sources are used for inflation, financing, construction and legal context.
Did residential prices rise or fall in early 2026?
The Aqarmap-based measure reported by Global Property Guide showed a year-on-year nominal decline of 8.22% in March 2026 and an inflation-adjusted decline of 20.30%. This followed strong nominal increases in earlier years, showing why Egyptian-pound appreciation must be separated from real and foreign-currency performance.
Can foreigners buy residential property in Egypt?
Yes, but the applicable route, ownership limit, security approval, land status and geographic restrictions must be confirmed for the exact property. The official GAFI foreign-investment summary still presents Law No. 230 of 1996 limits and notes that amendments had been under review, while special rules apply in tourist and new urban areas.
What gross rental yield can an investor expect?
Global Property Guide's Q4 2025 survey estimated 6.72% nationally, 8.3% for Cairo, 5.14% for Alexandria and 7.29% for Hurghada. These are gross survey yields, not guaranteed net returns. Vacancy, service charges, furnishing, repairs, taxes, management and currency conversion can reduce net yield materially.
What is the biggest off-plan risk?
Paying large instalments before verifying the land allocation, developer authority, master-plan approvals, building permit, delivery obligations, escrow or payment protection, utility completion and registration route. Long instalment plans can hide a high embedded financing cost and expose the buyer to delay and currency risk.
Is Egypt suitable for a short-term property flip?
It can be unsuitable for a buyer who needs predictable registration, immediate resale, low legal friction or stable hard-currency returns. Transaction costs, legal approval, registration delays, foreign-ownership conditions, thin resale liquidity and exchange-rate changes can overwhelm nominal price growth.
