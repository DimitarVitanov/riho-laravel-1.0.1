EXECUTIVE DECISION FRAME

Serbia in one investment sentence

Serbia enters the second half of 2026 with apartment prices still rising, transaction value at record levels and a noticeably larger share of mortgage-financed purchases, but affordability remains tight and market performance differs sharply between Belgrade, Novi Sad and the country’s lower-cost regional cities.

The strongest risk-adjusted opportunities are generally legally clean apartments in established urban districts with transparent comparable sales, durable local rental demand and realistic exit pricing. The weakest propositions are unregistered or partly regulated properties, highly leveraged purchases justified only by future appreciation, and new-build units where completion, fit-out and developer obligations are not independently verified.

01

2026 market snapshot

The Republic Geodetic Authority’s official apartment-price index for Q4 2025 was 190.43, representing 5.70% annual growth and 1.34% quarterly growth. New-build prices rose 5.71% year-on-year and resale prices rose 5.69%, showing broad rather than segment-specific appreciation.

Global Property Guide’s harmonized series reports a stronger 10.51% nominal annual change for 2025 and 7.55% real growth. The difference illustrates why users must distinguish the exact source series, reference period and methodology rather than combine indicators as though they were identical.

02

How to read the numbers

Official transaction evidence: RGZ apartment-price indices, transaction counts, values and registered contract data.

Official construction evidence: Statistical Office of the Republic of Serbia building permits and dwelling pipeline.

Official monetary evidence: National Bank of Serbia policy rates, inflation and housing-loan data.

Secondary market evidence: Global Property Guide and listing-platform asking rents and yields. Asking prices are not completed sale prices, and gross yields are not net returns.

PRICE CYCLE

From rapid repricing to a more selective expansion

Serbia’s apartment market accelerated strongly during 2021–2022 as inflation, migration, construction costs and demand for real assets pushed prices higher. The market did not reverse when interest rates rose; instead, growth slowed and buyers became more selective.

By late 2025, the official data showed moderate national appreciation but stronger transaction value, indicating that the market was still liquid enough to absorb higher prices. The 2026 investment question is therefore less about whether Serbia has demand and more about where that demand is deep enough to support rent, resale and financing costs.

03

Official apartment-price index

RGZ reported 5.70% annual apartment-price growth in Q4 2025. Belgrade led the regional increase at 6.32%, followed by Vojvodina at 5.77%, Southern and Eastern Serbia at 5.35%, and Šumadija and Western Serbia at 4.33%.

The index is based on transaction data and a mass-valuation model that incorporates characteristics from the Real Estate Price Register, cadastre and other official datasets. It is therefore more useful for broad market movement than a simple average of advertised listings.

04

Transaction activity and market value

During Q4 2025, 14,212 apartment contracts were recorded, 4.7% more than one year earlier. Their total value reached a record EUR 1.448 billion, up 13.8% year-on-year.

In Q3 2025, the wider property market recorded 30,511 purchase contracts with total value of approximately EUR 1.8 billion. Apartments represented about EUR 1.1 billion, or 60% of market value.

05

Regional market map

Belgrade: deepest market, highest values, strongest professional and international demand, but also the greatest affordability pressure.

Vojvodina: Novi Sad is the principal investment market, while other cities offer lower entry prices and thinner liquidity.

Šumadija and Western Serbia: lower prices, mixed rental depth and stronger dependence on local employment and university demand.

Southern and Eastern Serbia: Niš is the main liquid urban market; smaller municipalities require conservative exit assumptions.

06

Belgrade market intelligence

Belgrade remains Serbia’s benchmark residential market. In Q3 2025, median achieved apartment prices cited in the RGZ-based dataset were approximately EUR 2,517/m² for first sales and EUR 2,560/m² for resales.

Savski Venac, Vračar, Stari Grad and selected New Belgrade districts command premium pricing, but they represent different tenant and buyer pools. Investors should not treat a waterfront luxury project, a historic-center apartment and a mass-market New Belgrade unit as interchangeable.

The strongest liquidity usually combines public transport, clear title, functional layout, manageable common costs and a price aligned with recent completed sales—not only active listings.

07

Novi Sad market intelligence

Novi Sad is Serbia’s second most expensive and most investable city market. Q3 2025 achieved prices were approximately EUR 2,256/m² for new apartments and EUR 2,250/m² for resale apartments.

Demand is supported by the university, technology and service-sector employment, regional migration and owner-occupiers. Rental performance can be stable, but investors must account for new supply and avoid assuming that every peripheral development will achieve central-city rents.

08

Niš, Kragujevac and regional cities

Niš offers a lower entry price than Belgrade or Novi Sad and has a diversified tenant base linked to education, healthcare, logistics and regional employment. Kragujevac combines university demand with industrial employment but typically has thinner resale liquidity.

Regional cities can offer stronger headline yields, yet the realistic net return depends on vacancy, tenant turnover, building quality and the depth of the resale market. A higher gross yield is not automatically a lower-risk investment.

09

New-build market

New developments appeal to buyers seeking energy efficiency, parking, modern layouts and lower immediate repair costs. They may also receive more attractive financing treatment than older properties.

The underwriting must include the developer’s land rights, building permit, construction status, escrow or payment structure, completion deadline, technical acceptance, fit-out specification and registration pathway. Promotional discounts should be compared with the effective all-in cost after parking, finishing and taxes.

10

Resale market

Resale apartments can provide immediate occupancy, observable building condition and established neighborhood demand. They can also reveal hidden risks: unresolved inheritance, registered occupants, mortgages, enforcement notes, unregistered extensions or inaccurate floor area.

A clean cadastral extract and notarized contract are essential, but they do not replace physical inspection, review of building liabilities and confirmation that utilities and common charges are settled.

11

Mortgage demand and buyer leverage

In Q3 2025, credit financed 16% of all property transactions, six percentage points more than one year earlier. For apartment purchases, the mortgage-financed share reached 34.5%, compared with 24.5% in Q3 2024.

This matters because a larger financed buyer pool can support transaction depth, but it also increases sensitivity to interest rates, bank valuation rules and household income growth.

12

Interest rates and monetary conditions

The National Bank of Serbia kept its key policy rate at 5.75% on 9 July 2026, with the deposit facility at 4.50% and lending facility at 7.00%. June 2026 inflation was 2.7%, inside the central bank’s target tolerance band.

Global Property Guide reports an average housing-loan rate near 4.72% in October 2025. Actual borrower pricing depends on fixed or variable structure, currency linkage, down payment, bank valuation, income documentation and legal status.

13

Construction and future supply

The Statistical Office reported 2,329 building permits in May 2026, 4.6% more than in May 2025. Those permits included 3,554 planned dwellings.

Supply analysis must be local. A rising national permit count does not prove oversupply in a specific Belgrade municipality, while a large local development pipeline can pressure rents and resale values even when national construction remains balanced.

14

Rental-market structure

Belgrade has the broadest tenant pool and the highest asking rents, followed by Novi Sad. Niš and Kragujevac offer lower rents but can produce attractive income relative to purchase price when the property is correctly located.

Global Property Guide’s January 2026 listing sample showed monthly advertised rents ranging from roughly EUR 308 for a Belgrade studio to EUR 1,058 for a three-bedroom unit. These figures are asking evidence, not executed leases, and should be stress-tested for vacancy and negotiation.

15

Gross yields and net-return discipline

Global Property Guide estimated an average Serbian gross residential yield near 5.57%, with Belgrade around 6.35% and Novi Sad around 4.79% in its referenced sample.

Net yield is lower after vacancy, agency fees, maintenance, furnishing, utilities during empty periods, tax, insurance and capital expenditure. Investors should calculate a base case, a vacancy-stress case and an exit-cost case.

16

Purchase taxes and recurring obligations

Serbia’s eGovernment guidance states that a buyer of a new apartment from a developer generally faces 10% VAT, while a purchase of an existing property generally attracts a 2.5% absolute-rights transfer tax. The two are mutually exclusive for the same purchase.

Property owners also face annual property tax, normally paid in quarterly installments. Eligibility for first-home relief and VAT refund is conditional and should be checked against the buyer’s citizenship, residence and prior ownership status.

17

Foreign-buyer ownership rules

Serbia’s Ministry of Foreign Affairs states that foreign individuals who do not conduct business in Serbia may acquire an apartment or residential building under reciprocity. Foreign legal or natural persons conducting activities in Serbia may acquire property necessary for those activities, also subject to legal conditions.

Reciprocity must be checked for the buyer’s nationality before signing. Agricultural land and certain property categories require separate analysis and should not be assumed to follow the same rules as an apartment.

18

Transaction and registration process

A typical purchase involves legal and cadastral review, agreement of price and conditions, notarization of the sale contract, tax treatment, payment through an acceptable banking route and registration of ownership in the Real Estate Cadastre.

Serbia’s Ministry of Foreign Affairs notes that real-estate sale contracts must be certified in Serbia before the competent notary. Cadastre decisions for persons living abroad may be delivered electronically or by registered mail.

19

Due-diligence checklist

Verify the cadastral parcel and unit, registered owner, floor area, permitted use, mortgages, enforcement notes, restitution or inheritance issues, building permit, use permit, registered occupants, utility debts and condominium liabilities.

For new construction, verify developer title, permit validity, financing, completion obligations, technical acceptance and registration pathway. For older buildings, inspect roof, façade, lifts, plumbing, heating and potential special assessments.

20

Regulated versus partly regulated property

RGZ distinguishes transactions inside the fully regulated market from transactions involving properties or documentation that are not completely aligned with the formal system.

A partly regulated property may appear cheaper, but its financing, registration, insurance, resale and enforcement risks can be substantially greater. The discount should never be evaluated without a Serbian lawyer and independent technical review.

21

Investment strategies that fit Serbia

Long-term urban rental: established Belgrade, Novi Sad and selected Niš districts with durable employment and transport demand.

Value-add resale: legally clean older apartments where renovation improves usability without relying on speculative neighborhood transformation.

New-build hold: reputable developer, verifiable completion pathway and conservative rent assumptions.

Student and professional demand: smaller efficient units near universities, hospitals, business districts and transport.

22

Principal investor risks

Affordability risk: prices have risen faster than many household incomes.

Interest-rate risk: financed demand remains sensitive to bank pricing and borrower qualification.

Legal risk: unregistered areas, title history and partly regulated transactions can block financing or resale.

Supply risk: large local pipelines can pressure rents even when national demand is healthy.

Currency risk: income, purchase price and investor reporting may involve different currencies.

2026 SCENARIOS

Base, upside and downside paths

Base case: apartment prices continue rising at a moderate nominal pace, with Belgrade and Novi Sad remaining the deepest markets and mortgage participation gradually expanding.

Upside case: lower financing costs, stronger incomes and sustained investment accelerate transaction volumes without a large increase in distressed supply.

Downside case: weaker growth, tighter bank standards or excessive local construction reduce liquidity. In that scenario, secondary cities and undifferentiated peripheral projects are most exposed.

23

Who Serbia fits best

Serbia is best suited to buyers who can perform local legal due diligence, hold through normal market cycles and choose assets based on tenant and resale depth rather than promotional narratives.

It is less suitable for investors who require fully passive cross-border ownership, guaranteed liquidity, high leverage or a rapid exit from an unregistered or highly specialized property.

24

Real Estate Taxi decision framework

Start with completed comparable transactions, not listing averages. Then test rent, vacancy, financing cost, tax, maintenance and exit fees.

Reject any property whose title, permitted use, payment route or registration pathway cannot be independently verified. A lower price does not compensate for a transaction that cannot be safely completed or resold.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

Serbia residential real estate FAQ

Are Serbian apartment prices still rising?
Yes. RGZ reported 5.70% annual growth in Q4 2025, although the pace and liquidity differ by region and property type.

Can foreigners buy apartments in Serbia?
Foreign individuals may generally acquire apartments or residential buildings subject to reciprocity. Nationality-specific confirmation is required.

Which city is the strongest investment market?
Belgrade has the deepest demand and highest liquidity. Novi Sad is the main second-tier market, while Niš can offer lower entry prices.

What purchase tax applies?
New developer sales generally include 10% VAT; existing-property purchases generally attract 2.5% transfer tax. Individual eligibility and exemptions require professional confirmation.

What is the main legal risk?
Unclear registration, partly regulated property, undocumented alterations and unresolved title history.

What yield should an investor expect?
Secondary estimates suggest gross yields around the mid-5% range nationally, but net returns vary materially after vacancy, maintenance and tax.
